# 08 · Channels, timing, and the warm path

Document polish moves odds by percentage points. Channel and timing move the base rate.

---

## Referrals

- **Jobvite (reported secondhand):** referrals are about 7% of applications but about 40% of hires. A 2024 Jobvite/Aptitude figure puts the referral hire rate at about 30% vs. about 7% from job boards. *(C)*
  - Caveat: referral-software vendors publish these numbers, and referred candidates are pre-filtered, so part of the gap is selection, not the referral itself.
- **Jobscan 2025:** 69.4% of recruiters rate referrals "extremely" or "very" important; 60% source through referrals. *(C)*
- **Netflix:** a referral sends the candidate an email to apply. They aren't considered until they do (VERIFIED, portal configuration). The candidate still needs a strong application.

**Referral ask (warm-path note, under 600 characters)**
- Names the role (with a link) and the single strongest proof point.
- Makes it easy to say yes in under a minute: offer a 2-line blurb the contact can forward.
- Never pressures.

**Recruiter or hiring-manager note (under 600 characters)**
- Role, one proof point, one specific ask (a 15-minute conversation, or "open to considering my application").

**Internal note to the user only:** which fixed-signal gap (employer, title, tenure) a referral would help offset.

## LinkedIn behaviors that put a person in recruiters' filters

- **Open to Work:** the filter covers people who said they're open within the last 90 days, so refresh preferences. The public frame got about 40% more InMails, but the recruiters-only mode is safer while employed (privacy not guaranteed).
- **Engaged with the company:** follow target company pages; like, share, or comment where there's something real to say.
- **Company connections:** connect with people by role (team members, design leads, recruiters for the team). Notes stay under 300 characters, specific, and not salesy.
- **More likely to respond:** based on messaging history, so reply to relevant InMails promptly, even to decline.

## Timing

- **Talent Works** (about 1,600 applications; older; C):
  - Applying in the first 96 hours made applicants up to 8x more likely to get an interview.
  - Each later day cut the odds about 28%.
  - Near-zero value after about 10 days.
- Other Talent Works findings (C, older): apply 6–10am; Mondays were best and evenings worst. Treat as weak tie-breakers.
- **Fast lane rule:** postings 4 days old or newer ship verified facts now; open questions wait for the next application.

## Application slots

- Netflix's portal configuration has an application-limit setting of 2 (scope unknown). Choosing which roles to spend slots on is a one-way door.
- `apply-target` ranks fit across postings before any slot is used.

## Scams (the search increases exposure)

- FTC-reported job-scam losses rose from $90M (2020) to $501M (2024). *(A–)*
- Malwarebytes: #OpenToWork hashtags attract fake recruiters leading to phishing and malware. *(B)*
- **Scam screen:**
  - Verified Recruiter badge, or a corporate-domain email and calendar invite;
  - no fees;
  - no SSN or bank details before a verified offer;
  - no "interview software" from a DM;
  - check the job against the company's own careers site.

## Proof pages and tracking

- One **unlisted proof page** per priority posting:
  - role title;
  - a 3-line fit summary;
  - a table mapping the 5 shared requirements to deep links into the proof;
  - the password line, if needed;
  - contact info.
- One **tracked link** per application (UTM tag or a link tool).
- **The tracked link is the funnel diagnostic.** If the link is never opened, the loss is upstream (screening, resume, title); if it's opened with no screen, the problem is proof or level.
