# 04 · Cover letter lane

**Prompt:** `prompts/cover-letter-challenge-v2-draft.md` (draft; needs one challenger pass)

---

## Evidence

**Tailored letters pay; generic letters barely beat none**
- ResumeGo field experiment (7,287 applications to real postings, 2019–20). *(B)*

  | Letter | Callback rate |
  |---|---|
  | None | 10.7% |
  | Generic | 12.5% |
  | Tailored | 16.4% |

- 81% of hiring professionals valued tailored letters over generic ones (same study). *(C)*
- Jobscan 2025: including a cover letter correlated with a 3.4x higher interview rate. *(B, correlational)*

**Readership is split, so the letter must work both ways**
- Resume Genius (625 hiring managers): 83% always or often read cover letters, and 45% read the letter *before* the resume. *(C)*
- Novorésumé (December 2025, 200+ recruiters): 51.7% of recruiters never read them; heads of HR (29.4%) and HR managers (22.9%) skip them far less. *(C)*
- **Implications:** the opening must stand on its own, and no must-have can appear only in the letter.

**AI made generic tailoring worthless as a signal**
- Galdin & Silbert 2025: an AI-based tailoring measure predicted hiring before LLMs and stopped predicting it after. *(A–)*
- What still signals: checkable details only the candidate could know.

**AI tells**
- TopResume 2025 (600 hiring managers): 25% say AI is unacceptable for cover letters; 52% accept AI for drafting support or proofreading if the final version is human. *(C)*
- CV Genius: a tone mismatch between resume and cover letter is a common giveaway, and it carries into the interview. *(C)*

**Length**
- ResumeLab (205 HR professionals): 42% prefer half a page to one page; 40% prefer under half a page. Target 250–400 words unless a limit says otherwise. *(C)*

**Netflix**
- The portal reportedly accepts extra documents (ASSUMED).
- Whether Eightfold scores letter text is unknown. Assume both software and people may read it.

## Method

| Pass | What it does |
|---|---|
| 1 | The real job: the problem behind the hire; top 3 requirements; why the role matters now (VERIFIED or ASSUMED) |
| 2 | Division of labor: the letter adds why this role, why now, the thread through the career, context for a pivot or gap, and one proof story with stakes. Flag anything that just restates a resume bullet. |
| 3 | Opening test: role title plus the strongest reason, in the first two sentences. Write 3 alternative openings. Kill stock openers. |
| 4 | Proof: 1–2 stories with situation, action, how it worked, and a number. |
| 5 | **Swap test:** replace the company name with a competitor's. If the sentence still works, it's generic. |
| 6 | **Costly-signal test:** replace anything an AI could have written for any applicant with details only this person has. |
| 7 | Voice and authenticity; consistency with the resume and how the person talks out loud. |
| 8 | Machine and format: exact title once; 3–5 must-have terms in context; survives a paste into a text box; clean file name. |
| 9 | Length (250–400 words) and a specific, confident close. |

**Outputs:**
- verdict and scorecard;
- sentence-by-sentence audit;
- 3 openings;
- questions for the user;
- revised letter;
- warm-path notes (referral version and recruiter/hiring-manager version, each under 600 characters);
- a call on whether a proof piece is worth it;
- change log.
