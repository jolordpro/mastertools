# Editing checklist

Run in order. Mechanical checks first (`scripts/check_copy.py`), then these judgment checks.

1. **One point:** say the single point in one sentence. Can't? Not ready.
2. **So what?** Ask it after every claim until you hit a benefit the reader cares about.
3. **Competitor swap:** put a competitor's name in. Still works? Rewrite.
4. **Visualise / falsify:** can the reader picture it? Could it be proven false? If not, make it concrete.
5. **Reader first:** does line one serve the reader, not the company?
6. **Awareness:** does the opening match what the reader already knows?
7. **Right job:** brand work leads with feeling, story or scene; activation leads with a specific reason or offer.
8. **Character, incident, place** (brand and video): who, what happens, where?
9. **Specifics:** at least one number, name, place or customer phrase per key message.
10. **Cut 30-50%:** warm-up sentences, adverbs, qualifiers, throat-clearing first paragraphs.
11. **Read aloud:** fix stumbles; vary sentence length; end lines on strong words.
12. **Voice:** matches the traits and the tone this moment needs.
13. **Proof:** every claim backed by a number, name, demonstration or testimonial - or flagged as a placeholder.
14. **CTA:** one clear next step that names its value.
15. **Humour:** lands with the audience, not just the writer; nobody is the target.
16. **Truth:** nothing exaggerated beyond proof; no invented facts presented as real.
17. **Distinctiveness:** would someone recognise the brand with the logo covered?

## Anti-cliche list (replace each with the specific it was hiding)
- **Empty superlatives:** world-class, best-in-class, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, industry-leading, revolutionary, game-changing, next-generation.
- **Corporate filler:** solutions, leverage, synergy, seamless, robust, holistic, empower, innovative, streamline, unlock, elevate.
- **Fake intimacy:** "We're passionate about...", "At [Brand], we believe...", "We're excited to announce...".
- **Hollow benefits:** "quality you can trust", "one-stop shop", "next level", "peace of mind", "like never before".
- **Ad-speak structures:** "Introducing the all-new...", "Look no further", "Whether you're X or Y, we've got you covered".
- **Days of the week as color (banned):** "It's Tuesday", "Monday mood", "Sunday-best". Use the real detail instead; days are allowed only as schedule facts (hours, delivery days).
- **Visual cliches:** handshakes, lightbulbs, people pointing at laptops, glowing globes.

## Additional passes (Sullivan and the reference library)
- **Subtraction pass:** list the elements (headline, visual, body, tagline, logo) and remove one at a time. If it still works without one, cut it. The tagline is the first suspect (Sullivan pp.73-74, Neil French's exercise).
- **Bait vs. pill:** is the interesting part the sales message itself? If the hook could be cut from the product point, it's decoration (pp.66-68).
- **Disaster check:** show it to two people; if several flag the same thing, it's real. Colleagues want to like your work - the public expects the worst (pp.105-106).
- **Kill the weak sister** in any set: one okay piece drags the rest down (p.106).
- **Worst-tone read** of every tagline to catch a second meaning (p.105).
- **Footnote test:** if a piece needs explaining to make sense, rewrite the piece (p.288).
- **Production check after approval:** line breaks, punctuation, the final mock-up - approved work often gets damaged on the way out (p.266).
- **Pitch questions** before presenting a concept (see 11, section 10).
