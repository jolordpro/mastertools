# Slogan library, grouped by technique

Use this to find a technique, not to borrow a line. Format: line - brand - agency/writer - year - the move. Pre-1960 dates and credits vary by source.

Contents: 1. Imperatives and identity · 2. Honesty and the admitted flaw · 3. Single-minded benefit · 4. Owning a moment · 5. Questions and catchphrases · 6. Wit and implication · 7. Challenger and social · 8. Anthemic · 9. Direct-response classics · 10. Modern lines 2015-2026

## 1. Imperatives and identity
- **Just Do It** - Nike - W+K (Dan Wieden) - 1988 - three monosyllables; universal and personal at once.
- **Think Different** - Apple - TBWA\Chiat\Day (art director Craig Tanimoto credited with the line) - 1997 - the grammar break acts out the message.
- **Be All You Can Be** - U.S. Army - N.W. Ayer - 1980 - sells self-actualisation, not a job.
- **Because I'm Worth It** - L'Oreal - McCann (Ilon Specht) - 1973 - first-person voice turns a purchase into self-respect.

## 2. Honesty and the admitted flaw
- **We Try Harder** - Avis - DDB (Paula Green, AD Helmut Krone) - early 1960s - admits being No. 2 and turns it into a service promise.
- **Think small.** / **Lemon.** - Volkswagen - DDB (Julian Koenig, Helmut Krone) - 1959/60 - embraces the car's smallness; an insult reveals rigour.
- **Don't Buy This Jacket** - Patagonia - 2011 - anti-consumption on Black Friday; values proven by self-sacrifice.
- **Good things come to those who wait** - Guinness - AMV BBDO - the slow pour becomes anticipation.
- **Love it or hate it** - Marmite - DDB London - 1996 - owns the polarisation.
- **Probably the best lager in the world** - Carlsberg - 1973 - one hedge word makes a boast charming.

## 3. Single-minded benefit
- **Melts in your mouth, not in your hand** - M&M's - Ted Bates - 1954 - sensory, testable.
- **When it absolutely, positively has to be there overnight** - FedEx - Ally & Gargano - 1978 - the adverbs dramatise the customer's anxiety.
- **The Ultimate Driving Machine** - BMW - Ammirati & Puris - 1970s - one benefit owned for fifty years.
- **1,000 songs in your pocket** - Apple iPod - 2001 - a number you can picture.
- **Tastes great, less filling** - Miller Lite - 1974 - two benefits staged as an argument.
- **Refreshes the parts other beers cannot reach** - Heineken - CDP - 1974 - absurd hyperbole that invites executions.
- **Vorsprung durch Technik** - Audi - BBH - 1982 - untranslated German signals engineering.
- **Our blades are f***ing great** - Dollar Shave Club - founder Michael Dubin - 2012 - founder voice; profanity as candour.

## 4. Owning a moment
- **The pause that refreshes** - Coca-Cola - 1929 - ties the brand to a daily moment.
- **Have a break, have a Kit Kat** - JWT London - 1958 - repetition locks name to break time.
- **Don't leave home without it** - American Express - Ogilvy & Mather - 1975 - places the card at the door.
- **A Diamond Is Forever** - De Beers - N.W. Ayer (Frances Gerety) - 1948 - links product to permanence.

## 5. Questions and catchphrases
- **Got Milk?** - California Milk Processor Board - Goodby Silverstein - 1993 - deprivation insight.
- **Where's the beef?** - Wendy's - 1984 - a catchphrase that entered politics.
- **I Want My MTV** - George Lois - 1982 - viewers demand the product.
- **I can't believe I ate the whole thing** - Alka-Seltzer - Wells Rich Greene - 1972 - the problem in the customer's voice.

## 6. Wit and implication
- **"I never read The Economist." Management trainee. Aged 42.** - AMV (David Abbott) - 1980s - the reader completes the joke.
- **The end of the plain plane** - Braniff - Wells Rich Greene - 1965 - homophone sells design.
- **You don't have to be Jewish to love Levy's** - DDB - 1961 - names the assumption it overturns.
- **There are some things money can't buy. For everything else, there's MasterCard.** - McCann - 1997 - a repeatable format.
- **The man your man could smell like** - Old Spice - W+K - 2010 - talks to the buyer, not the user.

## 7. Challenger and social
- **Murder Your Thirst** - Liquid Death - 2019 - genre parody makes water noticeable.
- **Wow No Cow** - Oatly - three words, rhyme, category contrast.
- **A party game for horrible people** - Cards Against Humanity - 2011 - the pitch insults its buyers, who love it.
- **Privacy. That's iPhone.** - Apple - 2019 - noun sentence plus a confident full stop.

## 8. Anthemic
- **It's morning again in America** - Hal Riney - 1984 - warm narration sells a mood.
- **A different kind of company. A different kind of car.** - Saturn - Hal Riney & Partners - 1990 - parallel structure.

## 9. Direct-response classics
- **They laughed when I sat down at the piano - but when I started to play!** - John Caples - 1926 - underdog story in one line.
- **Do you make these mistakes in English?** - Max Sackheim - 1919 - a self-test.
- **Always a bridesmaid, but never a bride** - Listerine - 1925 - names a social fear.
- **At 60 miles an hour the loudest noise in this new Rolls-Royce comes from the electric clock** - Ogilvy - 1958 - one fact stands in for luxury.

## 10. Modern lines 2015-2026
| Line | Brand | The move |
|---|---|---|
| "Why Do It?" (2025) | Nike / W+K | Questions its own imperative to refresh it, then lands on "Just Do It" |
| "FCK" (2018) | KFC / Mother London | The logo rearranged into an apology |
| "Ketchup is Heinz" | Heinz | Claims the category itself |
| "Should've gone to Specsavers" | Specsavers | A payoff that became a catchphrase |
| "Toilet paper that builds toilets" | Who Gives A Crap | Product and mission in one symmetrical line |
| "The beauty of no artificial preservatives" | Burger King (Moldy Whopper, 2020) | Ugliness as proof |
| "We're Dwayne Johnson's favourite cereal" (2023) | Surreal | Setup headline, deflating small print (a namesake) |
| "Premium wireless for $15/month" | Mint Mobile | Price-first clarity in a deadpan voice |
