# Effectiveness evidence

Use when deciding emotion vs. reason, humour, abstraction, or one-off vs. long-running work.

## Binet & Field / IPA (large but self-selected award databank)
- About 60:40 brand-building to activation is the average optimum (B2B closer to 46:54). An average, not a law.
- Emotional campaigns outperform rational ones on long-term business effects and are almost twice as likely to deliver top-box profit growth. Rational campaigns give stronger short-term sales effects.
- Fame campaigns outperform on business metrics. Write things people will repeat.
- Campaigns aimed at the whole category beat loyalty-only campaigns. Avoid insider language in brand copy.
- Emotional effects build over years. Write platforms that can run.

## Peter Field, *The Crisis in Creative Effectiveness* (2019)
- Creatively awarded campaigns were about 12x as efficient in 1996-2008, under 4x by 2006-2018, as short-termism grew.
- Creativity multiplies effectiveness when deployed long-term and consistently. A clever one-off stunt is the weakest use of a great line.

## Orlando Wood / System1 (*Lemon*, *Look Out*)
The more right-brain features an ad has, the more likely it scores well; left-brain features go with weaker long-term effects. (The left/right-brain framing is a contested metaphor; the feature correlation is the useful part.)

| Write more | Write less |
|---|---|
| Characters with agency | Disembodied voiceover explaining benefits |
| Dialogue | Words on screen |
| A sense of place and time | Flat, placeless settings |
| Scenes that unfold | Features in isolation |
| Humour, wit, play | Literal claims ("X helps you Y") |
| Implicit meaning | Over-explanation |

Three things that hold attention: **character, incident, place.**

## Humour (Kantar)
- About a third of tested ads use humour, but about half of Kantar Creative Effectiveness Award winners do. Funny ads score higher on distinctiveness and involvement.
- Intentionally funny ads fell from 54% (2000) to 34% (2019).
- Ads that make people actually laugh beat ads that only intended to be funny.
- Consumer-survey claims like "90% remember funny ads" are self-reported; treat as anecdote.

## Write more / write less (summary)

| More | Less |
|---|---|
| Emotion, tension, story in brand work | Feature lists in brand work |
| People, dialogue, places | Abstract benefit claims |
| Humour that lands | Purpose platitudes |
| Lines built to run for years | One-off cleverness |
| Copy for the whole category | Loyalty-only insider language |
| Distinctive, repeatable assets | Generic category language |
| Specific reasons and offers in activation | Emotional vagueness on landing pages |
