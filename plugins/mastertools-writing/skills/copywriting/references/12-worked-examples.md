# Worked examples: before and after

Contents: A. Sourced rewrites · B. Illustrative rewrites by format · C. Annotation template · D. Long-form worth studying · E. Moves from Hey Whipple · F. Moves from real brand material

Examples steer output more than rules. Each pair names the moves. Evidence level: **tested** (a real A/B), **expert** (a published rewrite), **illustrative** (written for this skill to demonstrate a move - not a real campaign).

Move vocabulary: outcome-over-feature · customer words · specificity · active voice · de-nominalise · objection handling · outcome CTA · unique fact first · cut hype · vivid verb · contrast · reframe · scene · implication · enemy.

## B. Illustrative rewrites by format

**SaaS hero**
- Before: "The all-in-one platform to supercharge your workflow."
- After: "Close the books in 3 days, not 10."
- Moves: specificity (numbers replace "supercharge"); contrast; names the job.

**Tagline, challenger water brand**
- Before: "Pure refreshment for a better you."
- After: "Water with a grudge against plastic."
- Moves: enemy; implication (flatters the reader's values); ownable.

**Tagline, budget airline**
- Before: "Affordable travel without compromise."
- After: "The legroom's small. So's the fare."
- Moves: reframe (admit the flaw, make it proof); rhythm; parallel structure.

**Brand headline, outdoor jacket**
- Before: "Engineered for performance in any weather."
- After: "Tested in Glasgow. In February."
- Moves: scene and place; specificity; dry wit; proves "any weather" without saying it.

**Manifesto opening, running shoe**
- Before: "We believe everyone has a champion inside them."
- After: "Nobody claps at 6 a.m. Run anyway."
- Moves: scene (time, absence of crowd); tension; imperative ending.

**Email subject**
- Before: "Our latest newsletter: exciting updates inside!"
- After: "The 4pm slump is a sugar problem"
- Moves: specific claim; curiosity; cut hype.

**Launch email opening**
- Before: "We're thrilled to announce the launch of our brand new feature!"
- After: "4,312 requests later: dark mode."
- Moves: reader first; specificity; implication of listening.

**Social post**
- Before: "We're excited to share our new lemon flavour - you're going to love it!"
- After: "Lemon. Finally. The inbox can rest."
- Moves: dry voice; shows rather than gushes; fragments for rhythm.

**Brand social caption (voice first, offer second)**
- Before: "New: our coffee subscription. One bag a week, $14, free delivery. Link in bio."
- After: "Dave roasts in a garden shed and won't explain the playlist. Now he roasts for subscribers too. One bag a week, $14."
- Moves: character opens; dry voice; the offer arrives once the reader cares.

**Empty state**
- Before: "No data available."
- After: "No projects yet. Create one to start tracking time."
- Moves: why it's empty + next action.

**Error**
- Before: "Invalid input."
- After: "Enter the date as DD/MM/YYYY."
- Moves: how to fix; no blame.

**Nonprofit appeal**
- Before: "Your donation helps fight hunger in our community."
- After: "$30 fills Maria's fridge for a week. She's eight."  *(Illustrative; a real appeal needs a real, consented story.)*
- Moves: one person over statistics; concrete ask; scene.

**B2B ad**
- Before: "Robust, scalable security solutions for the modern enterprise."
- After: "The intern clicked the link. Nothing happened."
- Moves: scene; customer's real fear; implication; enemy (the phishing email).

## D. Long-form worth studying (described, not reproduced)
- **David Abbott's long-copy press ads** (Sainsbury's, Volvo): a conversational argument; every paragraph earns the next; facts deliver the wit.
- **Patagonia "Don't Buy This Jacket"**: lists the jacket's environmental costs in specifics, turning a product ad into an argument against buying; specificity makes the sincerity credible.
- **Monzo's tone-of-voice page**: a style guide written in the voice it describes; it persuades rather than dictates.
- **Innocent labels**: chatty asides in odd places (bottle bottoms) reward close reading and turn packaging into media.

## E. Moves from *Hey Whipple* (3rd ed.), described not reproduced

| Move | Example (paraphrased) | Page |
|---|---|---|
| Write like you talk | A 143-word corporate memo that meant only: the programme was killed because it cost too much | pp.99-100 |
| Voice is testable | Six car headlines with names removed; three from a plain-spoken small-car maker are instantly told apart from three by a German luxury maker | p.101 |
| Reveal at the end | The same airline line about arriving feeling human, rewritten with the reveal mid-sentence, loses its surprise | pp.91-92 |
| Own one word | Ask people in any city what Volvo stands for and they give the same one-word answer | p.28 |
| Flank the category | Heinz claimed "slow" while rivals competed on tomato taste, and sales rose | p.28 |
| Leave work to the reader | A camera ad made of black panels that describe famous photographs in words, so readers develop the pictures in their heads | pp.62-64 |
| Proof over claim | A MINI strapped to the roof of an SUV, asking about weekend plans | pp.56-57 |
| The naive question | VW's snowplow spot: how does the snowplow driver get to his snowplow? | p.39 |
| One sight gag, whole argument | Two hammers smash two bugs; the line asks which worm is deader | pp.122-123 |
| Felt moment, played straight | A banker calmly reads out mortgage paperwork while a young couple's nerves carry the scene | pp.201-202 |
| Candour recruits the right people | An old polar-expedition notice that promised hardship, danger and doubtful return, and only recognition if successful | pp.255-256 |
| Dramatise, don't demonstrate | Pizza cheese that stretches like rubber; the client objected that real cheese doesn't, but audiences read it as a joke about "more cheese" | pp.258-260 |
| Change the product, not the claim | A beer printed conversation starters on a second label to prove it was sociable | pp.188-189 |
| Present as strategy, not taste | "This visual is a killer" becomes "this visual says durability, as the research showed" | p.243 |

## F. Moves from real brand material in the reference library

- **Contrast pair, then checkable proof** (Hamilton Family Brewery About page): a lofty product claim paired with a down-to-earth description of its customers, proved by things a visitor can see (glass walls onto the brewhouse; the tasting-room tables are made in-house).
- **Values as refusals sound owned** (Pebble template): values that reject a corporate cliché ("a team, not a family") read as real.
- **A small funny fact humanises a timeline** (Pebble template): the first roaster named after the founder's dog.
- **A constraint inside the challenge** (Blind case study): grow sales and recognition *without* alienating existing fans - the tension makes the result feel earned.
- **From-to case blurbs** (BLVR deck): what the brand was, what it became, in three or four sentences, then "full study on request".
- **Say what's kept and what's cut in a rebrand** (LA Chargers): evolved and simplified, not reinvented.
