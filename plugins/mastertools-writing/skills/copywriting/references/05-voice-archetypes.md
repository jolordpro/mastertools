# Voice archetypes

Voice is the constant personality. Tone shifts with the reader's mood. Ask before writing: is the reader relieved, confused, angry, celebrating?

| Archetype | Exemplars | Sentence markers | Do | Don't | Fits / backfires |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Irreverent challenger | Liquid Death, Oatly, Cards Against Humanity, Dollar Shave Club | Mock-serious claims; dark puns; meta-commentary about advertising | Name a real enemy; commit everywhere (pack, site, replies) | Go halfway | Fits commodity categories. Backfires if the product is weak or the joke punches down |
| Minimalist confident | Apple | Fragments; noun sentences; full stops for emphasis; picturable numbers | Cut until it hurts; let one specific carry the page | Stack adjectives; explain the joke | Fits design-led brands with a real edge. Empty pretension without it |
| Inspirational / anthemic | Nike, "Think Different", Saturn | Second person; imperatives; short lines building to a peak | Ground it in real people and effort | Generic uplift ("unleash your potential") | Fits brands with cultural authority. Otherwise purpose-washing |
| Warm, human, helpful | Mailchimp, Innocent, Monzo | Contractions; plain English; small jokes in small places | Clarity first, charm second | Patronise; joke during errors or billing | Fits SaaS, food, service. Backfires when cuteness hides bad news |
| Dry British wit | The Economist, Carlsberg, Marmite, Guinness | Understatement; implication; hedges; no exclamation marks | Let the reader finish the joke | Over-explain; smugness | Fits audiences who prize intelligence |
| Plainspoken honest | Patagonia, VW (DDB), Avis | Short declaratives; numbers; confessions | Back every claim with behaviour | Claim virtue you don't practise | Fits challengers and purpose brands; hypocrisy if behaviour diverges |
| Playful / unhinged social | Duolingo, Wendy's, Ryanair, Maximum Effort | Lowercase; memes; roasts; deadpan replies | One trusted owner; respond in hours | Slow approval chains; roasting customers who didn't ask | Fits youth and entertainment brands. Backfires in regulated categories and crises |

## Lessons from cases
- **Wendy's #NuggsForCarter (2017):** a roast that ended generously (nuggets plus a $100,000 donation) - generosity protects a sharp voice.
- **Duolingo:** a mascot voice is an asset, but it can be hit by corporate news it can't joke away; it was toned down after the 2025 "AI-first" backlash.
- **Mailchimp:** "It's always more important to be clear than entertaining." When unsure, keep a straight face.

## Building a voice for a new brand
1. Name the enemy or convention the brand rejects.
2. Choose 3 traits, each with "this, not that" (confident, not cocky).
3. Scope where each trait applies (Monzo: kindness everywhere, wit mostly in marketing).
4. Write one sample per situation: celebration, error/apology, sales page, social reply.
5. List words always used and never used.
6. Cover the logo - would people still know it's you? (Ann Handley)

## Notes from *Hey Whipple* and real brand guides
- A brand voice is a personality sustained for years. To inherit one, read the old work until you hear its cadence (Sullivan pp.100-101).
- Don't impose your own style on a strategy; present the client's case memorably and your style will show anyway (p.102).
- Puns and wordplay suit challengers; mainstream and trust-led brands often forbid them (Hyundai). Check the brand's guide before using them.
- Prefer flawed, specific, recognisable people over polished stock models in scenes; audiences see themselves in the schlemiel (Sullivan p.261).
- Own a visual or verbal territory a reader can recognise at a distance, and avoid current trends, which date the work (pp.95-98).
