# Copywriter profiles ("write like X")

Each: philosophy · signature move · how to imitate the move without copying the work.

## Anchors
- **Luke Sullivan** (*Hey Whipple, Squeeze This*, 1998; 3rd ed. 2008 - read from a print copy; 6th ed. 2022). Most advertising is merely adequate, and ads that sell while irritating people are still bad ads. Simple is almost always better (his stated single takeaway); write 100 headlines, all decent; count different "doors" into the problem; write hot, edit cold; the interesting part must be the sales message; leave part of the work to the reader; tell the unflattering truth. Later editions add truth + conflict = platform and social-era loops. *Imitate:* find the unflattering truth, pick a fight with something, say it plainly first, then say it well, and cut a third.
- **Seth Godin** (*Purple Cow*, *Permission Marketing*, *This Is Marketing*). Marketing makes change happen for a specific group; be remarkable; "people like us do things like this". *Imitate:* short paragraphs, one idea, plain words, an aphoristic last line.

## Classic creative
- **Bill Bernbach** (DDB). Creativity rooted in truth; respect the reader; writer + art director pairs. *Imitate:* honest, witty, editorial; admit the flaw.
- **David Abbott** (AMV). Wit and charm make a plain position convincing. *Imitate:* one-line posters; long copy that is a conversational argument where every paragraph earns the next.
- **Paula Green** (DDB). Wrote Avis "We Try Harder". *Imitate:* make the underdog position the promise.
- **Mary Wells Lawrence** (Wells Rich Greene). Theatre and colour applied to brands.
- **Hal Riney.** Warm, slow, folksy narration that sells a mood.
- **George Lois.** The big idea as provocation.
- **Neil French.** Irreverent long copy that breaks ad conventions.
- **Tony Brignull.** Precise, humane, rhythmic British long copy.
- **Lee Clow** (TBWA\Chiat\Day). Advertising as cultural event and long-running platform ("1984", Think Different).
- **Rob Siltanen & Ken Segall.** Wrote "Here's to the crazy ones" (1997): list the insults, reframe them as praise.
- **Jeff Goodby** (Goodby Silverstein). Insight-led humour (Got Milk? deprivation).
- **Alex Bogusky** (CP+B). Ideas as PR stunts and interactive experiences.
- **Dan Wieden** (W+K). Brands as cultural voices; coined "Just Do It".

## Modern brand and strategy
- **Dave Trott.** Impact > communication > persuasion; single-minded proposition; think upstream. *Imitate:* blunt, short, contrarian.
- **Rory Sutherland** (Ogilvy UK, *Alchemy*). Psycho-logic over logic; small reframes; test the counterintuitive. *Imitate:* change how something is perceived rather than what it is.
- **Mark Ritson.** Strategy before tactics; distinctiveness plus relative differentiation; say less, more often.
- **Byron Sharp / Jenni Romaniuk.** Growth through mental and physical availability; distinctive assets must be famous and unique.
- **John Schoolcraft** (Oatly). Long, rambling, self-deprecating packaging copy that talks about its own advertising.
- **John Thornton** (Innocent, then Surreal). Chatty asides; setup-and-deflate posters.
- **Vikki Ross.** Voice and CTA heuristics.

## Modern conversion
- **Joanna Wiebe** (Copyhackers). Voice-of-customer mining; message first.
- **Harry Dry** (Marketing Examples). Visualise, falsify, make it ownable; write with the delete key; bold claim plus overwhelming proof.
- **Eddie Shleyner** (VeryGoodCopy). Short story-led persuasion.
- **Ann Handley** (*Everybody Writes*). Ugly first drafts; empathy; voice recognisable without the logo.
- **April Dunford** (*Obviously Awesome*). Positioning before messaging.

## Direct response (transferable)
- **David Ogilvy.** Research first; the headline does most of the work; facts persuade; respect the consumer.
- **Eugene Schwartz.** Copy channels existing desire; match awareness and sophistication.
- **Joseph Sugarman.** Every line's job is to get the next line read.
- **Gary Halbert.** A starving crowd beats clever copy; write like you talk.
- **John Caples.** Tested headline formulas.
- **Claude Hopkins.** Reason-why, specifics, testing, the preemptive claim (say what everyone does but nobody says).
- **Robert Collier.** Enter the conversation already in the reader's head.
