## 1. Building a voice system

**NN/g's four tone sliders:** Funny - Serious · Formal - Casual · Respectful - Irreverent · Enthusiastic - Matter-of-fact. Users notice tone differences; casual and conversational tones read as friendlier.

Recipe:
1. Pick 3 voice traits (Monzo: straightforward kindness, everyday magic, warm wit).
2. **Scope each trait** - say where it applies. Monzo uses kindness everywhere, magic mainly in marketing, wit almost only in brand work. This is the most useful idea in modern voice guides.
3. For each trait, write "this, not that" pairs (confident, not cocky; plain, not dumbed-down).
4. Word list: banned > replacement.
5. Tone map: place onboarding, error, outage, promo, apology and legal on the four sliders. Voice stays; tone moves.
6. Three before/after examples per situation.

Deliverable shape for a voice guide: traits with this-not-that pairs and scope · tone map table · word list · before/after examples · one short sample in the voice.

## 4. Voice discovery (when a brand has no voice yet)

Ask these in one pass, then draft (Column Five brand strategy toolkit and voice questionnaire):
1. How should people feel after dealing with us?
2. Which 3-5 adjectives describe us?
3. What do our competitors sound like?
4. Whose voice do we love, and why does it work on us? (Find the why; never imitate.)
5. If the brand were a famous person, who?
6. How do we want to talk about ourselves?
7. What do we refuse to be - including words and phrases we hate?

Then:
- **Keep a "not us" list** beside the traits. A banned list is faster to apply than an adjective.
- **Run a spectrum exercise** with stakeholders: paired sliders (traditional-progressive, corporate-friendly, serious-playful, understated-bold, familiar-disruptive, realistic-idealistic). Keep only the 3-5 everyone agrees on. Discovery can collect 3-5 adjectives; distil to three working traits for drafting.
- **Audit three competitors' voices** (tone, tagline, how they talk about customers) and state in one line where this voice differs.
- **Define traits by behaviour, not adjective.** "Formal, confident, respectful" could describe a butler or a president. Cast the brand as an actor, say what it finds funny and how it shows anger (Ben Burns, style-guide guidebook).
- **Persona shorthand** ("a famous person, but with another's humour") is for aligning stakeholders only - then convert it into what the voice would and wouldn't say.
- **Add a limit clause** so one trait can't swallow another (earnest and welcoming, never at the cost of quality; playful, always honest) - Hamilton Family Brewery guide.
- **When adjectives feel generic, describe the personality as a relationship scene:** the friend you can go months without calling and pick up with as if no time passed.
- **Paired traits are how clients naturally talk** ("commanding but kind") - each pair is a ready-made "this, not that".
- **Reduce big trait lists.** A guide with 15 traits can't steer a sentence; collapse them into a short chant plus the cultural world behind it, then three working traits (LA Chargers guide).

## 5. What a strong voice guide contains
- **Separate rules by text layer:** headlines (e.g. bold, short, drawn from real life), subheads, and body copy (crisp and informative, never dry) do different jobs (Hyundai).
- **A "never sounds like" list of named failure types**, not general warnings: e.g. fixating on product specs, brash or arrogant claims, aggressive or macho edge, puns and brand wordplay, heavy jargon (Hyundai).
- **A one-paragraph stance** on selling, humour and contractions: e.g. copy shouldn't feel like selling, the brand isn't trying to be witty, jokes never at anyone's expense, contractions because we write how people talk (Pebble template). Three or four such decisions steer more copy than ten adjectives.
- **Pillars turned into copy behaviours** (richness in simplicity: plain words, shared moments; refined detail: particulars over superlatives).
- **A tone map by content type,** not only by the reader's mood (comedic vs. dramatic promos get different treatments).
- **Tagline usage rules** (see 03).
- **A master style list** of mechanical choices the reference style doesn't settle: headline case, "&" vs "and", list punctuation, dash spacing, spelling variant, phone, time and date formats, abbreviations - plus a named fallback authority (AP, Chicago). Record the categories; the choices are each brand's own.
- **State the guide's intent:** it exists to help people make on-brand work faster and is a living document, not a rulebook.
- **Write the guide in the brand voice** - the quickest way to find out what the voice is (Burns; also Monzo). Offer key sections such as the welcome in two registers (warm, or professional) so the client can choose.
