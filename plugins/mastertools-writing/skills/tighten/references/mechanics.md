# Mechanics — the tightening move-list as rules

Read this on every run. Each move is a rule the passes apply. Every edit should
trace to one of these by name, so the changelog is auditable and the skill stays
inside the documented style instead of drifting to generic polish.

## Contents

2. Strip moves
3. Sharpen moves
4. Sound moves

6. Ship moves
7. Why fluency is the authority move

---

## 1. The one principle

**Deciding what matters is the writer's job, not the reader's.** Sort the pile
first. Decide the single thing that matters, cut the rest, put that thing where
it cannot be missed. Every move below serves this.

---

## 2. Strip moves (subtract)

**Open cold.** Delete the throat-clearing. Start inside the specific thing.
- Before: *In today's fast-moving media landscape, brands face many challenges.*
- After: *Two weeks before launch, the client killed the logo.*

**One idea per line.** A sentence carries one thought. Split the ones carrying
two. White space is pacing, like edit points.

**Kill hedges.** Cut *often, usually, arguably, I think, sort of, in many cases.*
A hedge is where authority leaks out.
- Before: *This might, in many cases, be a fairly strong option.*
- After: *This is the strongest option.*  (only if true — else say what is true)

**Cut filler.** Delete *really, very, just, actually, basically, simply.* They
add length, not weight.

**Collapse redundancy.** Say it once. "Free gift," "future plans," "end result"
each waste a word.

---

## 3. Sharpen moves (sentence surgery)

**Antithesis ("Not X. Y.").** Set up the expected thing, pivot to the true
thing. The contrast carries it. Cap at one per short run of sentences.
- *This isn't decoration. It's wayfinding.*
- *We're not selling the game. We're selling the two hours around it.*

**Load-bearing short line.** After a run of long sentences, drop a short one. It
rings. The short line is the one they remember.

**Concrete over abstract.** Swap a vague adjective for a number or a name.
Numbers and names read as true; adjectives read as opinion.
- Before: *an award-winning creative leader with extensive experience*
- After: *Two design awards. Twelve years. Three agencies, now a product studio.*
- When the specific is missing, write `[NEEDS: the real number/name]`. Never
  invent it.

**Active voice.** The subject does the thing.
- Before: *The campaign was seen by millions.*
- After: *Millions saw the campaign.*

**Cut jargon, keep the weight.** Plain word over fancy word, without thinning the
idea. "Habit formation methodologies" becomes "small habits that stick."

**Definitional reframe.** Define an abstract thing by setting it equal to a
familiar mechanism. Pattern: *[abstract idea] is the [familiar mechanism] of
[domain].*
- *A rebrand is the compound interest of reputation.*
- *A style guide is the muscle memory of a team.*

---

## 4. Sound moves (rhythm and fluency)

**Read aloud, fix the stumble.** The sentence you trip on is the one to cut. Edit
by ear.

**Vary sentence length.** Long, long, short. Rhythm is a pulse. Monotone is the
tell of a machine, and the script measures variance, so protect it.

**Em dashes to periods.** The punch a dash reaches for, a period delivers. Hold
the one-dash-per-paragraph limit; prefer none. Most dashes are periods in a
costume.

**Keep one sensory detail.** Even at its plainest, good prose leaves one concrete
image standing. Deploy it surgically: one vivid image per abstract point, not a
paragraph of them.

---

## 5. Stick moves (make it land and travel)

**The analogy engine — make the invisible visible.** For any abstract point,
find a physical thing the reader has already touched, and walk it beat by beat so
the shape of the sentence mirrors the idea. This is the highest-value move for
selling craft to people who cannot see it.
- Why 200ms of easing matters: *A door that closes with a soft click feels
  expensive. A door that slams feels cheap. Same door. The close is the whole
  feeling.*
- Rules: pick a familiar physical thing; narrate in real time; add one sensory
  detail; prove it once, cleanly. Do not invent an analogy that misleads, and do
  not add one to a point that is already concrete.

**Name the idea.** A named idea gets a handle someone can carry into a room you
are not in. Make the name concrete, not clever ("plateau," not "synergy
framework"). Attach a number when you can (Four Laws, not Several Laws).

**Make it portable.** Package the idea as a carry-able question, rule of thumb,
or test — specific enough to be useful, general enough to travel. *"Does it pass
the back-row squint?"* beats *"we value legibility."*

**Forge the key line to survive alone.** The one line at the top — the tagline,
the deck headline, the subject line — should still work screenshotted with
everything around it gone. See `aphorism-recipe.md`.

---

## 6. Ship moves (finalize and check)

**Strip the timestamp.** Cut trends, news hooks, dated slang. Write what will
still be true in ten years. Timeless reads as received wisdom and compounds.

**Honesty check.** Flag any line that sounds smooth but says nothing. Fluency
covering thin thinking is the one failure that matters. Sound smart because the
thinking is sound, not instead of it.

**Banned-word scrub.** Remove every entry in `banned-words.md`. Each one is a
tell that the sentence reached for the sound of importance instead of the thing.

---

## 7. Why fluency is the authority move

Not decoration. There is a documented cognitive bias — processing fluency, the
fluency heuristic — where easy-to-read statements are judged more likely to be
true, and their authors judged more intelligent. Short words, clean structure,
rhythm, and familiarity all trigger it, independent of content.

So the plainness is the credibility strategy. A clean rationale does not just get
understood faster; it gets believed more, and it makes the writer look sharper.
The caution rides along: the same ease sells thin ideas, which is why the honesty
check exists. Use fluency to land real things. Never to cover for thinking you
skipped.
