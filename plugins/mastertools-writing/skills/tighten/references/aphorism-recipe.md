# Aphorism recipe — compress a line into a maxim

Read this at Stick, when a piece needs one line that survives on its own: a
tagline, a headline, a deck's spine, the sentence people repeat. Not every
sentence should be a maxim. Most should not. This is for the one that carries the
whole thing.

## What a maxim has

The lines that get repeated share a shape:

- **Brevity.** Short enough to hold in one breath.
- **Balance and contrast.** Two halves set against each other. Rise and fall.
  Goals and systems. Once and repeatedly. The symmetry makes it feel finished,
  like a law rather than a thought.
- **Present tense, universal address.** A flat statement of how things are, not
  "I found that." This is what makes it feel permanent.
- **A turn.** The second half bends the first. The reader feels a small click.
  That click is the point.
- **No hedge.** No "often," no "I think," no "usually." Qualifiers are exactly
  where authority leaks out.
- **Stands alone.** Works screenshotted with everything around it gone.

## How to find one (you find them, you do not write them)

The clean maxim is the output of saying one idea many ways until only the
smallest, hardest version is left. The method is dull and repeatable:

1. Write the idea ten different ways. Ugly is fine.
2. Read each aloud. Cut a word.
3. Keep the version that survives. Delete the rest.
4. Repeat until you cannot remove anything without breaking it.

The maxim is what is left when nothing else can come out.

## Guardrails

- **Do not force it.** If the plain sentence is stronger, keep the plain
  sentence. A strained maxim is worse than a clear statement.
- **Do not fake profundity.** A line that sounds deep and means nothing fails
  the honesty check. The turn has to land on something true.
- **Do not fabricate.** If the sharp version needs a specific the writer has not
  supplied, `[NEEDS: ...]`. Never invent the fact to make the line sing.

## Worked passes (illustrative)

- Flabby: *It's important to remember that consistency over time tends to matter
  more than big one-off efforts.*
  Sharper: *Big efforts win a day. Small habits win the decade.*
- Flabby: *Our editing tool has guardrails, which some people find limiting but
  which actually help produce better work.*
  Sharper: *The limits aren't in your way. They're the reason it's good.*
- Flabby: *A brand is really about how consistent you are across everything you
  make over a long period.*
  Sharper: *A brand isn't a logo. It's what survives repetition.*
