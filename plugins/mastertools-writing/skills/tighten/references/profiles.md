# Profiles — classify, then treat

Read this at Frame. Match the piece to a profile before editing. This is what
makes the skill work in every context instead of running one recipe on a tagline
and a 2,000-word essay alike. Each profile turns on the rules that fit, sets a
word budget and sentence-length target, and names what to switch OFF.

Targets are guides, not gates. The reading grade is approximate (the script uses
a heuristic). A profile that fits the audience beats a number that fits a
formula.

The four deepest profiles — website copy, blog post, newsletter, email — come
first, because they are where this writer lives.

---

## Website copy  (deep)

**Job:** make a visitor understand what this is and what to do, fast, and trust
it. Most visitors scan. Write for the scan.

- **Budget:** ruthless. A hero line is 3-9 words. A subhead is one sentence. A
  section is 2-4 short sentences. Cut every word the page can live without.
- **Sentence target:** short, average 8-14 words, high variance. Fragments are
  fine and often stronger.
- **Turn ON:** open cold (no "Welcome to"), one idea per block, concrete over
  abstract (a real number beats "trusted by many"), the key line survives alone,
  benefit before feature, one clear next action.
- **Turn OFF:** long build-ups, story openings, paragraph stacks. A homepage is
  not a blog post.
- **Hero-line rule:** it is a promise the page must keep. Say the specific thing
  the product does, not how it feels to use it. *"Ship your podcast in an hour"*
  beats *"Reimagine your creative workflow."*
- **Watch for:** the AI-homepage tell — "the perfect blend of X and Y,"
  "whether you're a beginner or a pro," "look no further." Kill on sight.

## Blog post  (deep)

**Job:** land one idea and make the reader act on it. One post, one idea.

- **Budget:** as long as the idea needs, no longer. Most posts are 30% too long;
  cut to the load-bearing lines.
- **Sentence target:** average 12-18 words, high variance, short lines to punch.
- **Structure:** Story, then Principle, then Action. Open cold on a concrete
  scene or a sharp claim. Nobody leans in for a principle; everybody leans in for
  a problem. Close by handing the reader the next move.
- **Turn ON:** the analogy engine for the abstract stretch, one named idea if it
  deserves a handle, subheads that each carry one idea, timelessness (strip
  dated refs).
- **Turn OFF:** the summary intro ("In this post, I'll cover..."). Just start.
- **Reading grade:** aim 6-9. Simple sentences, not simple ideas.

## Newsletter  (deep)

**Job:** be worth opening every time. Trust compounds through consistency.

- **Budget:** short. Respect the inbox. A 3-2-1-style issue runs under ~500
  words; a single-idea issue can be far shorter.
- **Format discipline:** a fixed shape removes the blank page. If the writer has
  a format (e.g. a weekly ratio of ideas / quotes / one question), keep it and
  fill it. The format is the discipline; the content is the easy part.
- **Turn ON:** one idea per item, a portable line the reader can carry into
  their week, a question that hands them the spotlight at the end.
- **Turn OFF:** meandering personal preamble unless it is the point. Get to the
  value fast.
- **Voice:** warmer than website copy, tighter than a blog post. This is where
  Clarity gear and the writer's warmth meet most evenly.

## Email  (deep)

**Job:** get one thing across and, usually, get one thing done. Respect that the
reader is busy and skimming.

- **Budget:** short. Most business email is 2x too long. One ask, stated plainly,
  usually near the top.
- **Sentence target:** conversational, average 10-16 words. Contractions on.
- **Structure:** a human opener (one line, real, not "I hope this finds you
  well"), the point, the ask, done. Bold or isolate the single ask so a skim
  catches it.
- **Turn ON:** cold-ish open, one idea per paragraph, kill hedges (hedged asks
  get ignored), concrete next step with a real date/name where needed
  (`[NEEDS: ...]` if missing).
- **Turn OFF:** maxim-forcing and heavy analogy. An email is not an essay. Keep
  the person, keep it plain, get out.
- **Caution:** do not strip all warmth from a relationship email. A cold-open
  rule is for the first sentence, not the whole tone.

## Lighter profiles

### Tagline / hero one-liner
- **Budget:** 3-8 words. Every word load-bearing.
- **Turn OFF:** "open cold" and "one idea per line" — a tagline is one unit.
- **Turn ON:** survives alone, concrete, a turn or contrast if it earns one, zero
  filler. Read it aloud fifty times; keep the version that survives. This is
  aphorism work — see `aphorism-recipe.md`.

### Headline / subject line
- **Budget:** under ~12 words. A promise with a specific payoff.
- **Frames:** *The [approach] to [specific outcome]*; *How [surprising thing]
  leads to [result]*; *The surprising benefit of [simple thing]*. Make it a
  contract the piece keeps. No clickbait the body cannot honor.

### Bio
- **Turn ON:** concrete over abstract (numbers, named wins, real places),
  load-bearing short lines, timelessness.
- **Turn OFF:** banter and hedging. Clean and specific. Profanity in a
  bio only if the writer's own rules allow it.

### Social post
- **Budget:** short, one idea, a strong first line (the hook that stops the
  scroll). Standalone and quotable. One post, one thought.

### Long-form (essay, case study, white paper, deck narrative)
- **Structure:** Story-Principle-Action per section; lead with stakes and the
  struggle, not the polished result. Name ideas so they travel. Cut hard to the
  spine, then build back only what is needed.
- **Reading grade:** keep sentences simple even when ideas are not.

## Story-gear (protect — do not flatten)

Brand films, memorials, founder and brand narratives, anything meant to move
someone emotionally. These want the long building sentence, the sensory image,
the earned pause. That is the writer's native strength, not a defect to fix.

- **Default:** stop and warn. Offer **Tighten-lite**: fix only throat-clearing,
  dead hedges, obvious redundancy, and banned words. Leave rhythm, imagery, and
  the fuller voice alone.
- **Never:** shorten every sentence, force antithesis, or strip the sensory
  detail. Tightening a memorial guts it.
