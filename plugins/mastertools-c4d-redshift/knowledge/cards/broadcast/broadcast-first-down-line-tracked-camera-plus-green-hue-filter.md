Three high cameras feed computers holding a 3D field model captured earlier by laser scanning; pan, tilt and zoom are captured for every frame so the real and virtual views align and the line is drawn in perspective [03:43-04:15]. To tuck the line under players but on top of grass, software keys on the average hue range of the grass, like a green screen, and a technician watches those hue values throughout the game since weather and light change [04:46]. It was introduced in 1998 and was seen as a storytelling device [03:11].

**Use it:** for a broadcast graphic that must sit on the field, the two dependencies are per-frame camera data and a grass key that someone watches during the game.
