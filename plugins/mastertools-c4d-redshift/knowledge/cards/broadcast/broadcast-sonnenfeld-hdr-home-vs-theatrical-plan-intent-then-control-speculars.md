Guest: Stefan Sonnenfeld (Company 3 founder and colorist), host Glenn Kaiser of Dolby (a vendor podcast).
For Dolby Vision he says theatrical peak brightness is a little over twice a normal theatre's (captions say "108", read as nits; unsure) and home video is much brighter again, so speculars must be watched or contrast "goes nuts" and pulls viewers out of the film [18:29-19:00].
His approach: agree a creative plan first, then emulate it in every format; use the extra range in chosen scenes, and set brightness per shot and scene to suit the story [19:00-19:31].
He pushes teams to work in HDR through the whole pipeline now that storage is cheap, and calls bad Dolby work hard to watch [07:27-08:32].
On benefits: more depth and detail in highlights (clouds out of a window that used to be blown white), though that can also expose problems [13:18-13:49].

**Use it:** For HDR deliverables, set the peak-brightness plan per scene and check bright highlights and emissive elements in the home HDR version, not only the SDR.
