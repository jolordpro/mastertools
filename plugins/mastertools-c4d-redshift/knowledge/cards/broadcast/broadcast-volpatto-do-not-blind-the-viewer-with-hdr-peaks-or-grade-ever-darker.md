He mocks showing 4,000 nits for 20 seconds and then 0.01 nits because it is possible; if you blind the observer you are not showing the image [34:28-35:30]. He likens it to poking the viewer's eye in stereo 3D [34:59].
The recommended viewing surround dropped from around 10 nits (SDR) to around 5 nits for HDR, which pushes every colourist and DP to grade darker even though modern TVs give a good picture in a normal room [43:54-44:55]. He did not name the standard clearly in the captions, so check before citing.
His general principle: ask what the technology should do for the story, not what it can do [45:28-46:29].

**Use it:** Test HDR graphics and grades in a normally lit room, and avoid sudden jumps from black to peak white.
