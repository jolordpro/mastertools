ELEVEN Sports (Belgium) wanted statistics and team presentations shown at pitch level, in two languages at once: one AR feed for the French channel and one for the Flemish channel [40:33-43:51]. DreamWall was shortlisted at the Broadcast Awards for creative use of AR.
Rig as described: one small crane per channel gives enough movement on its own. Baras suggests adding a PTZ (Panasonic UE150 is the one he names) for a wider or closer shot [44:58-46:33]. One project used two PTZ plus a crane. Each camera has its own render engine and its AR feed goes to the OB van, so the director just cuts between sources [56:13].
Tracking is Stype mechanical tracking on the main camera, with the lens calibrated by Stype, because stickers cannot be put around a stadium [55:39, 1:00:08]. Baras says he sticks to what he knows works. He says Reality has a pre-calibrated tracking node for Panasonic and Sony PTZ, and that PTZ chips look a little worse in poor light but are acceptable for AR [1:01:47-1:03:24].
Setup cost: arrive early, place the crane and calibrate tracking before the match [44:58].

**Use it:** When costing a stadium AR job, count crane, PTZ, tracking calibration time and one render engine per camera, not one per show.
