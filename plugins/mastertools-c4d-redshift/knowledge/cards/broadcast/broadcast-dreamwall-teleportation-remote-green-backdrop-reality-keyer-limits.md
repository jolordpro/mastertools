Baras on RTBF and TF1 teleportation (2018 on) [25:08-39:00]. The remote site needs only a small green backdrop, a green cube if the guest's arm should rest on something, matched in height to the studio desk. The camera feed is sent to the studio and keyed as a 2D plane with Reality Keyer.
Hardest parts: audio delay and a duplex talkback path (the guest wears one earphone to hear the presenter), and bandwidth. In 2018 bandwidth was a big limit; he now recommends sending 4K and streaming it [34:05-35:08]. Zoom or Teams calls gave poor quality and no control over sound delay, so he dismisses them for this [35:08-36:14].
Realism tricks: let the teleported person appear behind a real desk, rest an arm on it, add a coffee cup, or hand a real object across [27:55-31:49]. Angle limit with a flat 2D image: rotate the studio camera only about 25-30 degrees around the guest [37:53]. A robotic camera can be mirrored at the remote end by sending tracking data [39:28-40:33].
The moderator (Zero Density) adds that Reality Keyer is image-based and keys per pixel from a clean plate, so a non-uniform backdrop such as a curtain still keys [38:27]. That is a vendor claim.

**Use it:** Brief any teleport segment with: matched desk height, clean plate shot, one-way delay budget for audio, and a camera move limit of about 30 degrees.
