On the Deakins forum a moderator (dmullenasc) says exposing to the right is a still-photography concept of limited use in cinema, because a sequence should be exposed so its shots fall in the same correction range, as with similar printer light values on film [§1]. In his example room with a person 3 stops under, one at normal exposure and one in sunlight, pushing each to the right would need about 3 stops and about 6 stops of over-exposure, and nobody on set would see the real look [§1]. Shots darkened a lot later also show less noise than ones left alone, so he advises choosing one ISO for the whole scene and exposing each set-up for the creative look [§2]. A small safety bump for a dim subject is fine, and a lower ISO helps for green screens or very dark subjects [§2].

**Use it:** When asked about exposing to the right, ask whether it is a single still or a matched sequence, and advise a scene-wide ISO with look-based exposure.
