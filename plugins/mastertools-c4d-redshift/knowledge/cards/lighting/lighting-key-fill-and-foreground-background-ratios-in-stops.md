One stop = 2:1, two = 4:1, three = 8:1, four = 16:1 key to fill [21:11]. Set the key level first, then bring up or down the fill [21:48]. A natural fill is a large, soft source above and behind the camera, pointed slightly down; some fill reaches the key side, so the true ratio is key plus fill versus fill alone [21:48-22:49]. Negative fill (a large floppy on the shadow side) darkens the fill side; larger and closer takes more ambient away [22:49].
Foreground to background: subject 1 to 1.5 stops brighter than the background pops while staying natural [23:17]. Keep the key and background lights separate, move the subject away from walls (even pull a sofa 2-3 ft out), use a topper/barn doors/grids to stop spill [23:58-24:58].
Eyes see light logarithmically, so one stop looks less than twice as bright [20:39].

**Use it:** the lighting check can read key-to-fill and subject-to-background ratios from luminance in stops and name the ratio; default subject over background target is 1-1.5 stops.
