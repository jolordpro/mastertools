Lukas Thorup (Cinema 4D / Redshift artist with a YouTube channel) places the key first from the side and slightly above, so half the subject is lit and a Rembrandt triangle forms on the shadow side [04:23] [08:08]. Then the fill goes on the other side, deliberately turned down (he lands near 5 in his scene units) so shadows open up without flattening the subject [09:13]. The back light comes last, behind and opposite the key, to separate the subject from the background and give a halo [09:44].
His test for the back light: toggle it. Without it the statue's knee reads as a flat odd shape; with it the knee gets GI and a rim and turns 3D again [11:20]. He calls this a five-minute starting point for a render, not a finished look [11:52].
He picks this order because the fill is easiest to place once the key exists [09:13].

**Use it:** Use as the default recipe for a product or hero shot: key, fill, back, with a toggle test on each. In a lighting review, ask whether the back light is earning a shape the key cannot.
