Josh Toonen (film VFX artist and supervisor, 8 years) [14:20-16:31]. Draw an invisible line along where the actor looks (the eyeline / 180 line). Keep the key light on the far side of that line from the camera: from there almost any position works and it keeps producing a Rembrandt triangle under the eye. Farther away = softer and flatter; right beside the head = moody and contrasty. Height and position then move within that zone. Often rim light is added; fill and rim are used less because in a dark scene they turn the look into stage lighting. He prefers this over the three-point recipe, which gave him no reason why it worked and led to over-lighting simple scenes [01:02].

**Use it:** Starting point for any character or product-with-face lighting: place the key on the camera-far side of the eyeline, then test positions around that zone.
