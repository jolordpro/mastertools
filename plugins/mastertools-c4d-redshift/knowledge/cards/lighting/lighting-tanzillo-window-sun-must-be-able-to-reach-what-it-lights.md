Mike Tanzillo [21:20-23:25]: the sun patch on the back wall was much dimmer than the patch on the girl, and the window angle that was set could not reach that wall (it would land on the floor). Window light should feel the same across the room unless something outside shadows it.
So keep the back wall dark, hold the background tonality the same as the first shot, and spend the intensity on the characters. He calls it theatre or film-noir lighting: light in the area of action, dark around it.
Shadows on a back wall should also soften as they travel into depth [18:41].

**Use it:** Run a sun-reach check in a lighting pass: trace the light from window to surface and drop any lit area it could not physically reach.
