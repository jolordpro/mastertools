Fraser and the director knew the film could be so dark it became unwatchable, so the rule was: bright enough that we can see, not so dark that the mood is lost. Every scene had pockets of bright light. Batman stays outside the bright diner in shadow, and places he enters, such as the Iceberg Lounge, are kept down in tone so he fits. References were 1970s noir films and unnamed hobbyist night urban photos with just the right rain and mist. [§2]

**Use it:** For dark broadcast frames the coach checks for a readable subject and more than one tonal level, not simply a lower exposure.
