Thorup's whole method is stated at [25:02]: make the spotlight first, then slowly add fill until the subject is legible, and hold on to as many shadows as possible because shadows give contrast and interest.
On the jacket the fills were two big soft boxes at very low intensity (about 1) so they never overpower the key [23:30]. One sat under the arms and one to the right of the garment; he moved the second further out to bring shadows back, and said "you can always improve" [24:31].
Re-lit from scratch, a big white light behind the jacket, a small side facet light and an under-the-subject fill gave wrinkle definition and a cleaner light-to-dark transition [26:05] [29:29]. He judged the 3-light result about as good as, or better than, the first, a bit less puffy [30:01] [30:32].
He likes a diagonal beam crossing the object [28:27].

**Use it:** Review rule: if a subject looks flat, check whether fills outrank the key; cut fill intensity and move fills outward until shadows return. Build with fills at low values so the key still leads.
