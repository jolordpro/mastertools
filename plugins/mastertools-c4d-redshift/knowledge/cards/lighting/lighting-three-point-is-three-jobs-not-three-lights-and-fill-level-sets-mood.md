Teacher (Ruffle Boomboom) walks an Arnold scene [1:00-22:28]: a lone dome light gave a flat, "dystopian lab" look with only contact shadow telling you form [5:46]; add an area light as key about 45 to 60 degrees up and 45 to 60 degrees around from the camera, not from straight above or below [6:48]; add a fill on the opposite side from the camera, in his scene about a quarter of the key's intensity then lower again until it stopped looking like space [11:27-12:34]; rim sits opposite the camera, peeking over the object, and is bright (he used 100 vs key 100 with exposure tweaks) [14:07]. Lower or kill the fill and the frame gets dramatic; a bright fill reads as a ball on a floor [18:21]. The rim must not hit the floor, so he uses Maya's relationship editor, light linking [16:17]. Sitcom lighting: little shadow, lots of bounce; vampire drama: bright key, almost no fill [19:55-21:57]; noir: rim or kicker lights that do not need a practical source [21:57]. Intensity numbers are his scene's, not rules.

**Use it:** Frame the fill decision as a mood decision in the coach: ask what the shot should feel like, then set fill, then check rim light-linking so edges do not spill onto the ground.
