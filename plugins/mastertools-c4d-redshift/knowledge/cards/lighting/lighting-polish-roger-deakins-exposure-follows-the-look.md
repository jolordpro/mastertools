In a January 2026 forum thread, Deakins agrees with moderator David Mullen: exposure is a personal choice and only a generalisation. For No Country for Old Men he exposed a front-lit subject by more than a half stop, probably double that (he notes the posted frames may not show it), and let a backlit subject sit about 2 stops under, because they wanted a harsh, almost bleached landscape. On The Assassination of Jesse James he might set exposure closer to the meter reading. The answer depends on intense side light or distance from the source and on the overall feeling [§2]. Mullen's leaning: a face in bright frontal sun a half stop over or more, in backlight 1 to 1.5 stops under, sometimes 2; very pale actors can look overexposed even at normal exposure [§2].

**Use it:** Exposure advice is relative to the intended look; offer stop numbers only as starting points.
