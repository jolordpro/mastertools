Justin Phillip (cinematography YouTuber, low). Each stop doubles light; the ratio is key side versus fill side, and the two choosers are location and mood [00:03-00:35]. Ladder read with false color [00:50-07:48]: 1:1 (Ted Lasso, bright and family-friendly, flat on purpose); 2:1 (Midsommar, daylight with large bounces, makes horror more unsettling by staying real); 4:1 (Barbie, a little uplight under a hat to keep the key side two stops over); 8:1 (The Holdovers, three stops; hard to tell from 4:1 until you squint); 16:1 (Fargo season 5, golden-hour day exterior with a competing edge light); 32:1 (Oppenheimer, five stops; very hard to control outdoors, needs flags and a big crew); 64:1 (The Outsider, jail, no edge light, fill falls into darkness); 128:1 (Oppenheimer interrogation, seven stops, one small window, split face matches a conflicted character). Message: pick the ratio from the script and the setting, not copying a dark look because it is popular [08:20-09:55]. Note: the 4:1 example reads two stops over the fill on the face, which is a 4:1 by stop count.

**Use it:** Lighting-ratio target by shot mood for a C4D scene; read the render in a false-colour or luminance view and count stops key-side to fill-side. Examples are his reads from frames, not published specs.
