Reviewer Justin Phillip (a YouTuber analysing Fraser's frames, not Fraser) reads cave scenes lit by roof holes [02:04]. He says top-down light alone gives dark eye sockets, so there is always a second source: a small push from the ground that shows as a glint in the eyes and in sweat [14:33-15:35, 17:08].
It can be tiny and need no fixture: a 4 x 4 floppy or an ultra bounce, or a bounce board, shaped with a snoot or black wrap [15:35].
In bright top-down close-ups, the face is wrapped by roughly two main sources, one above and one from the side; one close-up has the shadow side three to four stops under while another is only half to one stop under [19:13]. The big overhead sources are large, shaped and modified, not small units [20:16].
He guesses lighting details; treat as an outside read, not Fraser's stated method. Low confidence.

**Use it:** Light-rig cards: for overhead keys in Redshift, add a low-level bounce card from below to lift eyes, and check the catchlight.
