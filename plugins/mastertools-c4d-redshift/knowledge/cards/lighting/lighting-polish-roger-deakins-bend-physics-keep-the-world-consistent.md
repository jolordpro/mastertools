Deakins's philosophy in the Rotten Tomatoes video summarised by No Film School: reality does not have to be naturalistic or exactly the way light is; you want a coherent world the audience believes, and the biggest challenge is a world that hangs together for the whole film [§1]. On The Assassination of Jesse James a 5K tungsten PAR for the train spotlight lit the hooded men from almost 90 degrees, which makes no sense in reality but works; the writer's summary is that if it looks good and nobody notices, you win [§1]. He also likes to join location scouting, set design and key style conversations so the location allows the camera moves he needs [§2].

Page spots (No Film School, Jan 2020): Deakins' stated philosophy that reality need not be naturalistic and the hardest part is a world that hangs together for the whole film [§1]; the 5K tungsten PAR on the Jesse James train lighting hooded men at almost 90 degrees [§1]; involvement in scouting, set design and style talks, and picking a camera to fit the job such as a light rig for 1917 [§2].

**Use it:** A cheated key light is acceptable if shadows and falloff still agree with the rest of the frame.
