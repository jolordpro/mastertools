Sudhakaran sorts subject and background each as under, grey (within about two stops of middle grey) or over: nine combinations [02:13-02:45]. Low key: background underexposed or black regardless of subject, used for sombre mood [02:45-03:17]. High key: subject correctly or over exposed against a bright background, used for comedy, sitcoms, cartoons [03:17-03:48]. Silhouette: background correctly exposed or bright, foreground dark [03:48]. Everything else is normal lighting. He notes photography and cinematography use the terms differently [02:45]. Look at the background, not the face, because all shots in a scene must match [04:54]. Mood also comes from wardrobe, expression and context; he gives The Shining (high key, dreadful) and Manhattan (low key, comedy) as counterexamples [05:56].

**Use it:** an automatic classifier for the lighting check: compare mean luminance of the background and subject in stops against middle grey; treat the result as a hint, not a rule.
