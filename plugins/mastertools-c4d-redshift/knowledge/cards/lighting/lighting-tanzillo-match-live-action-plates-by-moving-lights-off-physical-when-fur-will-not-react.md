An unnamed guest in Tanzillo's AMA lit digital doubles for Cats against on-set reference plates [39:33-41:07]. Light rigs were moved from day to day, so five shots in one sequence could each have a different light position, and the artist had to work out where each light had gone on the actor's face. The double had to match the actor one-for-one in skin colour, light shaping and shadow. Subsurface did not always respond as wanted, and with fur added a physically correct rig still did not look right, so in one or two shots lights were put in at an angle purely to catch the fur and hide the problem. The guest says to just get the note done and hope the director understands.
Transfers: when an element will not take light the way the plate shows, add a non-physical cheat light and check the result against the plate, not the rig diagram.

**Use it:** Advice for plate-matching comp or relight work: match the plate by eye first, keep the cheat light linked to the one problem object.
