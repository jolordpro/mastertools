Guest: Erik Messerschmidt (ASC), host Ben Consoli. For Mank's deep-focus look the plan was to shoot around f/8.5 to f/11, with some scenes at f/16 and a few at f/8 [21:35-22:13].
Closing a lens far down brings diffraction: astigmatism-like softness and lines that stop being straight. He tested large-format lenses on a hunch they would hold up better, and they did resolve more, but their physical iris was larger, so the same T-stop looked shallower. A T11 on the large lens showed less depth than an 11 on the Leica set. He went back to the Leicas he already knew from Mindhunter [19:07-21:42].
He also used a Harrison orange 2 filter. Diffraction is worse in blue light, so the filter reduces it, and it evens out skin blemishes and freckles [38:44-40:17].
Transfers: a render with a very small physical aperture or heavy DoF-off look can feel crisp and sterile; real deep-focus footage has its own softness. Test, do not assume.

**Use it:** When asked for a deep-focus film look, fake a slight diffraction softness and shape on skin tones rather than razor-sharp everywhere.
