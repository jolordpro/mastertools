Low Kelvin is orange, high Kelvin is blue; 3200K tungsten, 5600K daylight [14:41-16:36]. Examples [16:36-18:28]: camera and key at 3200K, practical lamp at 2000K and a light shaft at 4800K for contrast; a 5600K light read with the camera at 4800K feels cold (winter), at 6500K warm; 5600K camera with a 4200K light feels late afternoon; a 5600K light with the camera at 4200K reads blue moonlight; fluorescents matched at 5000K with a quarter plus-green gel and the camera at 3800K gives a cyan cast that the grade then enhances.
A gel is a filter that removes part of the spectrum, so light loses intensity [18:41-19:02].

**Use it:** when building a colour script in C4D/RS, treat the camera white point and the key Kelvin as two separate dials.
Transcript support: Volkov adds the saturation reason: the camera white point shifts to the dominant moonlight, so tungsten looks more saturated and the lamp should go more amber (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RCCaQmwxNB4 [27:27])
