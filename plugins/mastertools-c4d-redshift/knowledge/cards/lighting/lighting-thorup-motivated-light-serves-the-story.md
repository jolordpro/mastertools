Thorup frames motivated lighting as asking what the light has to do to tell the story [11:52]. In his three spot setups on a seated statue, a narrow key plus a back light that only outlines the shape reads as dark thoughts [13:57], a box of light makes the figure look trapped [14:29], and a sliver on the hands alone points all attention at the hands [15:31].
Rules he repeats: let the shadow side stay dark and do not fill it, keep the silhouette readable so the background is not pure black [16:03], and "don't be afraid of shadows" [12:24] [19:47].
He contrasts this with spilling light on everything, which flattens the scene [19:16].
Good to know: compare the existing card lighting-messerschmidt-motivated-light-no-rules, which makes the same point for film.

**Use it:** In a motion-critique or elevate pass, name the emotion in one word, then check each light supports it. Anything that fills the dark side without a story reason is a cut candidate.
Transcript support: Tran duplicates the key for a Rembrandt look while she smiles, then flags it off when she turns, so the light follows her inner state (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ETgt9YXSaeg [12:41])
