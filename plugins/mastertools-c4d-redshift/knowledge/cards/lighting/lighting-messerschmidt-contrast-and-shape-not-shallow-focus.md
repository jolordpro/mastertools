In black and white a very shallow depth of field turns backgrounds mushy and you cannot use contrast and shape the same way [Part II §3]. The Hearst Castle interiors were f8.0 or higher and powerful characters at low angles [Part II §1]. Lenses were chosen for f8 and f11, with some shots at f16, because modern lenses lose resolution past f5.6-f8 from diffraction [Part I §3]. A CineFade collapsed depth of field without a visible exposure change, and a swing/tilt lens put an eye in focus [Part II §3]. [§1,§3 Part II; §3 Part I]

**Use it:** Separate subject from background by tone and edge light first, blur second.
