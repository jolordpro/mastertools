Young says a 50 mm lens close on a face flattens and portrays humanely, whereas an 18 to 20 mm lens within six inches can reinforce old anthropological 'inferiority' imagery; the fault is the mentality behind the eyes, not the optics [07:48-08:48].
He loves wide lenses but is now much more conscious of why he uses one: on a recent film they chose a 28 over a 20 for the faces [08:18-08:48]. He says a very wide lens can still work for a particular story (he cites 18 mm in another film) and sees it as a choice, not a rule [09:18].
The interview captions are garbled in places, so details are approximate.

**Use it:** Decide lens by how it treats the subject's face; test wide versus normal on a close-up and choose on purpose.
