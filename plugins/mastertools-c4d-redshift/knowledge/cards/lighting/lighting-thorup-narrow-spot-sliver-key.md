Thorup's dramatic key is a narrow-beam light: make a light, set spread to 0.1 and intensity to 10, then aim, scale and keep tightening spread [17:38] [18:43]. For the Patagonia jacket he went to roughly 0.025 spread and tuned intensity after [22:56]. He likes the light shaped rectangular and a bit wide, not round [18:10].
He says this setup is in about 50 percent of his projects because of the shadows it gives [16:36]. A narrow beam reads like window light or a door opening [13:25] [19:16].
The key can be animated to travel up a body and reveal a face, or to wobble slightly [16:03].
Intensity numbers are scene-specific; the habit is spread first, then intensity.

**Use it:** A coach tip for a flat render: try the key as a narrow sliver with spread near 0.1 or below, then rebuild fill around it. Pair it with the shadow-keeping rule in lighting-thorup-start-with-the-spot-then-fill.
