Joris Hermans (video creator): flicker comes from the combination of frame rate and shutter, not from NTSC/PAL mode [02:13-02:49]. In a 50 Hz country, shooting 30 fps at 1/60 flickered, 1/50 stopped it; this breaks the 180-degree rule slightly, needing a bit more light and a slightly different blur [02:13]. Rule he gives: NTSC rates in a PAL (50 Hz) country, use 1/50 or 1/100 shutter; PAL rates in an NTSC (60 Hz) country, use 1/60 or 1/120 [02:49]. 120 fps in a 50 Hz country has no safe flicker-free combination except good LED lights, which do not flicker [02:49-03:24]. He links a calculator tool for safe combinations [03:58].
Existing card on a 172.8 degree shutter covers the related fix.

**Use it:** Pre-shoot note for any plate or LED-wall capture: ask the local mains frequency before choosing fps and shutter.
