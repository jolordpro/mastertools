Arthur Whitehead [00:00-06:46]. Only very soft lights, which makes the watch look shiny and delicate; many gradients (black-white-black across faces) give contrast instead of flatness [00:00-01:02]. Method: start from black, list the features (two straps, middle section, pins, top face, rim, sides), and give each its own light, then switch lights off to see which areas are still dark [01:36-04:27]. Lights go symmetric where possible (top rim, bottom rim) [02:40]. Straps and edges get gradient lights, light to dark, from one edge to the other [04:27-05:00]. Sides and crown get their own lights last [05:00-05:38]. He builds the soft lights in HDR Light Studio rather than Octane area lights because he finds the light quality better, and says the choice of tool is not required [03:10]. Hard-edge lights, or a mid-grey background, make the render look flat and washed out [06:11].

**Use it:** Same feature-list method as the car card; use it as the elevate checklist for glossy products. The HDR Light Studio preference is one artist's view.
