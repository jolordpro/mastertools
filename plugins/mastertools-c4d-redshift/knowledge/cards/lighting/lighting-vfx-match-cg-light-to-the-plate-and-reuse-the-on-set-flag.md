Thomas (VFX supervisor, Game of Thrones) says CG lights need to be very close to identical to the lights during photography, and that a mismatch always reads fake [48:51]. Example: a bright light from far right on the actor on the dragon's back came and went because a crew member held up a card to flag it; the CG dragon wing was then animated to block that sun, so the actor and the CG share one lighting story [49:52]. Distant dragons were also hazed by the compositor, and trees, snow and dragons were rendered as separate passes then assembled in comp [50:23].

**Use it:** When matching CG to footage, read the plate's light changes (flags, shifts) and give the CG a matching cause. On broadcast CG over live footage, note the hard key direction first.
