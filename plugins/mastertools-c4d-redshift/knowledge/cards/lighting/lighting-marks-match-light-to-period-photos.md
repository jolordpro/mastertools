Raoul Marks says he relied heavily on source photography from the Apollo era to reproduce the quality of light and grading of that period; the opener itself was inspired by a 1968 LIFE magazine series of an astronaut falling through darkness [§1]. As the sun shifted over cratered, sub-polygon displaced surfaces, he wanted realistic GI light to bounce off the small details to make a believable lunar surface [§2].

**Use it:** Pick a real photographic reference for the light before tweaking, then let fine detail catch the bounce.
