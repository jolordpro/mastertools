Real stocks give more saturation at lower speed (about 50 to 125) and exposing down gives a denser negative with richer colour, in the subtractive sense [57:32-58:04]. In his test the AGFA 250 profile gave subtle warm highlights and split-tone shadows with a green-cyan cast, for jobs where the look should not be reinvented; the 125 was too much [58:37-1:00:42]. AGFA 125 and 320 were the stocks Eastern European cinematographers learned on, and that taught them very precise exposure [36:25-37:57].

**Use it:** Offer a subtle and a strong version of the same look, and choose by how much the job should change.
