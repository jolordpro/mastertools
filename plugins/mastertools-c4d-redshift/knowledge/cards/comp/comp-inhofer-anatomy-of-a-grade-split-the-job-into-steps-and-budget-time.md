He teaches an "anatomy of a color grading job" template, scaled by assigning hours or minutes to each step so every shot is looked at and the deadline holds [28:22-28:54]. Beginners must learn time management, not just software: what to hit first (shot matching or look creation), and when to send the first review for revisions, given the days available [28:54-29:24]. A good colorist can quote hours or days and hit that number; otherwise four days become nine and the craft turns into a black hole [27:22-27:52]. Related idea from the same stretch, spoken by a co-host and agreed by Inhofer (captions have no speaker labels): the core skill is a unified visual timeline that looks like one set of eyes, even with phone, Red and Venice media mixed, and quickly [25:49-26:51].

**Use it:** Estimator for post: set step budgets (conform, match, look, review, revise, deliver) before starting. Schedule the first client review early enough to leave a revision cycle.
