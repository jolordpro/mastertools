He points to Roy DeCarava's photograph of Mahalia Jackson, where the blur on the singer's teeth came from a long exposure and shows movement in time [12:37-13:09]. He also notes DeCarava's rule of staying out of the way and using the tools he had. A second example, a Ming Smith photo, shows a shutter speed choice giving motion; Young stresses that this was rigour and intention, not luck [22:30-23:02].

**Use it:** Treat motion blur and trailing as a deliberate shutter-time choice (angle/length), not a default to remove.
