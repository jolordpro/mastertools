Commentary channel (Ranjith A) on CorridorKey 2.0 [1:35-3:43]: the tool uses neural matting with temporal awareness, but its demo compared a first-pass key to traditional keyers (Keylight, IBK, Primatte) and used plates not denoised first. He says keying is about 70 percent keying and 30 percent rotoscoping, and real comps separate core, fine hair, edge and spill breakups; that is the workflow, not a weakness. Real green screens are uneven, wrinkled and spilled. A VFX supervisor in the comparison called it excellent for a "garbage matte" style starting point (the captions say "for Cormat"). Conclusion: usable key fast, but do not confuse a first pass with a finished composite. Opinion of a commentator; no measured test shown.

**Use it:** The coach should ask for the plate prep (denoise, uneven green) before comparing keyers, and plan separate passes for core, hair and edge.
