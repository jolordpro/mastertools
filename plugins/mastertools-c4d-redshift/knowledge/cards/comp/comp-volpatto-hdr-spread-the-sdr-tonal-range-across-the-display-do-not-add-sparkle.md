The eye works by ratios, not absolute nits: he spot-metered about 7,000 nits on his daughter's face in sun and she looked normal [37:15-38:07].
A shot made with a seven-or-eight-stop ratio looks fine on a 100 nit set and on a 400 nit set, because the ratios stay; the new TV should not use the extra nits only for sparkle [38:07-39:43].
His method is to take the full tonal range and spread it between the display's minimum and maximum, then recover some highlights for a little sparkle [39:43-40:15]. On Sweet Tooth he put an eight-stop representation between zero and an arbitrary 650 nits [43:22-43:54].
He puts real contrast at 12 stops, about 4096 to 1 or roughly 5000 to 1, and says a TV doing 600 nits is already enough for a picture of that range [34:28-36:03].
He disagrees with colorists who leave the tone as it was and open the top [37:15-38:07].

**Use it:** For an HDR deliverable keep the SDR picture's ratios and map the whole range across the display rather than stacking extra bright highlights.
