Walter Volpatto (feature colorist, Picture Shop; captions spell him "Valpado") starts a scene on the shot where the lead delivers the key line and trusts that the cinematographer lit it on purpose [53:00-54:34].
His first tool is an offset (printer lights) in log, which acts like opening or closing the lens or the lights by stops; he prefers it to piling on plugins, and notes it works less well in some log curves where middle grey sits very low [54:34-55:38].
He grades for the subject and lets the background go, unless handheld or multicam work forces ambient first [56:09-57:14].
He does not stay on a shot more than a few seconds on the first pass, then plays the scene as the audience would: too long on shot one means a film with hundreds of scenes never finishes [57:14-57:45, 1:06:32-1:07:35].
A shot-level reflection or bright spot is worth fixing only after the scene is in the right ballpark [57:14-58:17].

**Use it:** Pass order for a graded package: hero frame, scene balance with offsets, play through, then details.
