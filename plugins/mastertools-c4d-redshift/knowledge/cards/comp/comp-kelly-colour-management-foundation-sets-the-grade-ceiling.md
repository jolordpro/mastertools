Kelly [38:13-43:40] says all colour management does is turn what the camera saw into what the display can show. Aces, Resolve colour management, Baselight and T-Cam all claim to be objective yet give different images from the same footage, because their designers chose different ways to pack the camera's range into the display.
You cannot just match one system into another by grading: you rarely have the reference, and a more contrasty rendering fought shot by shot ends as higher contrast or an inconsistent cut. His line: the foundation defines the heights you reach in the grade.
For cameras he wants the same push (e.g. five stops) to feel the same on an Alexa, Red or iPhone, up to what each captured, so he grades scenes, not cameras or displays. [44:43-46:48]

**Use it:** pick the render and delivery colour pipeline first and test the default rendering before grading; do not plan to fix a poor default view transform in the grade.
