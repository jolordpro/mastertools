Guest: Walter Volpatto (feature colorist, Picture Shop). Film was imprecise: the same negative with the same printer lights, printed Monday morning and Thursday afternoon, gives different prints because the chemistry changes during the week, and two prints in two theatres differ. A printer point of green was a whole bucket, not a fine step [00:00-02:04].
In digital the budget decides: he says one cinematographer will spend 600 hours on colour while an independent film gets 4 days, so there is only so much matching possible [02:04-03:05].
Proof that rough is fine: the No Time to Die opening chase mixes shots in rain, cloud and sun, and the Dunkirk dogfight cuts between sky conditions. If the cut shots are close enough that you believe one place and time, and you follow the hero subject, audiences forgive a lot. "If you are not perfect, at least make your subject perfect" [03:05-04:39].

**Use it:** Match check: judge cut flow first, then the hero subject; chase small drifts in the background only if budget remains.
