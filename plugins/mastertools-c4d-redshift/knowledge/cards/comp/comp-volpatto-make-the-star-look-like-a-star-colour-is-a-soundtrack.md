Guest: Walter Volpatto. His mentor Dan Muscarella, a lab colour timer, gave two rules: find the original photography and what the DP meant before touching it, since days were spent lighting it; and make your star look like a star, with the subject exposed so it supports the story [04:07-05:11].
Colour should be like sound: present, supporting, not the story. He sees inexperienced colourists who find the saturation control and turn it to 11 [05:11-05:42].
Examples: a funeral should not be warm and happy unless the story is a release; a beach wedding photo (white dress, black tux, blue water) graded as post-apocalyptic teal and orange "does not serve the moment". Show-off looks need justification from the story [05:42-07:47].
Merged from `comp-volpatto-find-the-original-photography-then-make-the-star-look-like-a-star`: His first mentor, color-lab timer Dan Mascarella (Titanic), gave him two rules [17:45-18:17]. First: the DP, gaffer, set designer and others spend a long time crafting the image, so the first duty is to find it and bring it to the screen, then decide with them what to change [18:17-18:48]. Second: stars want to look like stars, so if the light is on her it should stay on her; do not make her too shiny [18:48]. He applies this from big ASC DPs to AFI students, and treats respect for the cinematographer's craft as the first thing a colourist must learn [18:48-19:19].

**Use it:** Review rule: ask what a grade says about the story; if the answer is "that I can do teal and orange", send it back.
