A generic Gen 1 print is the mathematically expected result and technically clean; real lab profiles (Japan, New York, Hollywood, London) show strange behaviours, and the same stock printed by different labs differs [28:45-30:47]. When a client says give me film emulation, he reads it as the look of film, not an accurate version [29:15-29:46]. MPF is Kodak-style target values used to check manufactured film, a very clean reference [30:47-31:49]. Gen 2 prints give very rich colours and a wide gamut [31:49-32:50]. FPE limits the result to what film could physically show; his input is far wider than a negative could capture, so output can be pushed beyond film unless FPE is on [32:50-33:53]. Bumpy highlight lines are typical of real film print emulation; generic prints clean them [42:39-43:10].

**Use it:** Offer two versions of a film look: a clean generic one and an authentic-profile one, and choose by what the picture does.
