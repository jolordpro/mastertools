Doyle [52:40-1:03:11]: MTF (the frequency response of the image) is as important as tone and colour. Anything done to the picture has to be lived with for about two hours, so a very soft glow or a super-sharp hard look must still serve the story [54:46-55:49].
Blur the picture so skin looks soft, then sharpen only the very highest frequencies, which brings back the eyelight and the "wetness" of the eyes, where actors do their work [59:30-1:00:33]. Highlights can stay sharp [1:01:05]. Too sharp makes pores hard and fine clothing patterns moire [1:00:33].
It works like equalising in music mixing, and DPs always varied diffusion between close-ups and wides [1:01:37].
Lens examples: Panavision C-series anamorphic at 28 mm is not sharp but beautiful; Zeiss Master Primes at T5.6 are very sharp [56:20-56:52].

**Use it:** Grading or comp advice should treat sharpen and soften as frequency choices per area (eyes, skin, highlights), not one global sharpen.
