For Joel Coen's Macbeth, Doyle explains the problem [21:40-23:50]: the digital cinema chain is colour; achromatic projection exists only in medical monitors. The upside is that you are not held to the stock's white point: you choose warm or cool and can even vary it through the film.
His team ran the picture as a truly desaturated colour image and subtly moved the white from warm to cool across the whole film, which no one had thought of before [26:28].
He framed it in the filmmakers' language (they collect photographic prints): creamy Japanese silver paper versus platinum-palladium [24:53]. Options to try include the old filter choice (green, red or orange) and tint, as 1920s-30s films were tinted [30:35, 31:36]; Newton used a green filter for stark skin contrast, Avedon platinum-palladium warmth [31:05].

**Use it:** The coach asks which white point a mono or low-saturation grade targets and shows warm and cool versions before locking it.
