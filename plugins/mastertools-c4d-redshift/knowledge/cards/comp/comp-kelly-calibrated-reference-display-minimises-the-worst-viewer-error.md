Kelly's dartboard picture [1:27:25-1:30:30]: an uncalibrated display lands somewhere off-centre, and some viewer's screen will be off in the opposite direction, so the gap can be the whole board. If the master display is dead centre, the worst viewer gap is about half the board. He says the maths is not literally one to one but the point holds: control the one variable you can, so the maximum error any viewer sees is minimised.
He rejects the "car stereo test" (grading on a deliberately poor screen): bad screens miss in every direction, so biasing toward one of them hurts the next. Everyone who signs off should review on an accurate image. [1:31:31-1:33:35]
He also argues viewers should extend trust to the author when a show is dark; the grade belongs to its authors. [1:36:42]

**Use it:** the review rule for any deliverable: sign off on a measured reference display, not on a phone or a flattering monitor, and treat client-device checks as a separate sanity pass.
