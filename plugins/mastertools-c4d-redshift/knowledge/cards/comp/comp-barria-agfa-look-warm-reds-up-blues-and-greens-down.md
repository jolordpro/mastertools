Barria's thesis for the Color Training master's programme (interviewer unnamed) used the tools at hand, DaVinci Resolve scopes on remasters and stills, accepting that it was an approximation and not scientific [10:39-15:22]. Ozu's 1958-62 colour films, Fassbinder's 1970s films and 1980s-90s AGFA titles all showed more saturation in warm and red hues and less in blues and greens, consistent over decades [40:02-50:05]. Ozu said another stock made skies too blue, and he liked how AGFA rendered reds [12:16-13:19, 41:37-42:41]. Scholars describe graying greens, deep reds and warm blues; an American cinematographer (captions say David Muan, probably Mullen) is cited for soft, low-contrast, warm skin, shadows pulled in [40:02-41:05].
Caveat: remastered stills may be faded or regraded, and many AGFA films were printed on Kodak [16:26, 49:00].

**Use it:** Use it as a named look direction (warm-red-led, cool-restrained) and test a render against a scope read of the reference, not by taste alone.
