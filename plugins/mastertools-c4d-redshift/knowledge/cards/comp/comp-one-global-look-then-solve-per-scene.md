Walter Volpatto (feature colorist, Picture Shop; captions spell him "Valpado"): if every one of 2,000 shots is graded alone with copied settings, averaging all the work would give one global look anyway, so start from that look [58:48-1:00:22].
The camera sensor responds alike to like light, and the look is made after the camera, so one look on top fits the director of photography's consistent tonal range [1:00:22-1:01:56].
Then fix at scene level (warm the whole scene) and, if two angles differ, solve the whole of camera B in one move instead of nudging shots one at a time [1:02:27-1:03:28].
He tells new colorists to learn matching before breaking rules, and says a shot-by-shot approach is the real source of inconsistency [1:06:32-1:07:35].

**Use it:** Coach tip for multi-shot packages: set one look for the whole package first, then scene or camera fixes, then only the odd shot.
