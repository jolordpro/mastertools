Doyle (colorist: Lord of the Rings, Harry Potter, Inside Llewyn Davis) says his role is to provide the technology and workflow so the production designer, DP and director can create their world, and he answers to that "trilogy" [04:18, 11:43].
A good note is a feeling, not a button: the Coens say a beat should feel more angst, dark and heavy, which gives him room to interpret with his tools; he does not want to be told which buttons to push, and filmmakers do not want to learn his [28:30-30:03].
Respond by offering a few considerations to try, then narrow them with references, which becomes a common slang for the 200 hours of grading [30:35, 25:57]. Aim to find the common language, not to change your own eye [13:49]. Start from what the hope is, not a reference to copy; interpret rather than make an homage [12:15].
Rule from late DP Andrew Lesnie: it is always about presentation [24:22].

**Use it:** In review, the coach asks for the note as a feeling and replies with two or three options, not a list of settings.
