For Flamin' Hot's factory, Volpatto drew on his first job on an assembly line, which felt almost lifeless: he made almost everything in that location a neutral grey except the workers' skin tones. It could not look glamorous, but they weren't making a horror film either [§2]. He and cinematographer Federico Cantini built three show LUTs early: a nostalgic 1960s childhood with faded golden hues, a grainy, contrasty, dramatic 1970s close to police shows, and a warm 1980s but not to the extent of childhood [§1]. Montañez's home was graded warmer and cozier as a safe space [§2].

**Use it:** Use colour to separate worlds; let one controlled accent (skin) carry the frame.
