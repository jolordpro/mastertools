Kramer [12:28-15:01]: on Super 8 he composited train destruction into a 3-second shot with 3D assets, stock destruction and a green-screen key, thought it was movie quality, then saw ILM's version: helicopters flying, dust blowing, emergency workers, the scene alive.
He could copy the idea only after seeing it; knowing what sells a shot at that scale is the difference between ILM and his first movie composite [14:31].
His name for it is "the 10 percent": small final changes (rolling dust, the helicopter, the right exposure or curves) that look unnecessary, but a curve 10 percent wrong can ruin a shot [14:31-15:01].
Also: recipes look simple when revealed, yet he spent a week finding the cloud element at the right scale for an AC-130 shot [11:57-12:28].

**Use it:** When a CG shot reads flat, the coach looks for secondary life (dust, atmosphere, small activity) and scale cues before re-lighting the hero.
