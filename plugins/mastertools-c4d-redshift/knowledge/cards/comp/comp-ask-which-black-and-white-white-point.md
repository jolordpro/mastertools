Walter Volpatto (feature colorist, Picture Shop; captions spell him "Valpado"): video white is D65, but film white (print stock plus a projector lamp) lands near D55 at best and likely D50, so it looks yellowish [30:39-31:40].
He measured a film black-and-white print with a spectrometer, matched it in a digital projector, and the client still disliked the yellow; the cure was to print on colour stock so the white point could move [35:18-36:22].
On Oppenheimer he explains that mixed black-and-white and colour sections forced colour print stock, so split-toned casts compound with each optical pass [32:42-35:18].

**Use it:** Brief check: ask white-point intent (D65 neutral vs warmer film) before any mono grade or monochrome graphic treatment.
