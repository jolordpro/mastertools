Walter Volpatto (feature colorist, Picture Shop; captions spell him "Valpado") says a grade that mixes the film look, the scene look, the balance of one shot and its detail work into one pile cannot be edited afterwards [58:48-1:03:58].
His node structure keeps a block for each: difficult keys and masks cached first, shot balance next, a scene pass, and (mainly for commercials) a last look block that can be bypassed for one shot, such as a brand-red logo [1:04:28-1:05:31].
With balance already done, a whole scene can be pushed brighter, darker or warmer in one place, and a client can iterate live without breaking anything [1:03:58-1:05:01].
He frames this as strategy before tactics: the plan holds in any grading tool, only the method changes [1:07:35-1:08:37]. Transfers to comp: grade a render in separate, named, re-orderable stages (look / sequence / shot / detail) instead of one stack.

**Use it:** A comp or grade check can ask whether look, sequence and shot corrections sit in separate nodes or groups; flag a single merged correction as hard to revise.
