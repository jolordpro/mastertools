His complaint about plug-in film profiles: projects end up looking alike, and he wants every project to have its own identity and the freedom to modify profiles [21:10-21:42]. Colorists who keep a signature look do so on purpose, but he does not [21:42].
His explanation of why subtractive works: digital sensor photosites capture one colour, while film light passes yellow, magenta and cyan layers (with halation bounce), so blending between layers is where the film look arises [22:14-22:50].
Practical use: push cyan a little when the look runs too warm, then push/pull density to recover [22:50-23:59].
This is a vendor-side claim about his own tool; the "nobody can match it with lift/gamma/gain" statement is sales talk [24:00].

**Use it:** Treat film emulation as adjustable layers, and compare any preset with a version where you changed one layer deliberately.
