Examples [51:35-55:00]: Karen X. Cheng's (captioned Xchang) stop-motion loop used AI only to extend a roof and to offer 20 balloon/umbrella options to pick from; a painted rough in Krea plus a short prompt gives a matching grungy paper asset in real time, then an upscale step makes a roughly 10k asset in about 2 minutes.
Ink-in-water mats: he gave Sora a black frame plus a white-ink frame made in Midjourney and got a 3-second clip; usable only at one moment, but crushed into a luma matte in After Effects it could work [52:36-53:38]. Wait time and the slot-machine feel are the cost.
Rule he repeats: a human has to know what is wanted and choose, and without taste it is worthless [54:39].

**Use it:** when an element is needed to animate (texture, mat, prop), generating and picking can beat shooting or buying; check the client's AI policy first.
