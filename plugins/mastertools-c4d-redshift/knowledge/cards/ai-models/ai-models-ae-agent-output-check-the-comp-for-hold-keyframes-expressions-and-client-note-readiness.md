Test 2, a 5-6 second stop-motion bumper (crumpled paper reveals logo, with generated assets, sound and music), took clarifying questions first (length, background surface, SFX) because he ends prompts with "ask questions before starting" to protect usage [05:08-06:41]. Result impressive and close, but he marked problems: paper texture artifacts, logo shrinking between frames, a drop shadow that rotates with the paper (not photo real), and a thin background [07:12-08:50]. Under the hood: hold keyframes on opacity, expressions and keyframes to hold each photo three frames, which he would have done with posterize time or a lower comp frame rate [08:50-09:52]. His warning: if the client has notes you must dig through whatever the AI built [09:52]. Test 3 (title from four reference frames, 24 fps, 5 s, native layers, halftone and handmade texture): passable but not what he imagined, 10 minutes and about 3 percent of his ChatGPT usage when matching reference screenshots, result a poor composition [10:23-14:33].

**Use it:** After any AI-built comp, open it and read the layer names, hold keys and expressions before sending to anyone; tell the model the frame rate, length and reference frames up front.
