Kramer [17:37-19:11]: the fastest way to improve is to run into challenges nobody has solved for you. Take a camera, film something no one has, add 3D to your own idea, and then deal with the problems you made: wait for people to walk by vs rotoscoping them, reflections that water should show, and so on.
That forces you to learn roto and the rest, and it is your perspective, not a tutorial everyone has seen [18:39].
On learning from tutorials: seeing the trick is only the first step, like magic where hours of card-shuffling make the magician [09:54-10:56]. A simple-looking breakdown hides the failed attempts [12:28].
Teaching: he kept tutorials casual and project-driven after finding viewers preferred a natural voice [04:11-05:12].

**Use it:** The coach sets a practice drill: shoot or build one original shot, then log the problems and how each was solved.
