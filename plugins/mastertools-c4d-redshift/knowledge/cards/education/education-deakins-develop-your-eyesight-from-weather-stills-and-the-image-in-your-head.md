To light you must understand how light makes you feel; technique matters less than the image in your head [00:08-00:38]. He learned from fishing as a boy, noticing some days feel ominous, some sparkling, from the light itself [01:16-01:52]. Look at paintings and at stills photographers (he cites William Eggleston's colour and melancholy) to see possibilities and develop your own way of seeing, not to copy [01:52-02:58]. Best stills strip away what is on the periphery and focus on one image [06:09-06:39].

**Use it:** Study the mood of a light before naming the rig, and collect stills for feeling rather than for recipes.
