The Motionographer writer uses Scott McCloud's building blocks from Understanding Comics, in order: Idea, Concept, Structure, Craft, Style; McCloud likens it to an apple, idea the core, style the skin [§5]. Concept turns ideas into storytelling units such as visual metaphors; structure shapes the argument, drops what is unnecessary and orders the information; craft builds it; style is the final polish [§5]. The writer says artists tend to learn these in reverse, starting at style, and traces Clair moving from style (MTV, Hungry Beast) to craft (Stuxnet), structure, concept and then idea (True Detective) [§5-6].
In Clair's current process, he develops the fundamental ideas with showrunners, visual researchers refine them into concepts from thousands of images, editors weave storyboards and playblasts into structure, and artists elevate craft and style [§7].

**Use it:** In review, check the idea and structure first. Do not give polish notes on a shot whose place in the sequence is unsettled.
