Robert Legato says the favourite is rarely the million-dollar, year-long shot; often a simple insert that moves you unexpectedly (the Apollo 13 shot of one model falling away over a real photo of Earth) [00:32-01:35]. Films do not live on one shot; they must cut, with a rhythm like music, so a good effect improves by fitting a sequence [01:35-02:06]. Study other art (music changes how you compose a shot), and learn editing, directing and even acting, since a performer's point of view tells you how to photograph [02:06-03:40]. Do not imitate him or others; your own view is what is wanted [02:36-03:07].
**Use it:** For critique, ask first how a shot works inside the sequence rhythm before polishing it alone.
