Ben Marriott: taste is the hardest thing to teach; it grows from huge numbers of small judgments (nudge an anchor point, spot a poster mistake) [30:07-32:43]. Be intentional so you can answer a client's "why", e.g. corners rounded to feel softer; "I just did it" is the worst answer [24:26-25:29]. Compounding choices give a cohesive result even if direction is wrong [24:26]. Use mood words on the second monitor as a test between options [26:30-27:32]. Make one clear statement confidently rather than scatter [30:07]. Accuracy also helps: make choices consciously, even for abstract science, so expert viewers are not annoyed [22:20-24:26].

**Use it:** coach prompts: "what feeling does this choice serve?"; log reasons next to design variants.
