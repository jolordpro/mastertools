Mike Tanzillo (feature-animation lighter and Academy of Animated Art host; June 2020 AMA) says there is no magic number of pieces [27:33-29:08]. He stays under 2 minutes, about a minute and a half is his sweet spot, 3 minutes the absolute ceiling [28:35]. He would rather see 3 very good projects than 3 good plus 2 mediocre: a weak piece makes viewers doubt the strong ones [29:08]. He watches every reel to the end, but the first thing on screen sways him most [28:03].
What a strong set shows [29:38-30:41]: character lighting (the one thing animation studios look for); the same scene lit three ways to pull different emotions from the same geometry; matching a supplied reference (art-department paintings or other films); a big environment that still feels vast and the right scale. Fancy edits and title cards distract [31:12]. Software does not matter, because studios teach their own proprietary setups; the art cannot be taught [31:12-32:14].
Transfers to broadcast motion: a reel of three tight pieces with one scene re-lit to different moods answers the same question a sports-package client asks.

**Use it:** Coach a reel review as: count the weak pieces first, then check for a re-lit-same-scene piece, a reference match and a scale test.
