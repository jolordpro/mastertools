Two StudioBinder staffers (a motion artist and an editor, casual, low confidence). Points: budget does not decide quality; titles can carry tone and story beats [00:34]. Catch Me If You Can used cut-out 2D animation over digital footage in a live-action film, and the choice is still remembered 20 years later [01:10-02:42]. The Room's titles are static shots of the set with no story; they say inserts and b-roll (photos, the football, flowers) would have told the story, and the sequence would work with any song and title swapped in, a sign it is generic [03:19-04:23]. Dawn of the Dead pairs found footage with a calm country song; subverting expected music gives personality (Tarantino, Scorsese cited) [04:23-06:19]. Glitchy MTV-style quick cuts read as early-2000s [06:52]. True Detective: double exposure from keyed green-screen cutouts with the gradient left in for depth; hidden elements pay off later in the season; by 2020 the look was already a trend that burnt out [07:26-08:49].

**Use it:** Critique prompts for an open: would this still work if the song and title were swapped? what does the style contrast with? is the look a trend that will date?
