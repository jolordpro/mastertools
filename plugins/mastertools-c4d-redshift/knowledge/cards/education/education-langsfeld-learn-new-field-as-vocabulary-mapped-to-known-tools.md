Ben Langsfeld (group creative director, BUCK New York): diving into new territory is learning the language of that space, not learning to think or create from scratch; once you know the terminology you feel in control of communicating [36:31] [37:03]. His example: modular synthesis felt familiar because of node trees, ins and outs, and layered textures from compositing and 3D lighting [38:06].
For real-time engines, friends who spoke his language mapped terms onto things he knew: this is like a precomp in After Effects, that is like a displacement map; project the familiar technology onto the unfamiliar until it attaches to your mental map [39:08] [39:38]. He also begins with YouTube and notes the studio shares knowledge freely [37:35] [39:08].

**Use it:** Teaching pattern for the coach: answer a new-tool question with a mapping to the user's known tool (e.g. C4D concept -> After Effects concept) before giving menu paths.
