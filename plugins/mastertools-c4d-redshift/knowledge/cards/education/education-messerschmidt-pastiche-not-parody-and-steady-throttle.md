Guest: Erik Messerschmidt. His worry about black and white was becoming a parody of itself. He leaned to pastiche: borrow from Citizen Kane but make the film for its own locations, which are bright, not cavernous [11:55-13:28].
He approaches each shot by what the scene needs and how it fits the film's puzzle, keeping a steady hand on the throttle, because stylisation is seductive and pulls viewers out if it has no reason [13:28-14:29].
He swung between "too far" and "not far enough"; a third of the way through he asked Fincher if it was enough, and was told to carry on, it suits the story [14:29-15:30].
Different scenes draw on different genres (noir for a Venetian-blinds office, soft modern light for the bungalow) as the scene needs [31:30-33:35].

**Use it:** Review question for a stylised piece: is each stylistic move motivated by this scene, or just fun? Ask a trusted second eye mid-way.
