Tanzillo and an unnamed guest (a junior-turned-lighter on The Lego Movie and Cats) describe studio habits [47:54-53:07]. A senior told the junior who stayed late polishing: if the note is hit, go home; an art director can flip the direction tomorrow and the extra three hours are gone [51:02-52:04]. Do not hold a shot back from dailies because it is not perfect; submit it, say so if it did not work, then reset [50:31].
Ask many questions, go away, do what was said and come back; people remember that [50:31]. Treat the job as a trade: you represent your work ethic. Tanzillo would pick the easier person over a slightly more talented one every time, as long as shots turn around quickly [52:35]. Save the energy for crunch; a lead refused a volunteer Saturday read of the docs, saying so [49:29-50:01]. Burnout from 20-hour days early in a project is common [52:04].
Juniors earn harder shots by first matching existing shots, then get fed more [43:12].
