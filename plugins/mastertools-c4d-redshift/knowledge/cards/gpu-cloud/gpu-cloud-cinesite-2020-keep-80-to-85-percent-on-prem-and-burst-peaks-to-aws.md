Graham Petty (COO, Cinesite Montreal) at an AWS Deadline event, Sept 2020. Case 1, Jack Ryan: 116 shots, seven episodes, about 2.5 months; the issue was farm capacity, not skill [12:56]. The farm gives 136,000 render hours per week, split between VFX and animation [13:59]. Order of escalation: borrow from the other department, then sister studios (London, Vancouver share the pipeline), then AWS [14:31].
Why not own it all [15:02-17:07]: on-prem covers roughly 80 to 85% of need, maybe 70 to 75% later; pay only when used, revenue from the extra work offsets it; hardware takes six to eight weeks to buy; a smaller farm saves rack space, rent and power and idles less (blades idle from Saturday to Monday); opex rather than capex; access to current blades. Outcome: a rush job accepted that would not otherwise fit [17:39].

**Use it:** a template for a studio's burst policy: order of who to borrow from, and a target on-prem share.
