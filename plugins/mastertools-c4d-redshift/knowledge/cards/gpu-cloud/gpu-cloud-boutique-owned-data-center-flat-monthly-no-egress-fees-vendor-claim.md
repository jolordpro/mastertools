Ryan Kelsey (VP at Center Grid, a sponsor of the CG Garage podcast, so this is the vendor's own pitch) says the company owns its facility and GPUs rather than reselling a hyperscaler's, so clients get dedicated, unshared hardware [11:53-12:56]. His pricing claims: no egress (data transfer) fees, upload and download as much as you like [29:24-29:55]; you pay only for the rental time, and storage comes in fixed buckets bundled with each workstation, with shared storage added to order [29:55-30:26]. Rental is most often per month, sometimes per week; hours used do not matter, and shifts can share one workstation [31:55-32:30]. The pitch is a sandbox where a bill cannot run away: he contrasts it with surprise invoices 30 days later on public cloud, and the host adds he knew firms that went bankrupt that way [12:56-13:26]. No dollar prices were given. Kelsey also claims AWS raised prices about 15% this year and Microsoft about 6 or 7%, while Center Grid has had no major hikes [28:23-28:53]; he said "I think", so treat the figures as unverified. Date: recorded around the HPA Tech Retreat, episode posted 2026-03-09.

**Use it:** price a boutique or owned-hardware quote as monthly, flat and egress-free; when comparing with a hyperscaler, add egress and the risk of an open-ended bill to the hyperscaler side; ask a boutique vendor for the dollar rate card, as none was stated.
