Prives (Google product manager) says a user can run up to 500 machines, 32,000 cores, billed by the minute; the hourly price listed is what you pay, with no separate transfer or licence fees [04:14, 07:20-07:51]. Machine types shown: 8 cores/16 GB, 16/32, 32/64 and 64 cores/120 GB; C4D users tended to pick the 32 or 64 core ones [23:50]. The beta had no prices yet; his '$2 an hour' was only an example [24:21]. Renderers: Standard and Physical at preview, V-Ray and Arnold said to be coming [08:22, 25:27]. Machines boot in 3 to 5 minutes at most, on Linux [39:41]. Google offered no GPU nodes then and judged GPU rendering demand low [40:11]. Pricing is 2016, so history only.

**Use it:** history baseline for what cloud C4D rendering offered before GPU: CPU nodes, per-minute billing, licences bundled. Today's platform differs on GPU, which is the point.
