Cooper [01:40-02:42]: he started a company to keep doing main titles and was trying to shoot, edit and make everything without one frame he would want to change. People made commitments based on him working on things he did not know he was expected to work on, and he could not control it.
He says he always felt that turning work down was a compromise, so running Prologue with no business partner and no boss meant he had to work out how to get everything done that people asked of him [02:12-02:42].
Earlier: his first pitch was a Scorsese film title (his board was picked), and a Yale typographic training with Paul Rand's "keep your hand in it" shaped him [01:08-01:40].
