Ben Langston (group creative director, Buck since 2003) says Buck's own brand had drifted: every colour used, the logo bent in many ways, and they preached consistency to clients [06:36-07:08]. The fix was a continuum from supporting client work to leading with expression: pitch decks sit back and borrow a colour from the client's brand since Buck often works embedded as their team; collateral sits in the middle; the website and own projects are the expressive end, with starter colour palettes so work starts fast [08:43-10:15]. Lanes and rules gave artists room to play in a controlled way [10:34-11:17].
A website that looked like a 'motion graphics graveyard' was a brand and positioning problem, not only a site problem; they stopped the build ("pencils down") to resolve that [05:34-06:06]. Two weeks before launch an artist's four-column grid redesign replaced the build and became the backbone [11:47-12:19].

**Use it:** when pitching or presenting for a client, move toward the receding end: use their palette and let the work lead.
