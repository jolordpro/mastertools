Louis Moss on the board the client sees [04:46-08:23]: the audience may lack visual literacy, so use fewer, more considered images that sit together as one object. If a strong reference looks jarring (clashing colour, off tone), recolour or edit it to fit; if it stands out to you it will to the client. Nothing pixelated or badly cropped.
Size is an editorial choice: bigger images carry more weight, so give the most central references the most space [05:48-06:20].
Captions (on the board or a second clean-plus-captioned page) say exactly what is referenced: palette, photography treatment, material, type mood. His reason: clients have reacted badly to something on a board that was not even the part being referenced, and once you are on the back foot the idea usually dies [06:20-07:52].
Direction has financial implications (premium materials, photographers, locations); the board should show why the choices are intentional [07:52-08:23].

**Use it:** apply to client style-frame decks: caption each reference with the one thing it stands for.
