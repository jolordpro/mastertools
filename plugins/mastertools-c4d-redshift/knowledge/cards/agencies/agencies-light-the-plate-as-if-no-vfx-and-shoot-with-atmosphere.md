Chris Maclean [20:35-21:39]: lighting was mostly the cinematographer's. Shooting with real smoke and atmosphere helps blending; he calls that the reverse of the old clean-plate habit, and says a messier plate grounds the shot and makes the effect more believable. His instruction to directors: shoot it as if you are not going to do anything, and he will say whether it works. The show's rule is not to let VFX get in the way of a nice shot [21:39].
For big sequences they show concept art, the stage and the rules of what can be shot, and listen to the DP when they ask to face a certain direction [19:02]. He says directors listen about 80% of the time [19:34].
Transfers to broadcast: real atmosphere and a real light direction in a plate make a composite sit.

**Use it:** Ask for a plate with real atmosphere and light direction; do not ask the shoot to be clean for the comp.
