Ben Marriott's three examples [09:14-10:48]: a detailed full-colour live-action commercial board, made because a contract of roughly $100,000 can justify spending a couple of thousand dollars on boards, and aimed at making the client feel safe more than instructing the director; character-focused production boards for animators on a Disney feature, which stress emotion; and a loose director-drawn board on a mystery film, enough for the director and team with no client to approve.
His rule: if you are making it for yourself you can be looser; if for a client you need to win or keep, be more detailed [10:48].

**Use it:** for a sports package pitch, expect boards to need client-grade finish, while internal animator boards need emotion and timing notes more than polish.
