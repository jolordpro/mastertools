Guest Hugo Guerra recruited for his department at the Mill. He looked beyond the reel to the interview, whether the person seemed professional and friendly, hobbies, and even university grades as a sign of responsibility [29:27]. On a pressure job you do not want people who are hard to work with; he would take a somewhat worse reel with a good team player over a prima donna with an amazing one [29:59, 30:30]. He also says artists who hide techniques are making a mistake, since with shared knowledge the differences become experience and professionalism [28:56]. Same point as TJ Kearney in a separate episode.

**Use it:** When the coach or a brief talks about team-building and sign-off, treat attitude as a first-class hiring criterion.
