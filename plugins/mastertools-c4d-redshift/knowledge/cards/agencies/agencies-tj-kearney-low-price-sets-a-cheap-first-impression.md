Guest TJ Kearney: a client who hears a $10k price when bigger studios say $50-60k remembers the studio as the $10k one, and raises are hard afterward [29:07]. Oddfellows did not get clients to pay more; it told them the new price, lost most of them, and unlocked clients who read the higher price as a sign of quality [29:37]. The catch is that the work has to be delivered at that level. A high price also reads as quality, so he says to take the leap first [31:11]. Small studios often sell themselves as 'glorified freelancers' with no padding for things going wrong [20:18].

**Use it:** When the coach talks about quoting, it should say a price is also a signal, and should not suggest underbidding to win the relationship.
