Ben Langsfeld (group creative director, BUCK New York): as the studio went global (about five years before 2020), it lost visibility; there were too many meetings and one office hired a freelancer while staff in another sat idle, which was a red flag [41:42] [42:12] [42:44]. Fix: departments modelled on the CG team's film-pipeline structure, called membranes, a homeroom to check in each day, with each department discussing discipline and reference [43:15] [43:48] [44:19].
Department heads know what each member is working on, struggled with and wants to do, such as an illustrator who likes AR, so in resourcing meetings people can be traded to the right project [44:50] [45:23].
Studio size at the time: nearly 300 across three offices; started with about 10 [03:43].

**Use it:** Reference when advising on how a small studio plans staffing: keep a one-line goal list per person, and check internal staff before hiring freelancers.
