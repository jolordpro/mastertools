Louis Moss (designer and art director, sports and lifestyle brands) sets the standard: a mood board is a snapshot of the finished project, not a pile of inspiration. Every image is a sentence in the argument, and if you cannot say why it is there, remove it [01:07-01:37].
His first test on any board, his or someone else's: can I feel the project in under 10 seconds? If not, change it [01:37-02:10].
Even the private internal board, which can be looser and hold similar versions, must say what each reference is taken for: colour, composition, texture or cultural reference. Volume is the trap: about 50 references blur what you are referencing, and fewer great ones serve better [02:10-03:13].

**Use it:** a pre-pitch check the coach can run: for each reference, one phrase for what is being borrowed, and a 10-second read test.
