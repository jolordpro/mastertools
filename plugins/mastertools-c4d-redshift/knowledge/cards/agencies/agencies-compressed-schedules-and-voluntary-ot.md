Nederhorst: work expands to the time given; he would rather have a third of the time so the team drives to finish, not fiddle [1:05:31].
He cites a film shot at about version 300 that looked like version 7, because there was time to revisit [1:05:00].
On Priest his team did 70-something shots of large CG cities in six months with 40-hour weeks until a few voluntary overtime weeks at the end [1:01:23]. Mid-project they shifted plan from all matte paintings to far more 3D because the notes pointed there (about 30% in) [58:48].
Host Allan McKay: heavy early overtime burns people out before the real push, and make-work R&D (asset libraries for shots nobody asked for) is wasteful [1:01:54].
Host's example: a large particle sequence (about 700 million, claimed) done in 3 weeks instead of 3 months [1:08:04]. Treat claims as anecdote.

**Use it:** Treat a short fixed window per task as a feature; keep overtime for the end and for work with an approved need.
