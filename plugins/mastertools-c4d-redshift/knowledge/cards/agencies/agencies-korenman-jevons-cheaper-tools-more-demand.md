Joey Korenman (School of Motion) frames three shifts (real-time 3D, Rive, AI) with Jevons paradox: a thing that is rare because it is hard gets easier, the makers panic, then demand explodes [03:05, 1:03:53].
His evidence: desktop editing and After Effects killed linear post houses yet created more work; CG replaced stop motion and practical effects, and there are far more shows now [10:17, 35:32]. His own homework figure: animation market about $50B in 1990, about $400B now, growing 8 to 10% a year [37:04]. The figure is his estimate from a talk, not a sourced statistic.
Condition he attaches: the extra work goes to people who adapt, and CG still needs taste and ideas [36:34, 37:36].
