Elmas skincare seaweed job: two weeks total, one week to make it, the rest for feedback and delivery [38:47-40:23]. He asked a colleague for an animated flat surface so he could focus on building the model; they traded back and forth [41:06-41:46]. His main takeaway: keep going back to the reference photos and ask whether a feature is too much, too small or too thin; once you do, the node tree is simple multipliers and ramps [48:12-49:16]. The client did not want the gritty underwater look; they wanted a beautified, iridescent version, and asked for the roots to be made very small and kept out of the way, after the team had gone elaborate with them [52:24-53:31]. A refraction AOV viewed during look dev showed the shape's skeleton, which he liked [53:31-54:05].

**Use it:** Teaching pattern: reference-first checks; plan for the client to tone down a detail you built.
