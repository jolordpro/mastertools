Guest Rob Nederhorst (compositor, VFX supervisor; Digital Domain, Activision, Gnomon Nuke trainer). His rule: decide what the client's ask actually is, hit those notes, and send at the internal cutoff time even though known flaws remain [16:37].
The version must be presentable and technically sound, but not "perfect", because his idea of perfect is not the client's [19:12].
He says polishing alone can cost 300% of the first-pass time (1 day to make, 3 more days of tweaks) before any client note arrives [17:39].
Client turnaround is about a day, so that day goes to a second version in the background; if the note moves the work elsewhere, the polish would have been wasted [18:12].
Host Allan McKay agrees, calling it "broad strokes": get timing and direction approved before secondary detail [20:16].
Transfers to broadcast: show animation timing on plain lighting before texture and look polish.

**Use it:** Plan each shot as a rough-then-refine pair: send a clean broad pass at the cutoff and spend the client wait on the next pass; never polish before timing is approved.
