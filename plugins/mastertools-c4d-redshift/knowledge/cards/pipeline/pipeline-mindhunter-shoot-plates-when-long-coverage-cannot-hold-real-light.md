A long pre-dawn scene in a parked truck had roughly 15 to 20 shots; Messerschmidt and Fincher judged that chasing real dawn light across that coverage would waste time and give no consistency, so they shot plates and did the scene on stage [30:40-31:43]. Plates were rolled for ten to fifty minutes at the time of day wanted, and the exterior bits were the only location work [31:43].
Another benefit: on stage a reflection could be placed to hide an actor's face in the one shot where the story required not seeing it [32:15-33:50].
They were not afraid to pull car doors and windows when reflections would not land [34:21-34:51].

**Use it:** When a scene needs many setups at a time of day that does not hold, build it from a locked plate or HDRI so every shot shares the same light.
