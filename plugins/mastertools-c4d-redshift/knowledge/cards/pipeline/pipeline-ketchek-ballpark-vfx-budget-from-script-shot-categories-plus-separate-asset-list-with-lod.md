Michal Ketchek (VFX supervisor/producer, Magic Lab, Czech studio) in a CEE Animation webinar; auto-captions, so some figures may be misheard. VFX work starts at the script, with a supervisor (creative and technical direction, talks to director) and a producer (quality within budget and time) [35:28]. First pass, done while the script is still being written: split the script into scenes, give each shot a difficulty from category 1 (simplest) to 5 (heavy FX, crowd sims), count, apply a price per category [37:04]. Keep two lists from the start: shots and assets (models, vehicles, creatures, matte paintings, sims) [38:08]. Level of detail matters hugely: a full-screen hero plane can take dozens of days across the pipeline, a distant one about five [39:12]. Add contingency; at script stage the result is within about 30% [40:16]. The detailed breakdown needs script, storyboards, concept art, locations and talks with director and producer, kept in a spreadsheet or database, and is reused for budget, shooting schedule, data capture and asset starts [40:47, 46:57]. Ask VFX in when the first script and concept art exist [1:11:15].

**Use it:** the asset-versus-shot split and the LOD question are good prompts for a cost estimator: how big is each asset on screen.
