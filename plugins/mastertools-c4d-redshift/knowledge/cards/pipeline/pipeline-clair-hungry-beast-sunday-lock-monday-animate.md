Clair says two or three artists made 6 to 10 minutes of animation a week, 30 episodes over roughly 18 months to two years, run in blocks of about ten weeks with months off between [14:17-16:52].
Writers drafted Thursday to Sunday and the host pushed some stories through about 20 drafts; by Monday morning something was ready to animate [15:50-16:20].
The team worked until about 2 am Monday, slept about four hours, worked again until about 3 on Tuesday (the caption does not say am or pm), and saw the piece on air Wednesday night, hours after the last render [16:20-16:52]. The target works out to about four minutes of animation per person per week (he said it fast; treat the figure as approximate) [16:52-17:22].
Early in the week went on templates and rest while scripts were still being written [14:47-15:18].
Transfers: the same shape as a weekly sports or news package with a fixed air slot.

**Use it:** For a weekly-turnaround show, build templates in the quiet days, lock the script before animation starts, and size the team at roughly four minutes of animation per person per week.
