Spire drops the departmental hand-off chain: environments, story, lighting and effects are assembled and rendered interactively in Unreal so each artist sees the full context [03:10]. Sharma's reasons: faster iterations, so more of them; decisions in minutes instead of two or three hours waiting on a render [06:47-07:19]. Epic Mega Grant funded training and a prototype sequence [05:46].
Plans stated: multi-user editing and live remote reviews inside an Unreal session [09:58]; pixel streaming so a phone or iPad can view any shot or sequence, even the version history back to story stage [10:31, 12:07]; USD as the interchange so other DCCs round-trip into Unreal [12:39]; their own asset manager that versions shots and assets [21:30]. Feldman mentions Omniverse as a USD versioning and bridge option [13:11]. Whole animated feature storage is in the hundreds of terabytes [25:10].
A pitch-stage talk by a young studio and its vendor, so these are intentions more than results.

**Use it:** for sports graphics, the transferable idea is review inside a live, streamed scene rather than a rendered file; the USD interchange step is the one to copy.
