Nederhorst says getting estimates right is the hardest skill; he can usually land within about 20 minutes of a 2-hour estimate, and misses when a curveball arrives [49:27].
Log how long tasks take, even loosely, so bids can use that history; if a task always runs an hour over, quote it with that hour [50:29].
A supervisor should see cost actuals: idle but assigned artists still charge time to the job [52:30]. He was refused that breakdown at one studio [53:32].
His example: with a 10% margin, finishing a 10-day estimate in 9 days gives about 20% [53:32]. If you bid 10 and spent 5, you overbid, and next time you know how low you can go [51:00].
Bid and margin numbers are his illustration, not market rates.

**Use it:** Keep a per-shot actual-versus-estimate log and feed it back into the estimator; show the overrun factor per task type.
