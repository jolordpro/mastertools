Paul Franklin (VFX supervisor, DNEG) says digital lets a team push decisions off the shoot into post, but his default is to ask if a physical answer exists [39:08-40:11]. In pre-production he settles the physical-vs-digital balance with the director, DP, art department, special effects and stunts, and "protects" the chosen element [40:42-41:45]. His example: Interstellar used projected images on set screens instead of blue screen, which forced the director to choose the image content earlier, protect budget for it, and tell the whole crew to tolerate a huge projector rig and fan noise [41:45-42:46]. He also notes that filmmakers often retreat to "do it in post" once they see the real cost on set [42:46].
Transfers: any plan that wants a real element (real capture, a hero practical, a locked design) needs the budget, the date and the crew buy-in decided before the shoot or the build starts.

**Use it:** In a plan, list the elements to protect and who must be told, instead of leaving them as "fix in post" options.
