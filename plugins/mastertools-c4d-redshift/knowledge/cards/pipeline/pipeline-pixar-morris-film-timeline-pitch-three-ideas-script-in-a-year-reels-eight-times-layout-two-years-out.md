This is Pixar president Jim Morris, not a roster person (see report: wrong video for the roster) [00:00]. Films take about five years and are not based on bought books; Pixar picks a good storyteller and asks the director to pitch typically three ideas, so no idea becomes an obsession [01:05][01:35]. Director, writer, producer and head of story make a treatment, then an outline or script, aiming for a script within about a year [01:35][02:07]. A story team of six, eight or ten artists boards the script, and the boards are cut by an editor into a mini film; about half a year in a production designer and the editor join [02:07][02:39]. The film is put up in reels typically eight times, torn down each time, with very big changes before the next [02:39][03:11]. Assets begin about three years before delivery and the group ramps to 50-75 people, while story reels continue [03:11][03:41]. Layout starts about two years out; lighting is at the end of the pipeline and a separate tool set; at peak, up to 300 people [03:41][04:16][05:54].
