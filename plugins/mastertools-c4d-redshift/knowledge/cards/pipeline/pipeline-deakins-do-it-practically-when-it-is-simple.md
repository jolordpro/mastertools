Deakins [1:03:20]: for the train that hits the camera in 1917 they put the camera on a piece of foam on the flatbed rather than design a complex rig. It worked.
He dislikes blue/LED-wall stages: why not have the image on a big screen so actors react [1:04:51, 1:06:25]. On set the cry is "do it in vfx, only 15 minutes", and usually it is not [1:06:25].
On Nineteen Eighty-Four a glass-painted shot and an arc-projector image were done in camera [1:05:22]. In the remaster James noticed a real 1,200-person crowd feels different from digital doubles [1:06:55].
The cost of fixing in post: too much emphasis lands on the effect [1:07:27].
Transfer: if the shot can be photographed or played in-engine plate, do it, and keep CG for what cannot be real.

**Use it:** The coach asks 'can this be shot or faked simply?' before approving a heavy CG fix.
