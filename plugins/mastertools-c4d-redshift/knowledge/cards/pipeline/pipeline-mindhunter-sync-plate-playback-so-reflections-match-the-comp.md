Each clip was cued from its beginning, with a blue-light marker in the clip registering where in the timeline it sat, so post could sync it exactly [24:18-24:49]. Gonzalez adds that this is what sells process work: street lights passing in the plate light the actors at the same moment, whereas looping content unsynced makes reflections look fake [24:49-25:50].
Night scenes show errors worst: a street light going by with no matching reflection gives it away [25:19].
Interactive lighting came from panels hung at the sides, top, rear and front, plus floating panels on wheels; contrast on each could be adjusted for harder or softer reflections [17:59-18:31].

**Use it:** When a CG element or plate must carry reflections and light changes, give it a time marker so lighting events in the render land on the same frames as the plate.
