Doyle [36:51-38:28]: a high-end 30-second commercial grade could take a couple of days, so it would not scale to three hours, and Flames of the time were 8-bit. The brief for the first digital grade of a feature was a package that could record back to film at 10-bit log or 12-bit, with scaling and calibration; manufacturers thought it too early, so software people built it.
The point: define the throughput and bit depth you need before choosing tools [37:55].
Two decades later he says the big change is the 3D LUT and colour science being established, so you can say "if the package does not do it, build it" [18:29-20:04]. In the film era the print stocks peaked, with print runs of 27,000 on the later Harry Potters [16:52].

**Use it:** Estimator notes: use the working bit depth and render time per frame as part of the plan; 8-bit chains are only for throwaway tests.
