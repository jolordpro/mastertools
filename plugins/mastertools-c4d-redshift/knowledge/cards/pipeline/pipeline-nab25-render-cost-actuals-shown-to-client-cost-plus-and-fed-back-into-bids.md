Nathan Robinson (Entropic, co-founder of NIM Labs): the industry has billed on loaded rates (artist plus machine plus overhead) that hide what rendering costs; cloud usage gives a report he can hand the client showing what a revision costs [09:31-10:32]. He now wants to bill rendering cost-plus: agree an allocation, show actual cost, pass it on [10:32-11:03]. Sonagra: the same transparency lets the studio recover money, since a blanket CG cost lets clients try to cut hours, whereas a data-backed "this shot took this many hours" is defensible [11:03-11:34]. Robinson's plan: NIM project management captures actuals from the farm back onto the job, so bids, render analysis and actual expense sit together and predict the next similar bid; start the transparency at bidding and educate producers on render cost [13:06-14:08]. Later he suggests AI on that history, including pinpointing cost per shot or per artist and counting bad renders submitted [21:52-22:53].

**Use it:** bid render as its own line (hours x node price) rather than inside a day rate; log actual render hours per shot so the next bid can be checked against them.
