Guest: Erik Messerschmidt. He calls coverage the best part of the job and says cinematography relates more to editing than to photography [42:52-43:54].
With Fincher they are strict about screen direction and cross the line only at chosen points, so the dialogue scenes become a geometry puzzle planned around the cut [43:24-44:25].
Their method about 95% of the time: shoot the wide, let performances loosen, commit to the blocking, then cut it apart. The San Simeon scene had no master and was shot out of order around sun position after long rehearsal [44:57-46:00].
Unlike TV habits, the B camera is not "go get something": they block for two or three cameras and tick off a list of purposeful shots they know will be cut in [47:33-48:35].

**Use it:** Build a shot list where every camera or render angle has a job in the edit; fewer, planned angles beat spray coverage.
