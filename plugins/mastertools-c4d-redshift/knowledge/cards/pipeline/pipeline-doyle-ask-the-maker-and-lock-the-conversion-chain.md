Doyle [45:53-51:36]: a 3D LUT is easy to build, but know whether it is for grading or gamut mapping; on a 150-170 million dollar film nobody wants to be the person who hands over a LUT found late that has a problem in the cyan [46:25]. Give the brief to the specialist rather than micromanage [46:55].
Early Dolby Vision projection: the HDMI feed used a different range than normal full-range, a bit-depth gap, which only shows on very subtle low-light images as dither or noise in the blacks. The exact term was garbled in the captions [47:25-47:56].
A colourist sits in a pipeline of conversions (sensor, display) and should leave those to experts so a problem is a gamut-map fix, not hand-tweaking each shot [48:28-50:05].
How to be taken seriously: set up a test and say "show me"; everyone then reasons about it [51:06].
Fuji once changed print-stock chemistry after Doyle asked for a better cyan-blue for Lord of the Rings [15:50-16:22].

**Use it:** When noise or banding shows only in dark scenes, the coach checks signal range and bit depth in the display chain before regrading.
