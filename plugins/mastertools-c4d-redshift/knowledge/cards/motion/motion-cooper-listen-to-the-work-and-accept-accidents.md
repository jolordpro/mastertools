Cooper [40:44-43:19] says a director who insists on exact intent loses their mind when it rains; one who asks "why not shoot in the rain" gets something better. In dailies he looks for what he never planned and is willing to say "we sold the other idea, but look what happened, this is better". He adds that tight parameters on a job mean you take what you are given and look for serendipity. He listens to clients too, trying to hear what they want rather than prove something.
He also notes that he protected thinking time on one film by wearing headphones and taped glasses, because interruptions wrecked observation [39:42-40:44].

**Use it:** in review, ask for the happy accident: scan test renders for what looks better than planned before locking.
