Dan Perri (veteran film title designer, Academy interview; named as "Perri" in captions). Good title design evokes emotion and refers to the story, characters and setting, introducing credits almost incidentally [00:04]. Contracts fix credit order and relative sizes (an actor must be a set percentage of the main title, e.g. 50%) and he measures them [01:38]. He watches the film, visualises type treatments, sketches by hand, brings several comprehensive alternates to the director (his example: alternates for George Lucas) [02:10-02:40]. Typical job 2-3 months, sometimes over a year; on Days of Heaven the dissolving stills had to be reshot as a whole, about 30 times, when one changed [02:40-03:11]. Small decisions (size, colour, outline, shadow) collectively make the impact; know your tools [03:11]. After Hours: simple centred titles that appeared briefly to match the frantic cut, so the viewer's heartbeat sat at the right pace [03:42]. He avoids a single signature style, valuing relevance to the film over personal look [04:13].

**Use it:** Credit-size percentages and order are a contract check; also a prompt: does the title pace match the cut's pace and story.
