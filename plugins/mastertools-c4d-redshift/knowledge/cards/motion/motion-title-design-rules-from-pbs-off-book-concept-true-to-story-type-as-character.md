PBS Off Book montage; speakers are not named in the captions, so treat as anonymous title designers. A title is a curtain: a precious moment before the film that should not be wasted, a show of respect to the audience [00:03]. Memorability should come from being true to the story, not just striking [00:34]. Aim to reinforce the director's style in a different language rather than duplicate it; contrast is welcome [00:34][01:36]. TV titles must outlast the show, e.g. a final image becoming iconic [01:06]. Zombieland: type integrated into slow-motion shots, interacting with the scene and becoming a character; they avoided making the font do the joke, since the images were strong already [02:08][02:38]. Blue Valentine: the ending used out-of-focus fireworks (lens removed for bokeh) cut rhythmically, then on a test-screening note added nostalgic stills revealed in light and dark [03:39][04:10][04:41]. Saul Bass named as the model for not being literal [05:43].

**Use it:** review lens for opens and stings: does the type serve the story and stay beneath the image.
