Sir Wade's reference points [06:10-09:51]: 50 mm is a portrait-style baseline; 24 mm shows more background but starts to fisheye a close face; 14 mm distorts heavily and hides background objects behind the foreground face; 85 mm flattens and rounds the face; 200 mm squeezes depth so distant props look right behind the subject.
Wide lenses exaggerate anything that moves toward the lens (fast action, swinging arms). Below 50 mm reads comic or high-energy; 70-85 mm reads dramatic, with the blurry-background look of TV drama.
Distortion also grows as the camera physically moves closer, even at the same lens [09:19].

**Use it:** when a shot feels cheap or flat, check the lens before the layout; on product or logo hero frames keep 50-85 mm unless the brief wants energy, and treat a short lens plus a close camera as the distortion risk.
