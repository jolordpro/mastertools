Alex Grigg's order, shown on a baseball swing [04:39-14:12]: (1) research the real motion for inspiration, not to match poses [05:10]; (2) storytelling poses: the comic-book version, with only these the audience still knows what happens [05:42]; (3) extra extremes such as anticipation, overshoot or a settle after the follow-through [06:12]; (4) breakdowns halfway between extremes, each packed with information (weight shift, leading joint, smear frames), then slide them to fix timing [06:43-09:18]; (5) in-betweens, favouring one side so most motion sits on twos, and sometimes skipping a pose to sell anticipation [09:18-10:54]; (6) an effects pass on a new layer, drawn straight ahead [11:24].
Blocking is where big decisions are made and changing your mind is easy; do not leave it until in-betweens [14:12]. He says to treat the process as optional and improvise over it [00:33-01:03].

**Use it:** a note-giving structure: say which stage a shot is at, and give notes in that stage's language (pose, timing, spacing).
