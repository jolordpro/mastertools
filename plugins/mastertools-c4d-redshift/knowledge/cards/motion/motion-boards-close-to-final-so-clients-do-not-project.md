Ben Marriott draws the final boards in black-and-white line, as close to the final design as the style allows [06:40]. His example: the final character matched the board shape for shape, with only the frame design and background simplified [07:11].
Why: a loose stick figure is read as a placeholder, but clients still start forming their own picture of what a human looks like before they see options, and a colour illustration later may not match what they pictured [07:42]. Getting more style frames approved before boarding helps, and the closer final designs are to what clients saw, the more likely they approve with fewer changes [07:42-08:12].
The risk is wasted time if the client rejects the board, but he argues it is far easier to change line art than a coloured illustration or two days of animation [08:12-08:43].

**Use it:** judge a board's detail by what it is for: the more it must prevent a mismatched expectation, the closer it should sit to final shapes.
