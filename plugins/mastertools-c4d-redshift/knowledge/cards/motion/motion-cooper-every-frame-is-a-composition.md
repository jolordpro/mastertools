Wired (2004) says that for a two-minute movie, being obsessive about every cut, every transition and every manipulation of every letter is a job requirement for Cooper, and so is letting go, which he finds hard [§2]. The profile's examples of the same care: at 14 he etched a dragon's texture into a metal plate with a pin over seven weeks, and he still reworks his first feature New Port South in the edit bay [§2]. (Doing design in the edit room, setting rhythm first and planning shots ahead come from other sources, not this page.)

Page spots (Revert to Saved and Eye, not Wired): Cooper likes designing in the edit room to see every designed and animated frame, reviews composition frame by frame, and wants every frame to be a beautiful, perfect composition [reverttosaved §2]; from studying Eisenstein he learned to live and die by the look of each frame [Eye §2]. The edit-room habit is on the Revert to Saved page, so the bracketed remark below that it is 'not this page' applies only to Wired. The Wired anecdotes (dragon etching, New Port South edit-bay rework) are not on these two pages and stay unchecked, so the card stays V2.
