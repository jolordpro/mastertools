Fong warns of the temptation to make a sequence encompass everything about the show; the more you pile in, the mushier it gets [24:52-25:22].
Better to show the hands in the book, only the typography, or one man falling; start wide, then pull back to the impression [25:22-25:52].
Two cases: on a recent streaming drama the director refused to show the imagined world and kept the titles stark and about the characters' inner emotional life [25:52-26:25] (the show's name is garbled in the captions). On Boardwalk Empire she wanted a montage of the huge set, but the show side wanted something that makes people sit up, so the final open shows the lead as the eye of a storm and never shows the boardwalk [26:25-28:31].
She now agrees: the audience is about to see the set anyway [28:31].

**Use it:** When a brief says 'show everything', ask which single image carries it, and cut the rest; list what is deliberately not shown.
