Guest: Jessica Hische. Logotype work needs ego left at the door: the goal is quiet, perfect communication that is as idiot-proof, future-proof and layout-proof as possible, and matches the company's personality [01:35-02:07].
Clients want it ownable: broad style choices or tiny details such as an unusual ligature between two letters that become inseparable from the brand [02:07-02:37].
Unlike decorative lettering, a logo cannot rely on swashes and layered effects; it rarely lives only huge on a shirt or building, so basic letterforms must be precisely right, since decoration can hide bad type but not here [02:37-03:38].
Her listening test in refreshes: where in-house designers struggle to design around the logo (limited fonts, limited layouts); the aim is a logo that is easier to work with [03:38-04:09].

**Use it:** Test a logo or lower-third mark at tiny size and in a plain layout before trusting any detail effects.
