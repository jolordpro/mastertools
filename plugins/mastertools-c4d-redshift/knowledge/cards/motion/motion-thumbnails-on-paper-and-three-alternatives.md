Start with pen and pencil because it limits you: the first pass is a draft that is only about story structure, pacing and composition, not design details [02:36-03:06]. Sticky notes help because shots can be reshuffled; he uses them when arrows start confusing the page [03:06].
Storyboarding is where the biggest decisions happen and mistakes are cheapest: a new thumbnail takes about two minutes, whereas going straight into After Effects can cost hours or days before you learn it fails [04:08].
He demonstrated by drawing two alternative transitions for a bouncing cask (a spinning version and a 3D flip) and rejected the flip because a rotating 3D kangaroo would be hard to animate, found within minutes [05:09-05:39]. He also advises leaving as much time as possible between sessions, ideally sleeping on it, so the next day brings new ideas [06:09].

**Use it:** a pre-production checklist item: three alternatives for the hardest beat, with a production-cost note on each, before any 3D build.
