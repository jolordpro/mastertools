Karin Fong (Imaginary Forces), 2011 talk, on the Ken Kesey documentary titles. The director asked for a look that was not slick CG, rooted in a 1960s hospital room with a tape recorder [34:49-35:52]. She sourced reference from painting directly on film (Len Lye, 1940) and early-60s partial-erasure art (Rauschenberg), because tripping keeps some things and loses others [36:25-37:30]. Type used old rub-down letter sets for a hand-done texture, and the type mattered because the audio was muffled and every word had to be on screen [37:30-38:03]. Her rule: never ignore the source material; Kesey's margin doodles fed the style [38:03-38:36]. (Whisper transcript, so wording is approximate.)

**Use it:** Before inventing a look for a subject, collect the subject's own artefacts (handwriting, period tools, doodles) and pick the medium that was true then. Transfers to sports packages: a team's own archive and local texture beat generic chrome.
