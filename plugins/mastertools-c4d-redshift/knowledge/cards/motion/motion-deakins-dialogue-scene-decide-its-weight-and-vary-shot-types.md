Before choosing shots, decide the weight of the scene: who the focal character is, who is protagonist or antagonist, and note this can change after rehearsal with the actors [1:11:38-1:12:41].
Know where each shot will be used, because eyelines change with who you come off of; work this out in prep, since set gets hectic [1:10:35].
Vary the types of shot: two-shots, an over-shoulder, an insert, a move from one person to another [1:11:07].
Shooting everyone in the same 50 mm size is, in his words, death, because it does not advance the story [1:12:41]. Fun options: play a line on the listener, or shoot hands. James adds: if you go over someone, the foreground person tells viewers where they are, which gets you out of line-of-eye problems [1:14:47].
Directors differ: some want one wide for line one and singles only where needed [1:10:03].

**Use it:** The coach checks that each cut in a scene serves a reason (reaction, power shift) and flags a run of identical framings.
