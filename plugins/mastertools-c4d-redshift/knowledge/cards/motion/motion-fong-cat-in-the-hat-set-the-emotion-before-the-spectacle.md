Her team loved a 3D Rube Goldberg machine as a metaphor for the Cat's mind. The studio, producer Mike Myers and the director said it was not what the film needed [21:25-22:26]. The title had to put viewers back on a parent's lap with the book, the binding and the Seuss drawings, emotionally, before taking them into the built 3D world [22:26-22:57]. She calls it a big lesson: set up the film emotionally, not show off visual gymnastics. The fix she credits: altering the studio logos from the first frame, with flat 2D animation bridging into 3D [22:57-23:28].

**Use it:** Before polishing a clever idea, ask what the viewer should feel at that point in the piece, and let that drive the opening.
