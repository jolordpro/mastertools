Michael Parks: treat music like dialogue and read it frame by frame, scrubbing in Dragonframe, an editor or Audacity and writing which frames beats and accents hit on an exposure sheet [00:52]. Plan the animation to those frames. Animating in ones (24 fps) lets any beat land exactly, even if most moves are shot in twos [01:26]. Fast-changing Lego parts also take a motion-blur filter in After Effects better when shot in ones [01:26]. Trick: if the beat falls on frame 10, shoot to hit it 10th from the end and reverse the clip in post [01:58].

**Use it:** for sports-music cuts, log beat frames first and build hits on them; do not rely on ones-versus-twos defaults for hit frames.
