He is not a big storyboard fan: you draw in your living room with no input, and it is a statement in itself [13:39-14:12, 16:19-16:51]. On Titanic a lipstick camera on a 35-foot model with figurines gave live shots through calibrated lenses (a 20, a 40, a 100), printed as video grabs for the board artist [15:16-15:48]. Living the shot lets you notice the drapes or fireplace and move the chairs. Today he works in Unreal with goggles on, the dolly track included, and The Lion King used VR for every camera setup with Caleb Deschanel [16:51-17:22]. The host's own VR scouting method is also discussed [14:44-15:16, host]. His listed tools: Unreal, Avid, After Effects, sometimes Cinema 4D [31:52-32:22].

**Use it:** Block shots by moving a camera in the 3D scene with real lens values, then board from those frames.
