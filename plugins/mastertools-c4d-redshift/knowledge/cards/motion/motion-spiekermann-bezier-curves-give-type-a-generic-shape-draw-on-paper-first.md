His workflow in the late 1980s: draw letters in pencil and plot them in (the Ikarus program; the author is garbled in the captions), with corner, tangent and curve points [12:03]. Ikarus needs about eight points for a circle (one every 30 degrees), which gives more room to shape than the four of a Bezier circle [49:36].
His claim: Bezier curves have their own aesthetic, so if you put them nicely they all kind of look the same; a lot of Bezier work looks like it came out of a robot [49:06-50:37]. The pencil drawing need not be perfect because the digitising can be precise (hundredths of a millimetre) [50:07]. He worries variable fonts will look very mechanical [50:37].
Transfer (our inference): default Bezier ease handles produce a stock motion feel.

**Use it:** Sketch a motion or a shape by hand before pulling handles; vary the handle lengths away from the default symmetric ease.
