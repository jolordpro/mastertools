Marks says he is drawn to either the macro or the micro. The titles shouldn't merely retell the show's points but circle them, allude to something symbolic, or focus on a minute detail that reflects a wider simplicity [§4]. Example: Halt and Catch Fire's titles are an extreme close-up of electrical signal along wires and mainboards lighting a small flickering light on an 80s PC, standing in for young entrepreneurs under pressure [§4]. He values the double meaning because it lets you comment on themes without depicting them, and cites Dexter's breakfast sequence [§5]. For High Castle he credits the symbolic collisions of the source novel, such as the Nazi eagle laid over the American eagle [§5].

**Use it:** Brief a hero frame with one mundane object or moment and the second meaning it carries.
