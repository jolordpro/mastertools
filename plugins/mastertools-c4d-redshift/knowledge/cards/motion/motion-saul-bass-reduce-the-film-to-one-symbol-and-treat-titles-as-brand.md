Little White Lies, citing author Jan-Christopher Horak [02:45-05:23]. Bass often ended a sequence with a small joke (the Roman head that cracks in Spartacus). He deconstructed the film to find a graphic symbol of its theme (the jagged arm in a drug-addiction film, the corpse in a murder film) and the titles looked like posters because they were one piece of the film's branding; one title shared colour scheme, globe and character design with his earlier newspaper ads. Bauhaus principles: clean, uncluttered, sans-serif (the video says 37 of 53 credit sequences, caption quality uncertain), basic geometric shapes, limited palette of primaries or pastels, legibility first. Earlier titles already did inventive things (fourth-wall breaks in the 1930s and 40s). The video also notes a 20-year gap in his title work and a view that studios now use titles as portfolio calling cards.

**Use it:** Notes language for title concept review: what is the single symbol, how does the sequence end, and does it match the key art?
