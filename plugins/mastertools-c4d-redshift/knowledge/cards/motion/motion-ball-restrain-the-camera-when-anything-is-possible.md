The film has 30-something minutes of full CG with nothing shot [11:57]. Real shoots are limited by sunlight, crew and a truck stuck in mud; those spontaneous constraints are what they tried to keep. Ball likes a dynamic camera that feels made up on the fly, hard in a technical film [12:28-12:58].
With the power to do anything you must restrain yourself and skip the impossible camera move [12:58]; he says he tried to keep it 'almost mundane' and, for the first time, put a camera on sticks and just held the frame, since a synthetic character thinking and reading an expression needs no fancy distraction [13:29-13:59].

**Use it:** Give CG cameras the limits a real camera would have (no impossible moves unless story-driven) and let a locked frame hold the performance.
