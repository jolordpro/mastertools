End titles share the same challenges as main titles but come after the film, so there is more freedom and nothing to hold back [27:41-28:11]. For The Mummy: Tomb of the Dragon Emperor, the film already had the best effects, so the answer was not to compete but to consciously strip down and go abstract [30:15-30:46]. The concept was a Chinese scroll that retells the story as key scenes, like a Greek frieze, using silhouettes and graphic shapes that scale and transition (a face, then a dark void that becomes a cave) [30:46-31:49].
On transitions she says the filmmaker guides the viewer's eye, so surprise and delight come from changing one thing into another; she avoids plain morphs as a cheat [31:49-33:22].

**Use it:** When the main piece is visually heavy, build the follow-on piece in a deliberately different, reduced language and let transitions carry the surprise.
