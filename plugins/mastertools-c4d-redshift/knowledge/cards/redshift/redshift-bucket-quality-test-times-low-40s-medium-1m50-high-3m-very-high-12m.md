One test scene (colour user-data setup), everything else default, GI not changed, a Mac (hardware not stated): Low 40 s, Medium 1 min 50 s, High a little over 3 min, Very High 12 min per frame [2:00:39-2:05:21]. Biggest visible gain is Low to Medium; Medium to High looked nearly the same at double the time [2:03:18-2:04:19]; Very High showed a nicer difference but 12 min a frame [2:04:19-2:05:21].
Her habit: most work between Low and Medium; the second trainer (Darren) uses Medium for lighter scenes or Low plus denoise when there is little fine detail [2:05:53-2:06:25]. She once made a passable bright-plastic motion-graphics frame in under 30 s with Low bucket quality plus denoise [2:06:25-2:07:27]. Do not denoise precious fine textures, volumes, roughness or transparency detail [2:07:27-2:07:57].
She warns that people pick Very High for a nicer render, then wonder why an animation rendered 24 hours [2:05:21-2:05:53].

**Use it:** Use as a worked example when estimating: four quality levels on one frame cost roughly 1x, 2.75x, 4.5x and 18x. One scene only.
