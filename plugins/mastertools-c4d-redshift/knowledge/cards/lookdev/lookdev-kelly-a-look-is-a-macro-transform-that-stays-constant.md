Cullen Kelly [27:37-32:09] says "look" is too loose a word unless it means one creative transform that stays the same on every shot. Shot-by-shot work then sits underneath it. He takes the idea from photochemical timing, where each shot got its own printer-light fix but all of them printed to the same stock.
Rules change for a look: he moves middle gray freely when grading one shot, but does not move it inside a look, because the look is applied to the whole timeline (his example: 2000 shots) and one exposure shift will not suit them all. [30:39-31:43]
Shot grading and look building use different tools and different rules. Both aim at a pleasing reproduction.

**Use it:** when building a show look or LUT for a render set, keep exposure anchored and make the look the part that never changes per shot; put per-shot adjustments in a separate, later layer. Supports the existing Kelly look-order card.
