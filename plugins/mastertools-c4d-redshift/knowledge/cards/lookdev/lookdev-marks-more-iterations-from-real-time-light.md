Raoul Marks says Octane in Cinema 4D changed how he works: as a freelancer without a farm it replaces one with graphics-card power, and it lets him light and texture in real time, taking a big chunk out of the creative feedback loop so the number of iterations becomes almost infinitely larger [§4 of the spotlight page]. He says realism is not the goal in itself; photographers construct images that arrest the eye, and he wants the same [§4]. For titles he likes to allude to a show's themes through a macro view or a minute detail [§4]. On The Man in the High Castle, the Maxon page says Cinema 4D and Octane let the team accurately recreate the complex lighting of image projection, with one reality projected onto another inside one frame [§1 of the Emmy page].

**Use it:** Pick the preview mode that gives the fastest feedback and spend the saved time on more passes at the look.
