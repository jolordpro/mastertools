Dylan Cole says the best way to show a mountain floats is a nice sense of atmosphere or cloud between the mountain and the ground [§2]. For plants, the director wanted cyan for a long time. Cole found cyan sits close to the blue used for atmosphere and depth, so falloff into the distance was hard, images turned monochromatic and cold with none of the rich yellows of green foliage; green also reads as life, which suited the film's themes, so green stayed with wild-coloured plants as accents [§5]. Reference for the mountains was real: karst limestone formations in China, Venezuelan tepuis and Thai karst, balancing rock and vegetation [§1]; he says Roger Dean's whimsical quality was something they wanted to avoid [§5].

**Use it:** Check depth by looking at whether foreground and distance colours are separated by hue or value; haze is a depth tool.
