Hische: take a plain face like Helvetica and round the corners very slightly and it feels softer and more vintage, because ink on paper bleeds a little [28:35-29:36]. She also lists the knobs that change type feeling: width, weight, spacing, how hard or jagged the detail is, and how soft [28:05]. Related existing practice: blur then sharpen type to look burnt in.
Transfers: a small edge softening or bleed on graphics is a cheap way to move a clean digital look toward print or filmic.

**Use it:** Offer a "slightly soft edge" variant of clean type or graphics as an easy step toward a printed or filmic feel.
