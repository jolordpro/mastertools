Say (owner and creative director, Gameday Creative; broadcast graphics for sports and game shows) lists what makes scale add up: bevel width, shadow sharpness, texture density [08:53]. Everything real has a bevel; the mental default for a bevel is about a millimetre, so a bigger rounding makes the object look smaller [09:55-10:28]. Keep a default six-foot human figure in the scene and flip it on and off while texturing and lighting [09:25]. Sharper shadows make an object look bigger (a skyscraper shadow is sharp), softer shadows make it small; he shows the same logo shrinking when a soft-shadow light is switched on [26:39-27:41]. A scratch must be scratch-sized: a grunge tiled too large made the logo look like a penny; he prefers a 16K hand-painted scratch map, since tiling and procedural noise read as CG [37:45-38:16]. A hexagon tile with a tiny bevel looked wrong for the same reason [34:00-34:31].
Transfers directly to C4D + Redshift sports logos at broadcast scale.

**Use it:** Scale check: bevel vs size, shadow softness vs size, texture repeat vs size, with a human reference.
