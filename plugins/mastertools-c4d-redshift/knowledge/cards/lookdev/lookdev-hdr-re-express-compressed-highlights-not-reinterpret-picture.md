Hasegawa's shared idea across both cases: use HDR to preserve or re-express the role of light that was traditionally added in photography, not to reinterpret the entire picture [02:06-02:39]. In cel animation that light was a separate transmitted-light layer, so the digital job kept it separate (case one) [03:41].
For film, high exposure information recorded on the negative was compressed by the release print; the HDR remaster kept the film-derived colour formation and selectively relaxed the highlight limit set by the print. He notes this does not mean the negative contained a separate light layer [14:11-15:13]. Credit for the insight: a Studio Ghibli colleague, Mr. Okui [15:13].

**Use it:** when grading HDR from an approved SDR look, name which highlights are being re-expressed and keep the mid-tones locked.
