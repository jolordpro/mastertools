In Wired's profile the Se7en letters were hand-scratched by Cooper with a needle onto film stock, frame by frame, and then disintegrate to the industrial rhythm of a Nine Inch Nails remix; the effect captured the serial killer's addled mind [§1]. He used real human blood for Dawn of the Dead's titles [§1]. For Spider-Man 2 he skipped the heavy software of the first film and used old-school filmic techniques: stop-motion of real spiders and an octopus he kept and photographed [§3]. (Scratchboard, transfer smear and the tabletop objects are from other sources, not this page.)

Fincher Analyst page check (2026-10-30): typography was scrawled into scratchboard and shot on film, with every letter scratched, smeared and distorted through the camera, and the sequence was mostly assembled by hand rather than in computer graphics [§1]. Props such as fish hooks, razor blades, needles, twine and hair were photographed and tested in camera [§1]. Not on this page: needle-scratching onto film stock, real blood for Dawn of the Dead and the Spider-Man 2 methods, which come from the Wired source, so the card stays V2.

**Use it:** Treat dirt, scratch and smear as something to make once and shoot, or build as a real texture map, not a global filter on top.
