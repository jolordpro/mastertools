He says a cinematographer controls lighting, camera movement and the look with the director [04:11]. Lens and camera distance change how a face or body reads, and he cites old film practice that used close framing to stress features [04:41-05:42]. For viewers, the thing to watch is distortion: noses look larger when the camera is very close or the lens is wide [08:18-08:49]. His test: is the distortion communicating something, or is it just exploiting the moment because it looks good? He wants to be "more conscious of things that are saying something" than things that merely look good [08:49-09:19].

**Use it:** In athlete or portrait frames, state the focal length and distance, and check faces for stretch at frame edges. Pick wide-and-close only when the shot's meaning calls for it. Supports the existing stay-close card.
