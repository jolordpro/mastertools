Blender Guru (2014) [07:19-21:14]. (1) Monochromatic: one colour, best for a single subject; viewers read value and shape. (2) Analogous: neighbours on the wheel, calm and natural. (3) Triadic: three equidistant hues, hardest, suits cartoons and surreal. (4) Complementary: opposites; never use them 50/50, let one dominate (the weaker one, e.g. mostly muted green with red splashes). A small amount of the opposite, such as a blue rim on a yellow-orange character, balances a heavy dominant. (5) Split complementary: one hue plus the two neighbours of its opposite; three colours, livelier. (6) Double complementary: two opposite pairs, best split as foreground pair and background pair; never 25% each, which looks like chaos. Warm and cool are naturally complementary and can set warm/inviting versus cold. Colour planning gets harder in 3D because bounce light shifts the scheme.

**Use it:** Use as a vocabulary when giving or checking colour notes: name the scheme, name the dominant colour, and check the splash is small.
