For Selma he wanted skin that felt like wood, then said that tonality needs primary colours to offset it. He puts a strip of colour in the lookbook frames to show why contrast matters [40:36-41:36], and asked costume to build primaries into wardrobe so the film is not muddy [42:06-42:43]. He took angularity and lens placement from a Charles White painting [42:06].

**Use it:** Check: a dark, low-key, single-hue frame gets one controlled accent (jersey, light, graphic) to keep vibrancy. Useful for dark broadcast looks where team colour can supply the accent.
