In a Gnomon Workshop Live preview interview: separate the problems, solving composition in greyscale before colour. Study 19th-century landscape painters for how pools of light lead the eye, and make the focal point obvious even in huge scenes. Drama and realism come largely from lighting. Model simple geometry, render, then paint over; camera projection needs geometry to land on.

Page spots: bounce between drawing, simple greyscale value studies and colour studies, because full colour illustration makes you solve design, colour and composition at once [§4]; Hudson River painters Bierstadt and Church are his go-tos for pools of light that lead the eye [§4]; 3D for modelling simple geometry, renders painted up in Photoshop [§1]; camera projection needs geometry to project onto [§1]. **Use it:** Check a frame in greyscale first; if the focal point is not obvious there, colour will not fix it.
