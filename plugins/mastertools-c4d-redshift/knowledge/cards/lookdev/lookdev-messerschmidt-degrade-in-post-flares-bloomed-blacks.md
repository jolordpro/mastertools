Guest: Erik Messerschmidt. He dislikes heavy diffusion on the lens because it is hard to make real, and prefers atmosphere, so window glow is lit smoke on set [25:19-25:51].
In the grade they bloomed the blacks: copy the dark areas, defocus them a little. It mimics the softness black-and-white release stock got after being duplicated several times, and adds a pearl quality [25:51-26:23].
The 4K image was deliberately roughed up: extra grain per scene to match the lighting, dust and scratches, gate weave, a little distortion, and reel-change markers as a Fincher touch [28:56-30:29]. Gate weave means the film moving slightly in the gate as pins wear and tolerances play [36:09-37:41].
Modern lenses barely flare, so most flares were painted on in post, such as ring flares and star or striated shapes from the closed iris, adjusted for size and brightness shot by shot [40:48-41:50].
Both asked at times whether it was overdone; they kept riding that line [30:59-31:30].

**Use it:** For a period look in comp: bloom the blacks rather than the highlights, then add grain, dust, weave and flares as separate, adjustable layers.
