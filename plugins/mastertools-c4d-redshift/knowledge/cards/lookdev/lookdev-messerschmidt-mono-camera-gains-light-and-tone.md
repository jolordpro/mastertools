Guest: Erik Messerschmidt. They tested a colour camera graded to black and white against a true monochrome sensor (Red Monstro with the colour filter array stripped). On the cinema screen the colour version looked dull and could not get bite, while the mono had depth, tonal range and a creamy, silver-print quality. Seen next to each other the difference was obvious [16:01-18:37].
Removing the colour filters gives about a stop and a half in speed, so he rated it at 3200 and worked with about 100 to 150 foot-candles rather than around 500 [22:13-22:45].
He also says 3200 is beyond where the camera is cleanest (roughly 1600), so the shadow noise and highlight dithering act like film grain, and that was deliberate [28:56-29:27].

**Use it:** Run any big look decision as a real A/B on a projector-sized screen, not a thumbnail. A greyscale conversion of colour is a weaker base than a made-for-mono design.
