Hische says well-made geometric sans faces break their own grid on purpose. Where strokes merge (a two-story a, the shoulder of n or r) the joint is thinned so it does not read as a dark knot; heavier weights need more of this [34:14-36:51]. She says it separates an expert from a hobbyist. Test she gives: set one huge lowercase letter and compare a circle drawn at a join with one on a lone stroke [37:22].
Transfers: type in titles and lower thirds looks wrong at heavy weights unless joins are checked at large size.

**Use it:** When a heavy-weight title looks clotted, check the joins at full-frame size and prefer a face (or fix) that thins them; do not trust the grid.
