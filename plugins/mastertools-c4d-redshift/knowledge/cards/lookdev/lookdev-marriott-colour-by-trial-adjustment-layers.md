Ben Marriott on colour, which he admits is hard for him [1:04:53]: he colours the simplest frame first (the title) to find the palette before doing the rest [51:50]. In Illustrator he draws about nine boxes (three blues in dark/medium/light plus contrast colours), drops them into the design, duplicates the artboard and varies, and uses Recolor and its randomise button to escape generic choices like green plants and blue skies [52:22-53:56]. In Photoshop he tests with a flat colour layer on a colour or overlay blend and rotates its hue [55:00-55:30]. Once chosen, about three adjustment layers (e.g. orange at 20% overlay or soft light, small hue and curves tweaks) are copied to the other frames and swatches are matched under them [56:31-58:03]. He claims colour theory wheels are not useful because every colour can evoke any emotion; relations matter [46:10-47:00]. Opinion, not research.

**Use it:** for matching a set of frames, keep one shared grade stack and tune each frame's base to it.
