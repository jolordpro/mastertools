He says each stop needs the photosite bucket to hold double the electrons, so more clean latitude is hard; good cameras already give about 14 clean stops with a poor bottom end [28:11-29:15].
A photographic image as presented, with toe and shoulder, is only seven or eight stops, so he doubts more stops are needed; perhaps up to ten [29:15-29:45].
Eyes cannot take in more than about 12 stops at once in a scene, so cameras already out-capture us [32:51-33:55].
He cites the classic photographic scale: skin about a stop over mid-grey, diffuse white about three stops over and diffuse black about three under, seven stops in all, a way of imaging that people still shoot to [38:39-39:11].

**Use it:** When judging contrast or exposure, think in a seven-to-eight-stop picture inside a larger capture range, not in raw stop count.
