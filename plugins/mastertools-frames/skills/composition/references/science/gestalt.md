# Gestalt grouping and figure-ground

## Quick answer
- The eye groups elements by proximity, similarity, continuity, closure, common fate, common region and connectedness. `[E-strong]`
- These are probabilistic cues that compete. The strongest cue wins.
- In layout, group with proximity and common region before color. In motion, common fate dominates. `[Practice]`

## Definition
- **Proximity**: near things group.
- **Similarity**: alike things (color, shape, size, orientation) group.
- **Continuity** (good continuation): smooth lines and curves are followed as one.
- **Closure**: incomplete shapes are seen as complete.
- **Common fate**: things moving together group.
- **Common region**: things inside a shared boundary group.
- **Connectedness**: physically linked things group.
- **Symmetry**: symmetric regions read as figures.
- **Prägnanz**: the simplest stable organization is preferred.
- **Figure-ground**: one region reads as object (figure), the other as background (ground).
- **Border ownership**: the brain assigns each edge to one side; that side becomes figure.

## Mechanism
- Wagemans et al. 2012a: grouping and figure-ground have neural correlates; border-ownership cells exist in early visual cortex. `[E-strong]`
- Wagemans et al. 2012b: Prägnanz reframed through dynamical-systems, Bayesian and structural-information theories. `[E-strong review]`
- Figure cues `[E-strong]`: smaller area, enclosed region, convexity, symmetry, lower region, higher contrast, meaningful shape.

## Techniques
- `[Practice]` Use spacing to say "these belong together" before adding boxes or colors.
- `[Practice]` Use closure for logos and minimal marks (implied shapes).
- `[Practice]` Use continuity to carry the eye along a path to the subject.
- `[Practice]` Use deliberate figure-ground reversal for wit (positive/negative logos).
- `[Practice]` In motion, animate grouped items together; stagger only when you want them read as separate.

## By medium
- Photography: separate the subject from the ground with value, focus and color difference.
- Design: proximity and alignment carry hierarchy; inconsistent spacing breaks grouping.
- Film: blocking groups characters; a character apart from the group reads isolated.
- Broadcast graphics: keep label and value of a stat close and aligned; motion-group elements that belong together.

## How to measure
- Cluster elements by position (proximity) and by color, size, shape (similarity).
- Compare detected groups to intended groups.
- Figure-ground strength: luminance and color difference at the subject boundary; edge contrast along the mask outline.
- Motion: cluster optical-flow vectors; elements with matched vectors are a perceived group.

## Failure modes
- Unrelated items accidentally grouped by proximity.
- Equal spacing everywhere, so nothing groups.
- Weak figure-ground: subject merges with background of similar value.
- Unintended figure-ground reversal (negative space reads as a shape you didn't mean).

## Critique questions
- What groups does the eye see in the first second? Are they the intended ones?
- Is the subject clearly figure, or does it merge with the ground?
- Is any ambiguity deliberate?
