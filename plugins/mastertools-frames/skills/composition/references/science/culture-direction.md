# Culture and direction

## Quick answer
- East Asian traditions and participants favor context-rich frames: higher horizons, smaller figures. `[E-strong]`
- Readers of left-to-right scripts imagine action moving left to right and place agents on the left; right-to-left readers reverse it. `[E-strong]`
- For Western audiences, rightward motion reads as progress or forward; leftward as return or resistance. `[Infer]` Localize direction for right-to-left markets.

## Evidence
- Masuda, Gonzalez, Kwan & Nisbett 2008 `[E-strong]`
  - 15th to 19th century East Asian landscapes: higher horizons than Western.
  - East Asian portraits: faces take a smaller share of the frame.
  - Participants' own drawings and photos match their culture's pattern.
- Senzaki, Masuda & Nand 2014: Japanese children draw higher horizons and more objects than Canadian children. `[E-strong]`
- Chatterjee, Southwood & Basilico 1999: English speakers draw the agent on the left of an action. `[E-strong]`
- Maass & Russo 2003: Italian speakers place the subject left, Arabic speakers right. A small extra left-to-right effect may be hemispheric. `[E-strong]`
- Suitner & Maass 2016: spatial agency bias driven by script direction plus word order. `[E-strong]`

## Techniques
- `[Practice]` Hero moves left to right toward a goal; antagonist enters from the right.
- `[Practice]` Place the "active" team or speaker on the left in Western graphics; mirror for right-to-left markets.
- `[Infer]` Timelines and progress bars run with the reading direction.

## By medium
- Sports broadcast: home vs away screen sides, attacking direction and graphics sliding in should agree with play direction.
- Design: Western layouts start top-left; headline placement follows.
- Film: screen direction carries meaning across cuts (see `continuity-attention`).

## How to measure
- Motion vector direction per element; agent position (left/right).
- Flag direction conflicts: graphic slides against play direction.

## Failure modes
- Mirroring a frame for layout convenience and flipping the story direction.
- Assuming Western defaults for global campaigns.

## Critique questions
- Which way does the action move, and does that match the meaning for this audience?
- Is the frame built for context (wide, small figure) or for the individual (tight, big figure), and is that the intent?

## Terms
- spatial agency bias, script direction, horizon height, holistic vs analytic attention, screen direction
