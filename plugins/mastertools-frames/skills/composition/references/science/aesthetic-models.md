# Aesthetic experience models

## Quick answer
- Response moves from perception to classification to understanding to judgment. Form drives the early stages; meaning, context and expertise drive the later ones. `[E-strong framework]`
- So score formal composition separately from meaning and intent, and report both. `[Infer]`

## Models
- Leder, Belke, Oeberst & Augustin 2004
  - Five stages: perceptual analysis → implicit memory integration → explicit classification (style, content) → cognitive mastering (interpretation) → evaluation.
  - Two outputs: aesthetic emotion and aesthetic judgment.
  - Expertise changes the later stages most.
- Leder & Nadal 2014: update framing the "aesthetic episode," including context and social setting.
- Chatterjee & Vartanian aesthetic triad: sensory-motor, emotion-valuation, meaning-knowledge systems. `[Unverified]`

## Implications for critique `[Infer]`
- Early-stage formal features (value structure, balance, focal clarity, clutter) are the most measurable and most universal.
- Later-stage features (story, symbolism, brand meaning, novelty) need context the image alone cannot give. Ask for intent.
- Experts and novices disagree most on ambiguity, complexity and rule-breaking. Name the audience.
- A frame can be formally clean and still fail its meaning, or formally rough and succeed.

## By medium
- Broadcast and design: audience is mostly non-expert and time-pressed; early-stage fluency dominates.
- Fine art and festival film: audience brings context; later stages carry more weight.

## Critique questions
- What is the intended meaning, and who is the viewer?
- Separate: "Is it well built?" from "Does it say the right thing?"
