# Fluency and complexity

## Quick answer
- Images that are easy to process are liked more. `[E-strong]`
- Interest needs some challenge. People differ in how much complexity they enjoy. `[E-mixed]`
- Broadcast and UI graphics: maximize fluency. Key art and title sequences: trade some fluency for intrigue, but the focal point must still be found fast. `[Infer]`

## Definition
- **Processing fluency**: subjective ease of perceiving and understanding.
- **Visual complexity**: number, variety and irregularity of elements.
- **Liking vs interest**: liking tracks fluency; interest tracks novelty and challenge.

## Mechanism
- Reber, Schwarz & Winkielman 2004: the more fluently an object is processed, the more positive the aesthetic response. `[E-strong]`
- Fluency is raised by: figural goodness, high figure-ground contrast, symmetry, repetition and prior exposure, prototypicality, clarity.
- Berlyne 1971: arousal potential follows an inverted-U; liking peaks at medium complexity or novelty. `[E-mixed] [Unverified]`

## Evidence
- Reber et al. 2004 review `[E-strong]` (fluency → liking across many stimulus types).
- Street et al. 2016: fractal preference peaked near D 1.2 in one sample, with large individual differences. `[E-mixed]`
- Spehar et al. 2003: average preference for mid-range fractal complexity (D 1.3 to 1.5). `[E-strong on average]` See `image-statistics`.
- Experts tolerate and prefer more complexity and ambiguity than novices. `[E-mixed] [Unverified]`

## Techniques
- `[Practice]` One dominant idea per frame; supporting detail at lower contrast.
- `[Practice]` Order inside richness: complex textures contained by simple big shapes.
- `[Practice]` Repetition with one variation (rhythm plus anomaly) gives fluency and interest together.

## By medium
- Photography: simplify the background; let texture live inside a clear value structure.
- Design: limit type sizes and colors; use a grid.
- Film: allow complexity in long takes and wide shots; reduce it for emotional close-ups (Cutting & Armstrong 2016: clutter impairs emotion reading).
- Broadcast: viewers have seconds; fluency wins.

## How to measure
- Clutter (Feature Congestion), edge density, number of segments → complexity proxies.
- Fractal dimension and Fourier slope → naturalness proxies (`image-statistics`).
- Fluency proxies: figure-ground contrast at subject boundary, symmetry score, number of hierarchy levels.

## Failure modes
- Mistaking empty for simple: a sparse frame with no clear focus is still not fluent.
- Too many equal-weight elements.
- Novelty with no anchor: viewers disengage before they find the point.

## Critique questions
- Can a viewer say what this is about in under a second?
- Where does the complexity live, and is it contained by simple structure?
- Who is the audience, novice or expert?

## Terms
- processing fluency, complexity, arousal potential, inverted-U, prototypicality, figural goodness, optimal complexity
