# Rules vs evidence

## Quick answer
- Supported: center bias, inward bias, representational fit, luminance-first structure, figure-ground and grouping cues, clutter hurting glanceability, motion capturing attention.
- Weak or refuted as quality laws: rule of thirds, golden ratio or spiral, Arnheim's detailed balance theory.
- Unknown or untested: many studio rules of thumb. Treat as `[Practice]`.

## Verdicts
Each line: claim → verdict · basis · file.

### Placement and geometry
- Rule of thirds makes images better → `[Lore]` as a law · Amirshahi et al. 2014 found a minor role at most; Cutting 2015 found 19% of one-shot faces in thirds zones · `geometry-proportion`
- Rule of thirds helps novices like photos → `[E-mixed]` · Torabi & Teeravarunyou 2021: not more appealing to novices; more interesting to experts · `geometry-proportion`
- Centered subjects are boring → `[Lore]` for front-facing single subjects · Palmer et al. 2008: center is preferred · `spatial-composition`
- Leave space in front of a facing or moving subject → `[E-strong]` · inward bias in lab, painting, photo and film · `spatial-composition`
- Golden ratio rectangles are most beautiful → `[E-mixed]`, mostly lore · Green 1995: real but fragile; McManus 1980: 1.618 vs 1.5 to 1.75 unclear · `geometry-proportion`
- Masters used the golden ratio for canvas shapes → `[Lore]` as a general claim · Olariu: 565 paintings averaged 1.34 ± 0.12 · `geometry-proportion`
- Golden spiral placement improves composition → `[Lore]` · Stieger & Swami 2015: no automatic preference · `geometry-proportion`
- Never put the horizon in the middle → `[Practice]` · no direct test found; centered horizons suit symmetry and reflections · `framing-cropping`
- Rule of odds (odd numbers of objects look better) → `[Unverified]` · no controlled evidence found this pass · `rhythm-pattern`

### Balance and structure
- Good pictures are balanced → `[E-mixed]` · art photos slightly more balanced than random (McManus 2011); link to liking depends on image type (Hübner & Fillinger 2019) · `balance`
- Arnheim's structural skeleton predicts where weight sits → `[E-mixed]` · detailed predictions not supported (McManus 2011); rough saliency alignment (Jahanian 2015) · `balance`
- Symmetry is preferred → `[E-strong]` for liking via fluency · Reber et al. 2004 · `fluency-complexity`

### Light and color
- Value structure carries composition more than hue → `[E-strong]` physiology · Livingstone: luminance pathway handles depth, figure-ground, motion · `value-contrast`
- Warm colors advance, cool recede → `[E-mixed]` · aerial perspective (contrast and saturation loss) is the more reliable depth cue · `color`, `depth-space`
- Color preferences are universal and fixed → `[Lore]` · WAVE: preferences follow learned object associations (Palmer & Schloss 2010) · `color`
- Red means excitement, blue means calm everywhere → `[E-mixed]` · associations vary with context and culture · `color`

### Line and direction
- Leading lines lead the eye → `[E-mixed]` · edges and gaze cues guide attention; strength vs other cues untested in most cases · `line-direction`
- Diagonals feel energetic, horizontals calm → `[Practice]` · Arnheim, Bang; limited direct testing · `line-direction`
- People read images left to right → `[E-mixed]` · spatial agency bias is strong for imagined action; scanning order in images depends on content and task · `culture-direction`
- Viewers follow a depicted person's gaze → `[E-strong]` · gaze cueing research · `line-direction`

### Film and motion
- Faster cutting means modern films are better or worse → neither · Cutting et al. 2011: ASL fell from about 10 s to under 4 s; quality not measured · `film-statistics`
- Film shot lengths evolved toward 1/f → `[E-mixed]` · disputed as a statistical artifact (Salt; Redfern) · `film-statistics`
- Continuity editing works because viewers build a 3D map → `[E-mixed]` · Smith 2012: continuity is perceived through attention cues without a coherent spatial map · `continuity-attention`
- Higher audience brain synchrony means a better film → `[Lore]` · Hasson et al. 2008 say greater correlation doesn't mean better · `continuity-attention`

### Design
- More white space always looks premium → `[Practice]` with limits · fluency gains vanish when hierarchy is unclear · `negative-space`
- F-pattern and Z-pattern scanning → `[E-mixed] [Unverified]` · eye-tracking of text-heavy web pages; not general to images · `typography`

## How to apply
- Never deduct points for missing a `[Lore]` rule.
- When citing a `[Practice]` rule, give the reason it serves (direction, balance, hierarchy), not the rule's name.
