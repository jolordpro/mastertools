# Balance and visual weight

## Quick answer
- Treat balance as a soft constraint, strongest for abstract and graphic images. `[E-mixed]`
- Visual weight comes from size, value contrast, saturation, isolation, interest (faces, text) and position. `[Practice]`
- Left-right balance matters more than top-bottom. `[E-mixed]`
- Imbalance is a tool for tension. Score it as a flaw only when unmotivated.

## Definition
- **Balance**: distribution of visual weight around the frame's center and axes.
- **Symmetrical balance**: mirrored weight. **Asymmetrical balance**: unequal elements balanced by weight. **Radial balance**: weight around a center.
- **Structural skeleton** (Arnheim): hidden lines of force (center, vertical and horizontal axes, diagonals) that pull on elements. `[Unverified]`

## Mechanism
- `[Unverified]` Arnheim: elements on the structural skeleton feel stable; elements slightly off it feel pulled.
- `[Practice]` Steelyard principle: a small, bright or isolated element far from center balances a large mass near center, like a lever.
- `[E-strong]` Faces and text attract attention strongly, so they carry extra weight beyond their size (see `attention-saliency`).

## Evidence
- Wilson & Chatterjee 2005 `[E-strong for simple images]`
  - APB (Assessment of Preference for Balance) compares weight across 8 axes.
  - Explained 73% and 78% of preference variance for simple circle and hexagon images.
- McManus, Stöver & Kim 2011 `[E-mixed]`
  - 455 art photographs vs 595 random photographs.
  - 46.6% vs 39.7% met the balance criterion.
  - Center of mass 0.0444 vs 0.0516 picture widths from center.
  - SAYS: the detailed results did not support the Arnheim-Ross theory.
- Hübner & Fillinger 2016 `[E-mixed]`
  - Center-of-mass deviation (DCM) predicts perceived balance better.
  - APB predicts liking better, because it also captures homogeneity.
- Hübner & Fillinger 2019 `[E-mixed]`
  - Balance-liking link depends on picture type; weaker for photos.
  - For Japanese calligraphy, balance ratings were unrelated to liking.
- Jahanian et al. 2015 (MIT CSAIL) `[E-mixed]`: saliency hotspots roughly align with Arnheim's net. Conference paper.

## Weight factors `[Practice]`
- Size: larger is heavier.
- Value contrast: high contrast against surroundings is heavier.
- Saturation and warm hue: heavier than gray or cool.
- Isolation: a lone element is heavier than the same element in a group.
- Interest: faces, eyes, text, hands, animals outweigh their area.
- Position: farther from center is heavier (lever). Upper areas often read heavier than lower. `[Unverified]`
- Texture and detail: complex regions outweigh flat ones.
- Direction: elements pointing or looking add weight to where they point.

## Techniques
- `[Practice]` Counterweight a large dark mass with a small bright accent on the far side.
- `[Practice]` Use symmetry for stability, ceremony, authority; asymmetry for life and movement.
- `[Practice]` In layouts, balance an image block with a type block of similar visual weight.

## By medium
- Photography: horizon and main mass placement drive balance; check in grayscale.
- Design: balance includes type, logos and empty space.
- Film: balance can shift across a shot; a frame can resolve as a character moves.
- Broadcast: persistent bugs and score boxes add constant weight to corners; compose talent with them in mind.

## How to measure
- Weight map = luminance contrast × saliency (optionally add face and text boosts).
- Center of mass of the weight map → offset from center as % of width and height.
- APB: compare total weight on each side of 8 axes (vertical, horizontal, two diagonals, plus quadrant pairs).
- Report left-right and top-bottom separately.
- See `metric-recipes` for formulas.

## Failure modes
- Equating balance with symmetry.
- Ignoring faces, text and logos as weight.
- All weight in one corner with no counterweight and no story reason.
- Over-balancing into static, dead centered layouts when energy is wanted.

## Critique questions
- If the frame were a seesaw on its center, which way would it tip?
- Is any imbalance doing story work?
- Does a small high-interest element counter the big mass?
