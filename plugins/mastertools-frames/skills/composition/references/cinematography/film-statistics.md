# Film statistics

## Quick answer
- Hollywood films became quicker, more kinetic and darker over 75 years. `[E-strong descriptive]`
- Average shot length fell from about 10 s (1930s to 1940s) to under 4 s after 2000. `[E-strong]`
- The claim that shot lengths "evolved toward 1/f" is disputed. `[E-mixed]`

## Evidence
- Cutting, Brunick, DeLong, Iricinschi & Candan 2011, 160 films, about 1,100 shots each `[E-strong]`
  - ASL about 10 s in the 1930s and 1940s, below 4 s after 2000 (r = −.75).
  - Longest ASL in sample: *The Seven Year Itch* (1955), 26.2 s. Shortest: *Rocky IV* (1985), 2.2 s.
  - About 99% of transitions have been cuts since the 1970s.
  - Motion within shots rose (r = .583). Mean visual activity index 0.05 (*Barry Lyndon* 0.008; *Toy Story 3* 0.122).
  - Moving-camera shots fell from 16% (1959) to 6% (1999).
  - Films got darker (r = −.385); mean 8-bit luminance ranged 162.0 to 72.1.
  - Conflict: Cutting & Armstrong 2016 restate the trend as about 14 s to about 4.5 s. Prefer the 2011 figures.
- Cutting, DeLong & Nothelfer 2010, 150 films `[E-mixed]`
  - Shot lengths became more autocorrelated and closer to 1/f (release year r = .43).
  - Salt and Redfern argue this is a statistical artifact.
- Cutting et al. 2018: shot durations, scene durations, motion and sound became more fractal-like; luminance, clutter and shot scale did not. `[E-mixed]`
- Cutting 2015 and Cutting & Armstrong 2016: shot scale distribution and shot duration by scale (see `shot-grammar`).

## Implications `[Infer]`
- Faster cutting relies on centered, high-contrast focal points so viewers find them fast after each cut.
- Darker films shift the burden of hierarchy onto local contrast and practical lights.
- Broadcast audiences are trained by this pace; graphics holds must still be long enough to read.

## How to measure
- Shot boundary detection → shot length list → ASL, median, variance.
- Mean luminance per shot; motion magnitude per shot (optical flow).
- Power spectrum of the shot-length sequence (for 1/f claims, with caution).

## Critique questions
- Does the cutting rhythm match the intended energy?
- Are focal points easy to find at this pace?

## Terms
