# Camera angle, lens and movement

## Quick answer
- Angle sets power: low looks up (strength, threat), high looks down (vulnerability), eye level is neutral. `[Practice; partial evidence]`
- Lens sets space: wide exaggerates depth and distance, long compresses and isolates. `[Physics + Practice]`
- Movement must be motivated by story, character or reveal; unmotivated movement draws attention to the camera. `[Practice]`

## Angles `[Practice]`
- Eye level: neutral, equal.
- Low angle: power, heroism, menace, scale.
- High angle: smallness, vulnerability, surveillance, overview.
- Overhead / bird's eye: pattern, fate, choreography (sports formations).
- Worm's eye: extreme monumentality.
- Dutch / canted: unease, disorientation, instability.
- ShotBench includes camera-angle labels for automatic classification.

## Lens `[Practice]`
- Wide: deep space, distortion close up, characters feel far apart, environment dominates.
- Normal: natural relationships.
- Long: flattened planes, crowded backgrounds, voyeuristic distance, isolation via shallow focus.
- Dolly zoom (push with zoom out or the reverse): background stretches or compresses while subject size holds; vertigo, realization.
- Anamorphic: wide aspect, oval bokeh, horizontal flares; reads "cinematic."

## Movement `[Practice]`
- Pan / tilt: survey, follow, reveal.
- Dolly / track / truck: move through space; parallax adds depth.
- Push in: increasing intensity, realization, intimacy.
- Pull out: reveal context, isolation, ending.
- Crane / jib: rise for overview or departure; descend to enter a scene.
- Handheld: urgency, documentary truth, instability.
- Steadicam / gimbal: floating, following, immersive.
- Long take (oner): time and space unbroken; attention managed by staging instead of cuts (Lubezki, see `filmmakers`). `[Infer]`
- Zoom: draws attention to the act of looking (1970s style, documentary).

## Evidence
- Cutting et al. 2011: moving-camera shots fell from 16% (1959) to 6% (1999) in their sample while motion within shots rose. `[E-strong descriptive]`
- ShotBench 2025: camera movement is among the hardest dimensions for AI models; ShotVL improved most here. `[E-strong for AI difficulty]`

## By medium
- Broadcast sports: high wide angle for play readability; low angles for player intros; slow pushes in for drama in features.
- Motion design and 3D: virtual cameras follow the same rules; easing on camera moves matters as much as on objects (see `motion-principles`).

## How to measure
- Horizon tilt and vanishing-point position → angle estimate.
- Optical flow patterns → pan, tilt, dolly, zoom classification (zoom = radial flow without parallax; dolly = radial flow with parallax).
- Shake magnitude → handheld vs stabilized.

## Failure modes
- Unmotivated moves and drifting.
- Wide lens close on faces when flattery is wanted.
- Accidental slight Dutch angle.

## Critique questions
- Why is the camera here, at this height, with this lens?
- What motivates this move, and does it end on something?
