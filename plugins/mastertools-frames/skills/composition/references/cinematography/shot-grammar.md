# Shot grammar

## Quick answer
- Shot size sets emotional distance: wide = context and isolation, close = emotion and intensity. `[Practice]`
- Popular film centers on medium scales; tighter shots run shorter. `[E-strong descriptive]`
- About 90% of shots in popular movies show at least one character's face. `[E-strong descriptive]`

## Shot sizes `[Practice]`
- ELS / EWS (extreme long or wide): landscape, scale, establishing.
- LS / WS / full shot: whole body with environment.
- MLS / cowboy (mid-thigh up): body language plus space.
- MS (waist up): conversation default.
- MCU (chest up): personal, readable expression.
- CU (face): emotion, reaction.
- ECU (eyes, mouth, object detail): intensity, revelation.
- Others: two-shot, three-shot, over-the-shoulder (OTS), single, insert, cutaway, establishing, POV, reaction.

## Evidence
- Cutting 2015, 48 films 1935 to 2010, about 14,000 images `[E-strong descriptive]`
  - Shot-scale share: ELS 8%, LS 14%, MLS 24%, MS 30%, MCU 16%, CU 6%, ECU 1%.
  - Characters per frame fell from about 2.5 (1935 to 1950) to about 1.5 (1995 to 2010).
  - Each added character needs about 1.5 s more per shot.
  - 41% of faces in thirds zones overall, 19% in one-shots.
- Cutting & Armstrong 2016, 24 films, about 31,000 shots `[E-strong]`
  - Duration falls from about 8 s (ELS) to about 2.5 s (ECU).
  - Fully 90% of shots contain characters. Clutter impairs emotion recognition.
- Cutting 2018: 90% of shots in popular movies show at least one face, constant across history.

## Framing conventions `[Practice]`
- Headroom shrinks as shots tighten; ECUs cut into the forehead.
- Look room toward the other speaker in singles; reverse shot mirrors it.
- Eyes often placed near the upper third line in MCU and CU. `[Practice, not a law]`
- OTS: foreground shoulder anchors depth and connection.
- Match shot sizes in a shot/reverse-shot pair to keep characters equal; mismatch to show imbalance of power.
- Mascelli's Five Cs: camera angles, continuity, cutting, close-ups, composition. `[Unverified]`

## By medium
- Broadcast interviews: MCU with look room; graphics in the lower third need clean space below the chin.
- Sports broadcast: wide game camera for play, tight iso shots for emotion, replays at multiple sizes.
- Social vertical: MCU and CU dominate; wide shots lose detail at phone size.

## How to measure
- Face height as % of frame height → shot size class.
- Character count per frame (face or person detection).
- ShotVL or ShotBench-trained classifiers for shot size, angle, framing.

## Failure modes
- Too much headroom in close shots.
- Inconsistent shot sizes in shot/reverse pairs without intent.
- Wide shots used for emotional peaks at phone size.

## Critique questions
- Does the shot size match the emotional distance the beat wants?
- Is the headroom and look room appropriate for this size?
