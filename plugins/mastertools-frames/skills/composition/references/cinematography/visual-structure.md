# Visual structure (Block)

## Quick answer
- Every visual component can be pushed toward contrast (more intensity) or affinity (calm). `[Practice] [Unverified exact framing]`
- Map visual intensity to story intensity: build contrast toward climaxes, return to affinity in resolution. `[Practice]`
- Plan components across a whole piece, not frame by frame. This is the tool for promos, opens and title sequences.

## The seven basic components `[Unverified]`
- Space (deep, flat, limited, ambiguous).
- Line (direction, orientation, quality).
- Shape (circle, square, triangle; basic vs complex).
- Tone (brightness range, contrast).
- Color (hue, saturation, temperature).
- Movement (object, camera, eye movement).
- Rhythm (alternation and repetition in space and time).

## Principle of contrast and affinity
- Contrast: difference between elements, within a shot or across cuts, increases intensity.
- Affinity: similarity reduces intensity.
- Applies within the frame (intra-shot), across a cut (inter-shot), and across a sequence.

## Story structure graph `[Practice]`
- Plot story intensity over time (exposition, rising action, climax, resolution).
- Plot each component's intensity under it.
- Align peaks: the climax gets the most contrast on the most components.

## Techniques `[Practice]`
- Start flat and affined, deepen space and raise contrast as tension rises.
- Shift palette from analogous to complementary at the turning point.
- Increase cutting rate and movement toward the climax.
- Hold a single component back to save it for the peak.

## By medium
- Film and series: plan per sequence and per act.
- Broadcast opens and promos: 15 to 60 seconds still have an arc; map intensity to the music.
- Motion design packages: transitions are affinity bridges between contrasting segments.
- Single stills: contrast and affinity still apply within the frame.

## How to measure
- Per-shot values: ΔL contrast, saturation, dominant line orientation, depth plane count, motion magnitude, shot length.
- Plot over time; compare to a story or music intensity curve.

## Failure modes
- Peak intensity everywhere, so nothing peaks.
- Visual intensity unrelated to story beats.
- All components rising together too early, nothing left for the climax.

## Critique questions
- Where is the visual peak, and does it land on the story peak?
- Which components are held back, and where are they released?
