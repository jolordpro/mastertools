# Canon: sports photography and athlete key art

Job type: action stills, portraits and media-day shots, athlete campaign key art (posters, hero images with type).
Library ids in backticks are mechanisms, not decoration. Evidence tags follow `01-evidence-legend`.
Evidence labels: **DOC** = the maker or the agency said it. **RETRO** = my reading after the fact.

How to read "See": these are pages that carry or describe the image. Open them to look. Nothing was saved.

## Exemplars

### SP-01 Ali stands over Liston · Neil Leifer, Sports Illustrated · 1965 · no award at the time (ran on the last page of the story)
- See: https://www.bostonglobe.com/2025/05/25/arts/greatest-sports-photo-60-lewiston-maine · https://amateurphotographer.com/iconic-images/muhammad-ali-vs-sonny-liston-by-neil-leifer-iconic-photograph
- Structure: Ali dead center, arm raised, over the fallen Liston. Square colour frame from a Rolleiflex with strobe. Plain off-white canvas, no logos on trunks or gloves. Strobes hung about 20 ft up lit the ring like a studio set; smoke turned the dark surround blue.
- Why it works: one subject wins on value, colour and size against a calm field (`hierarchy-focal-point`, `value-contrast`). The stage is stripped of ads (`clutter-peripheral`). Light was planned for where the action would land (`light`). Centered and near-symmetric, which suits a dominance image (`spatial-composition`, `balance`). The arm and gaze point down at the second figure (`line-direction`).
- Push move: pre-light the stage and remove everything that is not the story. In post: darken and cool the surround, keep the subject's key warm, paint out marks and logos.
- Evidence: DOC `[Practice]`. Leifer himself says much of the power is the subject, so do not over-credit the framing.

### SP-02 Ali over Cleveland Williams, overhead · Neil Leifer, Sports Illustrated · 1966 · Houston Astrodome
- See: https://rarehistoricalphotos.com/muhammad-ali-knocks-cleveland-williams-astrodome-houston-1966/
- Structure: remote camera hung about 80 ft above the ring. Williams down on the canvas, Ali posing. The ring becomes a flat geometric stage. Leifer calls it more distanced and abstract than the Liston frame and names it his favourite. Lens not stated.
- Why it works: the vantage strips depth cues so the scene reads as graphic shape (`depth-space`, `shape`). Figures are small marks on a large plane, so scale does the drama (`scale`, `negative-space`).
- Push move: change the vantage until the scene becomes a graphic. Plan the rig, take many frames before the event, wait. Needs a new shoot; in post the nearest move is a hard top-down crop of a high frame.
- Evidence: DOC `[Practice]`. Lens `[Unverified]`.

### SP-03 Orr flying after the Cup goal · Ray Lussier, Boston Record American · 1970 · no award found
- See: https://www.bostonmagazine.com/2014/05/08/throwback-thursday-bobby-orr-took-flight · https://thehockeywriters.com/bobby-orr-flying-goal-iconic/
- Structure: Orr airborne with arms spread right after the overtime goal in Game 4. Noel Picard tripped him as he started to celebrate; the goalie was Glenn Hall; the crowd behind has arms raised. Lussier took over another photographer's spot at the attacking end and, per one report, used a Nikon F with motor drive.
- Why it works: the celebration beat, not the shot beat, carries the picture (`sports-action`, `attention-saliency`). Feet off the ice make a single readable shape (`shape`). Position was chosen for where the story would land (`photo-craft`).
- Push move: pick the frame after the release. Arms out, feet off the surface, face open, beats a stick-on-puck frame. For game footage, mark that beat as the hero frame.
- Evidence: DOC (newspaper report of Lussier) plus RETRO. Exact framing `[Unverified]`; sources disagree on the credit line.

### SP-04 Miracle on Ice cover · Heinz Kluetmeier, Sports Illustrated · 1980 · SI cover with no headline
- See: https://dartmouthalumnimagazine.com/articles/miracle-ice · https://www.si.com/more-sports/2008/08/19/heinz-qanda
- Structure: players celebrating on the ice, a fan's American flag in the frame behind the goal. The cover ran with no cover line. Kluetmeier called the flag luck, then said "I made sure it framed the celebration" (Dartmouth Alumni Magazine).
- Why it works: a symbolic colour accent sits inside the group, not at the edge (`color`, `hierarchy-focal-point`). The story was known, so type was removed and the image carried the page (`typography` as omission, `fluency-complexity`).
- Push move: when the story is known, drop the headline and let the frame carry. Place the symbol inside the cluster.
- Evidence: DOC `[Practice]`.

### SP-05 Phelps wins the 100 fly, underwater finish · Heinz Kluetmeier, Sports Illustrated · 2008 Beijing
- See: https://www.si.com/more-sports/2008/08/19/heinz-qanda · https://www.ishof.org/honoree/honoree-heinz-kluetmeier-2/
- Structure: remote underwater camera at the wall with a live feed to a computer; an assistant spent about 40 minutes moving it under an inch. Eight frames a second, started before the swimmers entered. Kluetmeier wanted full body length, the touchpad and the start in frame and called it "not a pretty stroke picture".
- Why it works: the frame holds the evidence of the result, not only form (`photo-craft`, `camera-lens-movement`). Prefocus on one plane and wait.
- Push move: frame the decisive fact, not the prettiest pose. Include the object that proves it (pad, line, puck, net).
- Evidence: DOC `[Practice]`.

### SP-06 Kawhi watches the Game 7 buzzer-beater · Mark Blinch, NBAE · 12 May 2019 · World Press Photo 2020, Sports Singles, 1st prize
- See: https://www.worldpressphoto.org/collection/photo/2020/39654/1/Mark-Blinch · https://www.si.com/nba/2019/05/15/kawhi-leonard-game-7-buzzer-beater-raptors-76ers-vince-carter-joel-embiid
- Structure: shot from a high press-box seat. Blinch kept framing loose to take in the arena and liked that it shows everyone watching. He kept shooting as the ball bounced on the rim. Two colleagues made other choices: one fired a single strobe frame; one moved to midcourt for a clear view, then swung to reactions.
- Why it works: a small figure inside a crowd whose gazes all point one way. Gaze is a strong attention vector (`line-direction` `[E-strong]`), and common fate groups the crowd (`gestalt`). Scale shows the size of the moment (`scale`).
- Push move: go wide on purpose when the audience is part of the story. Do not crop a reaction shot down to the athlete.
- Evidence: DOC (Blinch, Garrabrant, Dow in SI). Camera settings not published `[Unverified]`.

### SP-07 Skier Ondrej Bank loses control before the jump · Christian Walgram, GEPA pictures · 2015 · World Press Photo 2016, Sports, 1st prize
- See: https://www.worldpressphoto.org/collection/photo-contest/2016/christian-walgram/1
- Structure: the entry lists 800 mm, f/8, 1/2500 s, ISO 800. Caption: he lost control before the final jump. Where Walgram stood is not stated. Background detail I did not confirm.
- Why it works: an 800 mm lens compresses space and simplifies the background; fast shutter freezes the moment (`photo-craft`, `clutter-peripheral`, `depth-space` focus falloff).
- Push move: isolate with distance. In post, crop in hard on a high-resolution frame to mimic a longer lens, and deepen the background falloff to match.
- Evidence: DOC for settings (WPP entry). Structure reading `[Infer]`.

### SP-08 Jockey falls at the Grand National · Tom Jenkins, The Guardian · 2016 · World Press Photo 2017, Sports Singles, 1st prize
- See: https://bynder.southbankcentre.co.uk/m/215ca8671a54e532/original/World_Press_Photo_press_release_2017.pdf
- Structure: remote camera placed at a fence in heavy rain; a jockey mid-air leaving his horse. Jenkins said tired horses failed to jump cleanly, which produced fallers.
- Why it works: the failure moment gives unusual geometry (`line-direction`, `shape`). The frame was pre-set at the place where the unexpected would cross it (`spatial-composition`).
- Push move: pre-place the frame where the odd moment will happen and wait. For graphics, favour the rare beat (fall, save, collision) over the routine one.
- Evidence: DOC (Jenkins, via a search summary of an interview; the page itself was not opened) `[Practice]`.

### SP-09 "Golden Moment", Medina above the wave · Jerome Brouillet, AFP · 2024 · World Sports Photography Awards 2025, overall gold (Aquatic gold)
- See: https://www.nbcnews.com/sports/olympics/brazilian-surfers-historic-ride-olympics-spawns-iconic-photo-rcna164393 · https://irishtimes.com/sport/2024/07/30/how-the-breathtaking-image-of-olympic-surfer-gabriel-medina-was-captured
- Structure: from a media boat; Medina above the wave lip after a kick-out, right index finger to the sky. Board and stance align and the leash runs near horizontal, so he seems to stand on solid ground. Sky and sea as the ground. One of four frames. Nikon Z9 per EXIF.
- Why it works: body, board and leash read as one shape (`gestalt` continuity, `shape`). The pointing gesture is an attention vector (`line-direction` `[E-strong]`). Clean sky and sea give a calm field (`negative-space`). Hidden ground plane flattens depth on purpose (`depth-space`).
- Push move: choose the frame where gesture, equipment and horizon line up into one shape, even over a sharper or bigger frame. The pointing hand also gives the eye an exit toward type room.
- Evidence: DOC (AFP, Time and NPR via Irish Times and NBC; Brouillet credits "experience, timing, and a bit of luck") `[Practice]`.

### SP-10 Bolt's grin at the finish · Cameron Spencer, Getty Images · 2016 Rio
- See: https://petapixel.com/2016/08/16/photographer-snaps-iconic-photo-usain-bolts-winning-smile · https://awfulannouncing.com/2016/getty-photographer-explains-how-he-got-amazing-usain-bolt-olympics-image.html
- Structure: handheld, 70-200 at 135 mm (one source writes "72-200"), about 1/40 s, feet planted, panning with Bolt. Spencer left his assigned field events to cross the infield. He saw the grin and eye contact only when he checked the back of the camera.
- Why it works: sharp face and eyes against a streaked background (`photo-craft`, `attention-saliency` `[E-strong]` for faces and eyes). Motion streaks stand in for speed without hiding the person (`texture-detail`, `depth-space`).
- Push move: slow shutter, pan, face sharp. In post, apply directional blur to the background only, behind a clean subject mask, and keep contact shadows.
- Evidence: DOC (Spencer, via PetaPixel and others) `[Practice]`.

### SP-11 Carl Lewis anchors the 4x100 relay; remote-camera method · Bill Frakes, Sports Illustrated · 1992 Barcelona
- See: https://altpick.com/news/bill-frakes-on-his-favorite-sports-photography-moment
- Structure: about ten remote cameras by 7 a.m., some on rails and magic arms, one F3 with a 180 mm f/2.8 on a floor plate, all wired to a foot switch. He logged the light all week. He also tracked Lewis on a 300 mm f/2 and judged that frame too tight. Kluetmeier picked Frakes's frame, which ran over two pages (which camera made it is not stated).
- Why it works: fixed frames pre-composed for light and depth of field (`photo-craft`, `light`). The "too tight" note is a crop lesson (`framing-cropping`).
- Push move: scout the light by clock time, pre-set loose and tight options, choose after. Keep a loose master so the tight crop exists later.
- Evidence: DOC (Frakes). Derby method (41 DSLRs per the headline, positions revisited through the 12 hours before the race) is from a PetaPixel interview seen through a search summary; the page was blocked.

### SP-12 "The Shadow", hockey warm-up · Bruce Bennett, Getty Images · shot before Jan 2025 · World Sports Photography Awards 2025, Ice Hockey gold
- See: https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/sports/world-sports-photography-awards-2025-in-pictures/12324469 · https://newsroom.gettyimages.com/getty-images/getty-images-photographers-recognized-at-2025-world-sports-photography-awards
- Structure: the caption says a Vegas player warms up before a game against the Islanders. The title points to a shadow as the subject. I did not see the image and no source describes its layout `[Unverified]`. Bennett also took special merit for "Score" and "Flip". His interview notes NHL glass has 4x5 in. camera openings and that Getty gives him room for creative angles.
- Why it works: a warm-up is not play, so the picture has to come from light and shape (`light`, `negative-space`). This is the closest award frame to warm-up and media-day material.
- Push move: in warm-ups, hunt for a light shape or shadow on the ice and make it the subject. Shoot the frame the game coverage cannot.
- Evidence: RETRO `[Unverified]`. Caption and award are DOC; the reading is a lead, not a finding.

### SP-13 Biles on the beam, black background · Loic Venance, AFP · 2024 Paris
- See: https://www.lanacion.com.py/mundo/2024/08/11/los-secretos-de-las-fotos-mas-iconicas-en-paris-2024/
- Structure: he looks for clean gym frames with a black background and no screens or logos. He arrived two hours early, took a three-quarter angle with the beam squarely visible and dark behind it, and crouched near floor level.
- Why it works: value contrast at maximum on the subject (`value-contrast`), no competing marks (`clutter-peripheral`), low angle for stature (`sports-action`).
- Push move: choose the angle where the background goes black, and arrive early to own it. In post, crush and mask the backdrop, remove sponsor hits.
- Evidence: DOC (Venance, via La Nacion Paraguay, Spanish) `[Practice]`.

### SP-14 Duplantis with the Olympic rings · Ben Stansall · 2024 Paris
- See: https://www.lanacion.com.py/mundo/2024/08/11/los-secretos-de-las-fotos-mas-iconicas-en-paris-2024/
- Structure: shot from the far side of the stadium on a 400 mm lens, apart from other photographers, so the rings stand out behind the vaulter. Agency not stated in the source `[Unverified]`.
- Why it works: distance and long lens stack a symbol behind the athlete at a size that reads as a second level (`depth-space`, `scale`, `hierarchy-focal-point`).
- Push move: stack an owned symbol (arena banner, crest, trophy) behind the athlete using a long, compressed frame. Same move works as a crop of a high-resolution frame.
- Evidence: DOC (secondary report) `[Practice]`; image not viewed.

### SP-15 "The Blue Dunk" · Walter Iooss Jr. · 1987 · Lisle, Illinois
- See: https://vault.si.com/vault/2013/02/18/through-the-lens · https://petapixel.com/2012/12/26/the-story-behind-an-iconic-photograph-of-michael-jordan-in-flight
- Structure: asphalt painted half red, half blue so the ground would suit whichever jersey Jordan wore; he came in red, so blue ground. Cherry-picker high angle, low sun for a hard shadow, a real hoop trucked in and lowered so Jordan jumped at less than full effort. Jordan seems to play against his own shadow. (Details come from search summaries of SI and PetaPixel; those pages were not opened.)
- Why it works: figure and ground are opposed colours (`color`). The cast shadow is a second figure (`gestalt`, `light`). The high angle flattens depth into a graphic (`depth-space`).
- Push move: make the ground a colour field chosen for the jersey, and give the subject a cast shadow. In post on ice frames: tone the ice to one cool hue, add a contact and cast shadow.
- Evidence: DOC (Iooss, secondary) `[Practice]`.

### SP-16 Jordan's ballet leap · Jacobus Rentmeester, Life · 1984 · Chapel Hill
- See: https://life.com/arts-entertainment/the-amazing-story-behind-jumpman
- Structure: outdoors on a hillside with a toy basket; Jordan asked for a ballet-style vertical leap with legs split rather than a basketball jump. Reports describe a clear blue sky behind him. Nike's Jumpman logo is alleged to derive from it; courts sided with Nike, so treat that link as contested.
- Why it works: a pose built for silhouette, which survives at tiny size (`shape`, `clutter-peripheral` thumbnail test), on a clean sky (`negative-space`, `value-contrast`).
- Push move: ask for the pose that reads as a shape at thumbnail size. Run the blur-to-20-px test on every hero crop.
- Evidence: DOC (Life; lawsuit-era claims) `[Practice]`.

### SP-17 Olympic Portraits · Annie Leibovitz · 1996 (shot 1993-96) · book
- See: https://www.lensculture.com/books/2939-olympic-portraits · https://www.csmonitor.com/1996/1205/120596.feat.books.3.html
- Structure: black-and-white, mostly posed. She shot athletes in training, away from Atlanta crowds, to focus on the athletes (her introduction, via a review). A reviewer notes rich tonal values and strong positive and negative space, and an unusual artistic quality in the action frames.
- Why it works: isolation from the event removes clutter and logos (`clutter-peripheral`). Black and white forces structure to live in value (`value-contrast`). Space is shaped on purpose (`negative-space`, `balance`).
- Push move: for a portrait, remove the event. Try black and white as a structure test: if the grayscale fails, the colour will not save it.
- Evidence: DOC (review of the book) `[Practice]`. Lighting and gear not found.

### SP-19 Media-day sets · Erik Isakson (Clippers) and Robert Foldy (basketball portraits) · years not stated

## What separates exceptional from solid

1. One subject wins, and everything else is stripped or dropped. Ads, screens, logos and clutter are removed in camera or in grade. SP-01, SP-13, SP-09, SP-18. Mechanisms: `hierarchy-focal-point`, `clutter-peripheral`, `value-contrast` `[E-strong]`.
2. The frame was built before the moment arrived. Remote cameras, scouted light, a chosen end of the rink, a painted ground. SP-02, SP-05, SP-08, SP-11, SP-15, SP-13, SP-03. Photo-craft `[Practice]`.
3. The beat after the play, with a readable face. Celebration, reaction, the grin. SP-03, SP-04, SP-10, SP-06. Faces and eyes pull attention `[E-strong]` (`attention-saliency`). John Biever's stated test is that he wants to see the athlete's face (CBS interview, via search summary).
4. The scene is one shape. Gesture, equipment and horizon align; a silhouette survives at thumbnail size. SP-09, SP-16, SP-15, SP-02. `gestalt`, `shape`.
5. Width is chosen by story. Wide when the crowd's gaze is the subject, tight when a face carries it, and never so tight that no crop remains. SP-06, SP-10, SP-11. `framing-cropping`, `line-direction`.
6. The background does a job: clean black or a colour field, or a compressed symbol that adds a second level. SP-13, SP-15, SP-14, SP-07. `depth-space`, `color`.
7. Key art treats image and type as a planned split, and removes type when the story is known. SP-22, SP-21, SP-04, SP-25. `typography`, `negative-space`, `accessibility-legibility`.
8. Restraint and system. One idea per piece, then a kit that survives the season. SP-20, SP-23, SP-24, SP-19. `unity-variety`.

Caution from Leifer: part of any great sports frame is the event. Do not credit composition for what is the story (SP-01).

## Solid-but-not-exceptional traps

1. Sponsor board behind the torso at equal sharpness, with letters bigger than the head. Fix: crop so the board band exits the frame; or mask-darken it by one to two stops and drop saturation; or blur it to match the crowd; in graphics, lay a dark gradient plate over the board strip and put type on it. (`clutter-peripheral`, SP-13)
2. Crowd behind the glass as bright and sharp as the subject. Fix: subject mask, then pull crowd value down, desaturate, add depth-matched blur and a vignette; check at thumbnail size. Do not blur the ice at the subject's feet. (`value-contrast`, SP-07, SP-13)
3. Correct but no beat: standing, waiting, stick on ice, no contact, no reaction. Fix: choose the extension, contact or reaction frame from the burst; accept a worse sharpness for a better moment. (`sports-action`, SP-03, SP-10)
4. Face hidden or tiny: visor shade, back of the athlete, goalie mask, an opponent's back filling the frame. Fix: choose another frame; crop so the face is at least a set share of the height (set per job); lift the visor shadow locally. (`attention-saliency`, SP-10)
5. Subject faces out of the frame, or is dead center with no lead room. Fix: recrop to add room ahead of the motion. Do not mirror the photo: jersey text and numbers flip. (`sports-action`, `spatial-composition` inward bias `[E-strong]`)
6. Edge tangents and fragments: a teammate or stick cut at the border, a stray blade in a corner, bench heads touching the top. Fix: recrop past them or clone them out; or lean in and make the second figure a full foreground frame. (`framing-cropping`)
7. Type fights the busiest area, or sits over the face. Fix: reserve a block before cropping; put type on quiet ground (flat ice, dark crowd) or on a plate; keep contrast at or above 4.5:1 for body-size text and clear the eyes. (`typography`, `accessibility-legibility`, SP-22)
8. Cut-out halos and fake falloff. Black jersey on bright ice shows a white fringe; blur that ignores depth looks pasted. Fix: refine the edge on the dark side, defringe, keep a contact shadow, and blur by distance. (`depth-space`, SP-10, SP-15)

## Push move candidates

| Name | When | How | Rung | Exemplars |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strip the stage | Boards, ads or screens behind the athlete | Crop them out, or mask-darken, desaturate and blur; remove marks | FIX | SP-01, SP-13, SP-18 |
