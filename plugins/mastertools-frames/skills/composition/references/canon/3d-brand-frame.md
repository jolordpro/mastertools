# Canon: 3D brand frames in broadcast sports

## Quick answer
- A 3D brand frame is one image that has to carry the mark, the sport, the network voice and room for live graphics. It has to read at thumbnail size and survive a team swap.
- The best ones in this canon do five things: they resolve into the mark with one light event, keep the value plan to two or three masses, make the surface tell the sport, stage depth behind the mark, and leave planned room for type and the score bug.
- Almost every exemplar below is also a kit. The hero frame is the proof of a system, not the product.
- Use `value-contrast`, `light`, `hierarchy-focal-point`, `depth-space`, `negative-space` and `broadcast-standards` as the checking facets.

## How to read this file
- Evidence markers follow `01-evidence-legend`. `DOC` means a maker, studio or primary record says it. `RETRO` means this file's own reading of stills, tagged `[Infer]`.
- "Frames viewed" in a See line means I looked at stills on that page in a browser pane (viewed, not saved by me). If it says "not viewed", the Structure line comes from maker text only and is weaker.
- BF-21 to BF-29 are the user's own reference frames. The folder does not record studio or year for them, so I do not claim any. Their analysis is `RETRO [Infer]`, backed by simple pixel measurements I ran (mean luminance, centre vs edge saturation) on the 640 px frames.
- Window: last ~10 years. BF-19 (2015) and BF-20 (2014) sit slightly outside it and are marked as anchors.

### BF-01 ESPN Euro Cup 2016 open · Imaginary Forces with ESPN · 2016 · PromaxBDA Gold, Motion Award (Broadcast > Sports Graphics), Sports Emmy nominee
- See: https://www.behance.net/gallery/73314799/ESPN-Euro-Cup-2016-Open (frames viewed: statue stills, pose sheets, logo cube) · https://motionographer.com/2016/06/23/making-the-espn-euro-cup-2016-broadcast-package/ · https://2016.motionawards.com/winner/broadcast-sports-graphics/
- Structure: Deep navy field cut with fine gold map lines; gilded player statues in extreme close crops, so gold is the only bright mass. Warm gold against cool navy is the whole colour plan. The finish flips value: the ornate gilded EURO logo sits in a glass cube over a bright Paris skyline.
- Why it works: The director says the concept was statues contained inside the ornament of the tournament logo, so the hero object and the mark are one thing. One material, one hue pair, and a dark body that makes the lit finish the biggest light event. `value-contrast`, `hierarchy-focal-point`, `texture-detail`, `color`.
- Push move: Hero object lives inside the mark.
- Evidence: DOC (director's Behance text; Motion Awards page; awards line on Behance) · RETRO `[Infer]` for the value flip.

## What separates exceptional from solid

Ranked. Each item names exemplars and the facets behind it.

1. **The logo arrives as one light event, and it is the only high-contrast junction.** In the best frames the body of the open stays in a narrow value band and a single beam, rim or flip of key brings the mark to full brightness and chroma. BF-01 flips from dark gold to a lit finish; BF-02 puts the light behind the shield; BF-29 keeps the whole frame low key (95th percentile 84 of 255) so the badge is the only light; BF-25 makes the dark wordmark the one junction on pale mesh. Solid work lights everything about equally. `light`, `value-contrast`, `hierarchy-focal-point`, `attention-saliency`.
2. **A value plan of two or three masses that survives a thumbnail.** BF-27 (white, red, white), BF-28 (dark rock, two bright marks), BF-23 (dark puck on pale ice). Squint at each: the mark and its main support survive. `value-contrast`, `clutter-peripheral`.
3. **The surface tells the sport, and the logo shares it.** Leather (BF-22), jersey mesh (BF-25), ice (BF-08, BF-23), pigskin on logo extrusions (BF-10), scanned fabric (BF-20), marble and gold (BF-12), place-specific linework (BF-11). The mark sits in the same material world as the field. `texture-detail`, `unity-variety`.
4. **A colour budget: restrained field, one accent on the mark.** BF-07 (venom over mono), BF-21 (colour only in the badge), BF-29 (lime on navy), BF-13 (dark with accents tuned to team and car colours). The counter-case is BF-26, where saturation everywhere leaves no room for the mark to win by hue. `color`, `hierarchy-focal-point`.
5. **Depth staged behind the mark.** Four readable planes: foreground occluder or particles, the mark, a set piece, a hazy deep field. BF-03 (tunnel to vanishing point), BF-02 (horizon silhouette), BF-28 (strata faces and gap), BF-16 (skyline through glass). `depth-space`, `line-direction`.
6. **Planned negative space for type, bug and lower thirds.** BF-02 leaves most of the frame as sky; BF-25's mesh frame is almost empty around a small wordmark; BF-12 sets athlete imagery priority because helmets hide faces, so the layout was designed around live use. Compose the hero frame with the title and bug already on it. `negative-space`, `broadcast-standards`, `accessibility-legibility`.
7. **The sport supplies the geometry.** Home plate as portal (BF-17), bases as a layout (BF-27), a split frame for a matchup (BF-19, BF-28), track outlines as a tunnel (BF-03), spheres as the unit (BF-05). The shape is not decoration; it organises the frame. `shape`, `geometry-proportion`, `gestalt`.
8. **The frame is a system.** Logo parity (BF-06), formula-driven kits (BF-19), 45 swappable elements (BF-17), film built as a kit (BF-09), constant framing with changing material (BF-12), and one wall with multiple states (BF-15). The hero frame is the best member of a family that holds up when a team changes. `unity-variety`, `rhythm-pattern`.

## Solid-but-not-exceptional traps

1. **Logo floating on a pretty, unrelated scene.** Fix: make light, shadow and material connect them. Put the mark in the same lighting set as the field, and share a surface (BF-22, BF-10, BF-20). `light`, `texture-detail`.
2. **Mid-key mush: everything lit about equally.** Fix: write the 2 to 4 value masses before lighting; keep the field in a narrow band and let the mark carry the extreme (BF-29, BF-28). Check at thumbnail and in grayscale. `value-contrast`.
3. **Chrome soup: shiny surfaces with nothing worth reflecting.** Fix: plan what the metal sees (a bright card, a sweep, a skyline as in BF-16) and use rim light on the edge, as in the chrome rim of BF-21. `light`, `texture-detail`.
4. **Detail competes with the mark.** Fix: set a detail budget; keep the busiest texture away from the mark or tone it down (the tone-on-tone relief in BF-29, the soft mesh in BF-25). `texture-detail`, `clutter-peripheral`.
5. **Centred and static with no depth cue.** Fix: add an occluder, a scale figure, a horizon or converging lines (BF-02, BF-03). `depth-space`, `scale`.
6. **Every colour at full strength.** Fix: pick the accent and mute the rest (BF-07, BF-21). BF-26 shows what the lack costs. `color`.
7. **No planned room for type and the bug.** Fix: build the hero frame with the real title, bug and lower third on it; hold the title-safe area (90%) and keep the calm zone where the overlay lands (BF-02, BF-25). `negative-space`, `broadcast-standards`.
8. **The hero frame works for one team only.** Fix: test the system on the worst logo (long, thin, multi-colour, dark) and set a size rule, as BF-06 did with medallions. `unity-variety`, `scale`.
9. **The idea is a literal prop (ball plus logo).** Fix: use the sport as geometry or material instead of a picture of it (BF-17, BF-27, BF-12). `shape`.
