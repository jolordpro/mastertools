# Canon: posters and social vertical

Job: one frame that wins in a second, at phone size, in a feed or a Story. Library ids in backticks are the mechanisms.

## How to read this file
- **DOC** = the fact (credit, date, process, number) is on the page I opened. **RETRO** = my reading of the composition, not stated by the source.
- I could not save or inspect images. Structure lines for the classic works come from the widely reproduced images plus the page text. Where a detail is shaky it says `[Unverified]`.
- Search budget ran out mid-task. Not found or not confirmed: Adweek / It's Nice That coverage of team gameday graphics, a Mondo sports poster, any F1 team case study, any MLB or NHL graphic with a credited designer, Dribbble/Behance sports case studies with text. These are gaps, not claims. Sports social is the thinnest part of the canon below. Treat PS-19 to PS-23 as documented practice, not as images I verified.
- Safe-zone numbers: see "Phone-size and safe zones". Sources disagree. Default recorded there.

## Exemplars

### PS-01 Anatomy of a Murder poster · Saul Bass · 1959 · MoMA collection (offset lithograph, 41 x 27 in)
- See: https://www.moma.org/collection/works/4824
- Structure: One idea (the victim as a cut-paper body). Silhouette of jagged dark pieces on a flat orange ground; hand-cut type matches the figure's edge. Almost no value steps: dark, orange, white. Reads as one shape at any size.
- Why it works: A single bold shape carries the whole story, so gist survives shrinking (`clutter-peripheral`, `shape`). Flat ground and a rough cut edge give figure-ground at a glance (`gestalt`). Type is made from the same cut language as the image, so image and title read as one object (`typography`, `unity-variety`).
- Push move: Build the subject as one silhouette with a "cut" edge and make the title from the same material.
- Evidence: DOC (MoMA record: year, size, gift of Otto Preminger Productions and United Artists). RETRO (structure). A blog also describes seven dark shapes and rough edges (weak source). `[Practice]`

### PS-02 Vertigo poster · Saul Bass · 1958 · Sotheby's London sale article, 2023
- See: https://sothebys.com/en/articles/the-deadly-design-of-saul-basss-vertigo
- Structure: A spiral as the one device, small falling figures set against it, limited palette (sources describe an orange ground). The spiral also ran in the title sequence, so poster and titles share one motif. Figure placement was varied by poster size across the US campaign.
- Why it works: The spiral turns the film's theme into a shape; the eye is pulled to the centre and the figures give scale (`hierarchy-focal-point`, `scale`). Repeating the motif across poster and titles builds recognition (`rhythm-pattern`, `designers`).
- Push move: Pick the film's (or game's) one abstract motif and reuse it across every format so each format is a crop of the same idea.
- Evidence: DOC (Sotheby's: aim to beat 1950s photo-and-scene posters, poster-to-title link, size-specific placement). One auction listing says John Whitney Sr. made the spiral art; other sources credit Bass alone: `[Unverified]`. `[Practice]`

### PS-03 Beethoven (Tonhalle Zurich) · Josef Muller-Brockmann · 1955 · Cooper Hewitt, Museum fur Gestaltung Zurich
- See: https://collection.cooperhewitt.org/objects/2318803418 (redirects to the Smithsonian collection landing; also https://www.emuseum.ch/en/objects/167580/beethoven-55/related/67, which blocked my fetch)
- Structure: Concentric black-and-white arcs cropped by the edge, read as sound waves. Type is small, grid-set, in a corner. Large empty field. 127.6 x 91 cm lithograph.
- Why it works: Geometry stands in for the music, so there is no figure to compete with; scale contrast between huge arcs and tiny type fixes the hierarchy (`scale`, `grids-layout`, `negative-space`).
- Push move: Let one abstract geometric field be the image and shrink the type until it is clearly secondary.
- Evidence: DOC (Cooper Hewitt: 1955, 127.6 x 91 cm, concert date 22 Feb 1955, printer, gift of Merrill C. Berman 2019). RETRO (structure from the widely reproduced image; I could not open the museum description). `[Practice]` The "Der Film" (1960) poster was not confirmed and is not used.

### PS-04 Tokyo 1964 poster 1, "The Rising Sun and the Olympic Emblem" · Yusaku Kamekura · 1961 · Olympic Poster Museum page; Milan Poster Design Award for the series
- See: https://olympic-museum.de/poster/olympic-games-poster-1964.php
- Structure: A red disc, the rings, minimal type. One symbol, large; the ground is almost all quiet space (`[Unverified]` on exact colours and type placement).
- Why it works: A flag shape is already known, so recognition is instant; the rest is space (`negative-space`, `clutter-peripheral`). Strong value and hue separation, one focal mass (`value-contrast`).
- Push move: When the audience already knows a symbol (club crest, sun, ring), use it as the poster and spend nothing else.
- Evidence: DOC (four-poster series, 100,000 copies of poster 1, photogravure, prizes). RETRO (structure). `[Practice]`

### PS-05 Tokyo 1964 poster 2, "The Start of Sprinters' Dash" · Kamekura with photographers Osamu Hayasaki and Jo Murakoshi · 1962 · British Museum record; Olympic Poster Museum page
- See: https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/A_2009-3026-2 (my fetch was blocked; facts via the Olympic Poster Museum page) and https://olympic-museum.de/poster/olympic-games-poster-1964.php
- Structure: A single high-contrast photograph of runners at the start, shot at night in the National Stadium in February 1962. Photograph fills the sheet, type small. Two kinds of poster in one series: symbol (PS-04) and photograph (this one).
- Why it works: The photographed moment has tension (the instant before the start) and an obvious direction (`sports-action`, `line-direction`). A night shoot gave controlled light and a clean ground (`light`, `clutter-peripheral`).
- Push move: In a set, make one frame a symbol and one a single decisive-moment photo. Do not mix them in one frame.
- Evidence: DOC (night shoot, models, sizes, copies 90,000). RETRO (structure). `[Practice]`

### PS-06 Mexico 68 Olympic poster · Lance Wyman (credit shared with Eduardo Terrazas and Pedro Ramirez Vazquez in museum records) · 1968 · V&A article
- See: https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/iconic-design-official-poster-for-the-mexico-1968-olympic-games
- Structure: Olympic rings become "68", joined to "Mexico" by parallel lines that run out to all four edges of a square. Black-and-white and several colour versions. Op-art lines plus pre-Hispanic and Huichol pattern.
- Why it works: Type is the image and the lines fill the frame to the edge, so there is no weak corner (`typography`, `line-direction`, `rhythm-pattern`). The same device became the identity, so every format is a crop (`unity-variety`).
- Push move: Make the logotype's own line system run edge to edge so any crop (9:16, 4:5, 1:1) still contains the idea.
- Evidence: DOC (V&A: construction, year 1968, logotype 1966). Attribution is contested across museums: `[Unverified]` on who gets credit. `[Practice]`

### PS-07 Munich 1972 sports posters · Otl Aicher (team; silkscreens by Gerhard Joksch and others per Poster House) · 1970-72 · Olympic Poster Museum page, Poster House
- See: https://olympic-museum.de/poster/olympic-games-poster-1972.php and https://access.posterhouse.org/?p=2236 (the Poster House URL failed on my later fetch; facts came from the search result snippet)
- Structure: A series, not a one-off. Each poster has one theme colour from the Games spectrum plus two to four more tones; left-to-right motion, horizontal lines, cut-out forms bleeding off the page. Photographs simplified in form and colour.
- Why it works: Signal-like colours replace the usual colour for each sport, so a seen-it-a-thousand-times scene catches the eye again (`color`, `fluency-complexity`). A shared grid and direction make the set read as one system (`grids-layout`, `unity-variety`, `culture-direction`).
- Push move: Give each game in a season its own signal colour and keep direction, crop rules and type spot fixed.
- Evidence: DOC (21 or 22 posters depending on the page, 950,000 copies, theme-colour system, "picture language" aim). RETRO (reading). `[Practice]`

### PS-08 World Cup 1970 (Mexico) poster · designer not named on the source page · 1970 · Creative Review
- See: https://www.creativereview.co.uk/a-world-cup-history-in-posters/
- Structure: Double in-line type, bold candy-bright colour; the type nods to the Mexico 68 poster (PS-06).
- Why it works: Display type in a tight colour set is the image; a shared visual family with the earlier Games lets people link the two (`typography`, `color`, `unity-variety`).
- Push move: Treat the tournament name as a graphic object with an outline, a fill and a second colour. No photo needed.
- Evidence: DOC (Creative Review calls it among the most iconic; links it to the 1968 poster). RETRO (mechanism). `[Practice]`

### PS-09 World Cup 2018 (Russia) poster · Igor Gurovich · 2018 · Creative Review
- See: https://www.creativereview.co.uk/a-world-cup-history-in-posters/
- Structure: Centred portrait of Soviet goalkeeper Lev Yashin, Constructivist angles, rays of light coming off the ball.
- Why it works: A centred figure with radiating lines pulls the eye in and works at any size (`hierarchy-focal-point`, `line-direction`, `balance`). The reference to a style (Constructivism) gives it a point of view without extra elements.
- Push move: Use one human face as the anchor and let diagonal rays do the energy work.
- Evidence: DOC (designer, subject, style). RETRO (mechanism). `[Practice]`

### PS-10 Star Wars original trilogy prints · Olly Moss (Mondo) · 2010 · Slashfilm; Mondo edition of 400 each, 24 x 36 in
- See: https://slashfilm.com/cool-stuff-olly-moss-star-wars-trilogy-posters-mondo
- Structure: Coloured character silhouettes (C-3PO, Boba Fett, Darth Vader are named by press) filled with imagery from each film. Small details sit in the negative space (press cites C-3PO's eyes as the Tatooine suns, the gap by an arm as a Star Destroyer).
- Why it works: A silhouette gives the big read; the filled content and hidden shapes give a second read on a closer look (`shape`, `negative-space`, `gestalt` figure-ground reversal). A prize for looking again.
- Push move: Plan two reads: a one-second silhouette, then a hidden detail in the gaps. Fits a Story that people pause on.
- Evidence: DOC (set, size, edition, Moss's rewatch-with-sketchbook method). The page does not say which hidden detail belongs to which print: `[Unverified]` on per-poster attribution. `[Practice]`

### PS-11 Funny Games poster · Akiko Stehrenberger · 2008 (US release; year `[Unverified]` on the page) · MUBI Notebook interview
- See: https://mubi.com/notebook/posts/1397
- Structure: One unsettling image built from a tiny DVD screenshot, noise added until it looked photographic. Sparse type, placed in a Muller-Brockmann spirit. She resisted adding a gloved hand, blood or a second poster.
- Why it works: Nothing competes with the image; roughness (grain) adds a quality of found footage and dread (`texture-detail`, `negative-space`). Subtracting is the push (`hierarchy-focal-point`, `rule-breaking`).
- Push move: When the client asks for one more element, test the frame with it removed. Add grain to a low-res source on purpose.
- Evidence: DOC (her account of process and client notes). `[Practice]`

### PS-12 Se7en main titles · Kyle Cooper (Angus Wall edited; R/Greenberg Associates) · 1995 · Art of the Title
- See: https://www.artofthetitle.com/title/se7en/
- Structure: Type scratched into scratchboard, shot on film, smeared and jittered. Tabletop close-ups of a diary. Mixed hand lettering and Helvetica. The article notes it breaks modern legibility habits.
- Why it works: Type is made by the story's character, so the letterform is the tone (`typography`, `texture-detail`). For poster and Story work, a single hand-made treatment on one word can carry a whole brand (`accessibility-legibility` sets the limit: keep the key word big enough).
- Push move: Make the one headline word physically (scratch, smear, burn), then keep the supporting text clean.
- Evidence: DOC (Art of the Title: process, credits, year). Scale and contrast of the title card were not stated. `[Practice]`

### PS-13 Criterion Collection covers (e.g. Paris, Texas: one photo of the lot sign) · Criterion art team (Eric Skillman, Sarah Habibi, others) · cover practice, article 2015 · The Arts Desk
- See: https://theartsdesk.com/we-made-it/we-made-it-designing-dvd-covers
- Structure: Each release gets its own look, not a template. Often one strong image, sometimes two elements (Quadrophenia: scooter photo plus the target graphic). Images are built digitally from stills or screen grabs with a handmade element.
- Why it works: A cover must stay honest to the film, and a single chosen image beats a cast montage (`hierarchy-focal-point`, `clutter-peripheral`). A distinct treatment per title keeps a thumbnail grid readable by variety (`unity-variety`).
- Push move: Pick the odd, specific object (a sign, a ticket, a stat card) over the star portrait.
- Evidence: DOC (process and principle statements, 2015). RETRO (mechanism). `[Practice]`

### PS-14 Bring in 'da Noise, Bring in 'da Funk poster · Paula Scher / Pentagram (Public Theater) · 1995 · Typeroom
- See: https://www.typeroom.eu/war-lust-type-paula-scher-the-public-theater-pentagram
- Structure: The title and logos wrap the dancer in wood type; flat colour; silhouetted photo. MoMA's description (as quoted by the article) stresses odd spacing, mixed weights and colours, historic faces.
- Why it works: Type is the image and the layout has rhythm, so it reads as sound (`typography`, `rhythm-pattern`, `scale`). It breaks the Swiss grid on purpose (`grids-layout`, `rule-breaking`).
- Push move: Let the headline wrap the athlete so type and body make one shape.
- Evidence: DOC (year, subject, MoMA quote via article). The article gives no type scale or palette: `[Unverified]`. `[Practice]`

### PS-15 Milano Cortina 2026 Olympic poster · Olimpia Zagnoli · 2025 · CTV News (AP wire)
- See: https://www.ctvnews.ca/sports/article/milan-cortina-2026-unveils-official-posters-for-winter-olympic-and-paralympic-games/
- Structure: A central figure looks through glasses shaped like the rings, against white mountain caps; bright flat colour. Companion Paralympic poster by Carolina Altavilla (six sports, Duomo foreground). Part of a Triennale Milano collaboration, curated by Damiano Gulli.
- Why it works: One gag reads in a second and puts the rings in an unexpected place (`hierarchy-focal-point`, `shape`). Flat colour keeps it clean at thumbnail size (`value-contrast`, `clutter-peripheral`).
- Push move: Hide the brand mark inside a character's prop so the logo is part of the story.
- Evidence: DOC (artists, curator, description by AP). Composition detail from wire text only; I did not see the image. `[Practice]`

### PS-16 Paris 2024 Olympic and Paralympic posters (diptych) · Ugo Gattoni · 2024 · Creative Review
- See: https://www.creativereview.co.uk/paris-2024-olympic-paralympic-official-posters-ugo-gattoni/
- Structure: Two vertical posters that join into a horizontal diptych; a hand-drawn, "Where's Wally?" style fantasy Paris with every event; 2,000+ hours. Gattoni said composition was the hardest part.
- Why it works as a limit case: Density rewards a poster you stand in front of, and it fails at phone size (`clutter-peripheral`, `fluency-complexity`). It has landmarks as anchors but no single entry point (RETRO).
- Push move: Use it as a contrast case. If the piece must also live as a Story cover, build a one-shape version for the small size and keep the dense one for the zoom.
- Evidence: DOC (diptych, hours, process, date 5 Mar 2024). RETRO (hierarchy judgement; the article does not assess it). `[Practice]`

### PS-17 Super Bowl LX theme art, 59 letterpress prints · Erin Fong · Feb 2026 · 7x7
- See: https://www.7x7.com/super-bowl-artist-erin-fong-2675100159.html
- Structure: Each print is built on a champion's quote, set in hand-set wood type (some 1800s) on a 1960s Vandercook press, over bright gradient grounds. Used in programs and digital tickets.
- Why it works: Words as the image, with real print texture, stand apart from photo-and-cutout feeds (`typography`, `texture-detail`, `color`).
- Push move: Make the quote or the stat the whole frame; set it in a material the feed is not full of.
- Evidence: DOC (7x7, 5 Feb 2026). Per-print composition not described; layered and gradient only. `[Practice]`

### PS-18 Seahawks gameday poster series · Ames Bros (curator) with local artists, first by Preston Singletary · 2019 · seahawks.com
- See: https://www.seahawks.com/news/seahawks-launch-new-gameday-poster-series-featuring-designs-from-local-artists
- Structure: Eight 18 x 24 in screen prints, one per home game; opponent and date plus a one-off artist design; unveiled the Monday before each game; platinum run of 12.
- Why it works: A fixed information block (opponent, date) with a free art area gives a set that is consistent and varied (`unity-variety`, `grids-layout`). The weekly unveil gives a rhythm (`rhythm-pattern`).
- Push move: Lock the data strip; hand the art area to a different voice each week.
- Evidence: DOC (article dated 3 Sep 2019). The article does not mention social use. `[Practice]`

### PS-19 NBA social "digital posters" · NBA in-house design team (credited: Ivan Dashkov, Justin Budish, Kevin Esteves, Jim Poorten, Evan Jacoby, Sam Farber, Melissa Rosenthal Brenner) · 2015 season · Shorty Awards entry
- See: https://shortyawards.com/8th/nba-social-graphics
- Structure: Digital versions of oversized player posters, in varied styles (software, hand drawing, watercolour); pre-planned for major events and made in real time for moments. Entry reports 120M+ interactions; top post, the Warriors' first Finals berth since 1975, 600K+ Facebook interactions. Fans used them as wallpapers.
- Why it works: Each graphic is one moment with one hero; variety of style keeps the feed from going stale (`hierarchy-focal-point`, `unity-variety`). Wallpaper use means a phone-shaped, centred layout.
- Push move: Treat the graphic as something a fan would keep as a phone wallpaper. That forces one subject and clean space.
- Evidence: DOC (self-reported figures in an award entry; no independent check). `[Practice]` Numbers are `[Unverified]` beyond the entry.

### PS-20 2022 NBA All-Star announcement posters · NBA Social and Digital Content Design team · 2022 · Hashtag Sports Awards shortlist
- See: https://www.hashtagsports.com/awards/shortlist-2022/2022-nba-all-star-announcement
- Structure: Event-poster style per selected player; three stars for the three previous Cleveland All-Star events, "The Land" text, a nod to a poster-wall campaign. Made to work online and printed in hallways at a fan event. Reports 70M+ impressions.
- Why it works: Local meaning (three stars, nickname) gives a symbol people can read at a glance (`hierarchy-focal-point`, `shape`). One layout across 24 players is a system (`unity-variety`, `grids-layout`).
- Push move: One local symbol repeated in every poster in a set.
- Evidence: DOC (entry text; impressions self-reported). `[Practice]`

### PS-21 Chicago Bulls content design · Jon Shoemaker, Jimmy Mitchell, Jeff Pitcock, Dave Zarzynski, Jamie Beyer (named in entry) · 2022 · Hashtag Sports Awards shortlist
- See: https://www.hashtagsports.com/awards/shortlist-2022/bulls-graphics
- Structure: Templates and style sheets for fast game-day graphics, plus intricate one-offs with small surprise details. Bold type around a stat or achievement. Reported: two DeRozan graphics at 650k and 475k likes; four of the ten most-liked posts that season; entry says Mercedes-AMG F1, ESPN and Team USA made similar graphics.
- Why it works: A strict system saves time for the hero piece, and the hero piece gets the extra detail (`unity-variety`, `typography`, `hierarchy-focal-point`).
- Push move: Run two tiers: a locked template for volume, a hand-built one-off for the player or moment that matters.
- Evidence: DOC (entry; engagement and "inspired others" claims are self-reported). `[Practice]` I did not see the graphics.

### PS-22 "Whole Squad Ready" WNBA season graphic · Dimes Studio (Jesse Wu) for D!srupt The Game · 2023 · Hashtag Sports Awards shortlist

### PS-23 Minnesota Vikings game-day Stories and graphics · Vikings creative and design team · 2018 · Adobe Express case study
- See: https://new.express.adobe.com/page/Tj2zOLu1tGFOQ/
- Structure: Stories as the main game-day channel; pre-game matchup graphic with one player from each team; score graphics; a five-part carousel after big wins; "two-tap" method (big play first, detail graphic second); under 90 seconds from play to post; watermark on every photo.
- Why it works: Splitting the moment from the explanation keeps each frame to one idea (`hierarchy-focal-point`, `motion-principles` for the beat). The photo carries the story, the graphic adds the fact.
- Push move: Make a two-frame Story pair: frame 1 is the image alone; frame 2 is one number and one line.
- Evidence: DOC (vendor case study, so promotional). The page does not state aspect ratio. `[Practice]`

### PS-24 FIFA World Cup 26 host-city posters · 16 local artists, one per city (Cuemanche made all three Mexican cities) · March-April 2025 · Print Magazine, Sports Travel Magazine
- See: https://www.printmag.com/poster-design/2026-fifa-world-cup-posters (my fetch was blocked) and https://www.sportstravelmagazine.com/2026-fifa-world-cup-posters-revealed/ and https://www.soccerbible.com/news/2025/04/fifa-reveal-world-cup-2026-official-host-city-posters/amp/

- Why it works: One brief with many local voices gives variety under a fixed job (`unity-variety`). The unveil schedule turns a set into a feed rhythm (`rhythm-pattern`).
- Push move: Fix the job and the data block; let each city's artist own the picture.
- Evidence: DOC (dates, artist list, selection numbers from press). No per-poster composition checked. `[Practice]`

### PS-25 Netflix title artwork selection · Netflix (Basilico QCon 2018 talk; Chandrashekar, Amat, Basilico, Jebara blog Dec 2017 via Dezeen) · 2017-18
- See: https://archive.qconsf.com/node/110208 and https://www.dezeen.com/2017/12/20/netflix-targets-film-artwork-depending-users-viewing-habits-design-technology/
- Structure: Artwork is a title's first and sometimes only contact with a viewer. Netflix picks one image per member per title and tests variants (viewers of romance see the couple; viewers of comedy see the funny lead). The talk gives no artwork-specific figures.
- Why it works: The image shows why this title suits this viewer, and it is one subject with a clear emotion (`hierarchy-focal-point`, `attention-saliency`).
- Push move: Make 3 crops for 3 audiences (the star, the rival, the moment) and test them against each other.
- Evidence: DOC (company-reported tests, not independently replicated). Widely repeated figures (82% of browsing focus on art, 1.8 seconds) did not appear on pages I opened: `[Unverified]`. `[Practice]`

### Conservative default (1080 x 1920)
- Hard safe box for anything that must be read (faces, headline, score, logo): **x 120 to 888, y 288 to 1248** (768 wide, 960 tall). This is the union of the largest margin on each edge among TikTok, Reels and Shorts ad guidance. `[Practice]`
- Lower-right quadrant (x > 850, y > 1150): treat as button territory. Nothing important. `[Practice]`
- Under a disclaimer or long caption: raise the bottom margin to 768 px (y 1152 max). `[Practice]`
- Feed / grid crop test: also draw a centred 4:5 (1080x1350, y 285 to 1635) and a centred 3:4 (1080x1440, y 240 to 1680). The hero subject and its title must survive both. The 4:5 line comes from the official YouTube behaviour, the 3:4 line from Instagram secondary reports. `[Practice]`
- Decoration and background may bleed into the margins; information may not.
- Phone tile size: a profile grid tile is about a third of the screen width (roughly 125-135 pt on a 390 pt wide phone). Test at that size. `[Infer]`
- Re-check every few months; the apps change layout between versions and the sources above already disagree.

## What separates exceptional from solid
Ranked. Each one is a thing I can see in the exemplars, not a general rule.

1. **One idea that survives as a single shape.** Bass, Moss, Tokyo 1964 poster 1 and Milano Cortina each put the story into one silhouette or one gag (PS-01, PS-02, PS-04, PS-10, PS-15). Solid work has a good subject plus supporting elements. Mechanisms: `clutter-peripheral`, `shape`, `gestalt`.
2. **A second read for people who look twice.** The silhouette gives the first second; a hidden shape, a stat, or a texture gives the next three (PS-10, PS-20, PS-22). Mechanism: `negative-space`, `gestalt`.
3. **Type is the image, or it gets out of the way.** Scher, Mexico 68, the 1970 World Cup, Fong: type carries the picture (PS-06, PS-08, PS-14, PS-17). Muller-Brockmann and Stehrenberger go the other way: tiny type, so the image owns the frame (PS-03, PS-11). The weak middle is medium type next to a medium image. Mechanisms: `typography`, `scale`, `hierarchy-focal-point`.
4. **A system with one place to be wild.** A locked data block, grid and colour rule hold a set together; one slot is free (PS-07, PS-18, PS-21, PS-24). Solid teams make every post equal. Mechanisms: `unity-variety`, `grids-layout`, `rhythm-pattern`.
5. **Subtract when the client adds.** Funny Games ran with the gloved hand and extra poster refused (PS-11). Mexico 68 uses one line system for the whole frame (PS-06). Mechanisms: `rule-breaking`, `negative-space`.
6. **Colour used as a signal, not as brand paint.** Munich replaced the colour you expect for each sport with a signal colour from a fixed spectrum (PS-07). Mechanism: `color`, `value-contrast`.
7. **Material the feed does not have.** Letterpress, scratchboard, watercolour, grain (PS-12, PS-17, PS-19, PS-11). Feeds are full of clean cutouts, so texture is a way to stand out. Mechanism: `texture-detail`.
8. **Split the moment from the explanation.** Image alone first, one fact second (PS-23, PS-21). Mechanism: `hierarchy-focal-point`, `motion-principles`.

## Solid-but-not-exceptional traps
| Trap | Why it fails | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 16:9 broadcast frame squeezed into 9:16 | Subject small, graphics tiny, text in the zones apps cover (`reframing`, `framing-cropping`) | Rebuild the frame vertically: re-stack, enlarge type, move the subject to the upper middle, and check the 288/672 box and the 4:5 centre crop |
| Too many elements (crest, sponsor, three players, date, score, tagline) | Fails the 1-second read at tile size (`clutter-peripheral`) | One hero, one line of text, one number. Sponsor goes in the margin or the caption |
| Headline or score over busy action | Contrast falls where the eye is (`accessibility-legibility`, `value-contrast`) | Put type in planned negative space or add a plate or darkening gradient behind it; check at 10% size |
| Dense, zoom-friendly art used as a feed cover (PS-16) | No entry point at phone size (`fluency-complexity`) | Make a one-shape cover and keep the dense art for the tap-through |
| Key text in the platform UI zones | Covered by captions and buttons (`reframing`) | Keep information in the safe box; background only in the margins |
| Every post is the same template | The feed turns to wallpaper | Two tiers like the Bulls (PS-21): template for volume, a hand-built piece for the moment |
| Star portrait with logos and a gradient | Looks like every other post | Use the odd, specific object or a signal colour (PS-13, PS-07) |
| Fine type, thin weights, long stats | Gone at tile size (`typography`, `accessibility-legibility`) | Heavy, short, few words; one number at headline size |
| Cover not tested in the grid crop | Face or title cut by the 3:4 or 4:5 crop | Draw both crops over the master; keep subject and title inside both |

## Push move candidates
Rungs: FIX = below competent, C→S = competent to strong, S→W = strong to world-class.

| Name | When | How | Rung | Exemplars |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Safe-box and crop pass | Any 9:16 or cover | Draw x 120-888 / y 288-1248, then 4:5 and 3:4 centre crops over the master; move anything out of range | FIX | PS-16 (limit case), safety section |
| Rebuild vertically | A 16:9 asset must go vertical | Re-stack into a column, raise type, subject upper-middle; do not scale | FIX | PS-23, PS-19 |
| 10% and tile test | Before delivery | Downscale to about 130 px wide and to 10%; message and focal peak must survive | FIX | PS-01, PS-04 |
| Strip to one hero | Frame has more than one competing focal point | Remove everything but subject, one line, one number | C→S | PS-11, PS-13 |
| Two-tier output | A season of posts | Locked template for volume; one hand-built piece per week | C→S | PS-21, PS-18 |
| Two-frame beat | A Story set | Frame 1 image only; frame 2 one fact | C→S | PS-23 |
| Three audience crops | Cover or thumbnail with an audience | Make the star, rival and moment versions; test | C→S | PS-25 |
| Silhouette plus hidden second read | Hero poster or Story cover | Build the subject as one shape and put a detail in the negative space | S→W | PS-01, PS-10, PS-22 |
| Type as the picture | Event, score, quote | Headline occupies 40%+ of the frame, shaped and coloured as the image; or shrink type to near nothing | S→W | PS-06, PS-08, PS-14, PS-17, PS-03 |
| Signal colour per game | A series | One colour per game from a fixed spectrum, off the team palette | S→W | PS-07 |
| Hand-made headline word | Brand moment | Scratch, burn or letterpress one word; shoot or scan it | S→W | PS-12, PS-17 |
| Edge-to-edge system | Logotype or crest work | Run the logo's lines to all four edges so every crop contains it | S→W | PS-06 |
| Symbol for the tile, photo for the story | Reel cover vs. content | Use the symbol poster (PS-04) for the grid, the single photo (PS-05) inside | C→S | PS-04, PS-05 |
