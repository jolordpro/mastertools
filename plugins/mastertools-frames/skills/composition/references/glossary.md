# Glossary

## Quick answer
- Format: **Term** (synonyms): definition. → file id
- Search by any synonym. Terms are grouped by area, then alphabetical.

## Perception and science
- **Aesthetic episode**: Leder & Nadal's term for one full encounter with an artwork, from perception to judgment. → `aesthetic-models`
- **Arousal potential** (Berlyne curve, inverted-U): idea that liking peaks at medium complexity or novelty. → `fluency-complexity`
- **Attentional synchrony** (gaze clustering): many viewers looking at the same spot at the same moment, typical of edited film. → `continuity-attention`
- **Border ownership**: the visual system's decision about which region an edge belongs to. Drives figure-ground. → `gestalt`
- **Bottom-up attention** (exogenous, stimulus-driven): attention pulled by contrast, motion, color. Opposite: top-down (task-driven). → `attention-saliency`
- **Center bias, aesthetic**: preference for a lone front-facing subject at or near center. → `spatial-composition`
- **Center bias, viewing**: tendency for fixations to land near image center regardless of content. Different from aesthetic center bias. → `attention-saliency`
- **Clutter** (visual clutter): state where adding an item would no longer draw attention. → `clutter-peripheral`
- **Common fate**: elements moving together are seen as a group. Strongest grouping cue in motion. → `gestalt`
- **Crowding**: objects in peripheral vision blur together into texture. → `clutter-peripheral`
- **Equiluminance** (isoluminance): two colors with different hue but the same luminance. Edges between them look unstable. → `value-contrast`
- **Feature Congestion**: Rosenholtz's clutter metric based on local variability of color, orientation and contrast. → `clutter-peripheral`
- **Figure-ground** (figure/ground, positive/negative): splitting a scene into object and background. → `gestalt`
- **Fixation / saccade**: a pause of the eye / the jump between pauses. → `attention-saliency`
- **Fluency** (processing fluency): how easily an image is processed. Higher fluency raises liking. → `fluency-complexity`
- **Foveal / peripheral vision**: sharp central vision / low-detail surround that keeps only summary statistics. → `clutter-peripheral`
- **Gaze cueing**: viewers follow the direction a depicted face looks. → `line-direction`
- **Gist** (scene gist): meaning grasped in a single glance. → `clutter-peripheral`
- **Gestalt principles** (laws of grouping): proximity, similarity, continuity, closure, common fate, common region, connectedness, symmetry. → `gestalt`
- **Inward bias** (lead room, look room, nose room, walking room): preference for a subject facing or moving into the frame. → `spatial-composition`
- **ISC** (inter-subject correlation): how similarly different viewers' brains respond to the same film. → `continuity-attention`
- **Pop-out**: an item found instantly because it differs from its surround in one feature. → `hierarchy-focal-point`
- **Prägnanz** (good form, simplicity principle): perception favors the simplest stable organization. → `gestalt`
- **Representational fit**: placement matches meaning, e.g. a "sinking" object sits low. → `spatial-composition`
- **Saliency** (salience, conspicuity): how strongly a region attracts attention. → `attention-saliency`
- **Scanpath** (eye path, eye flow, visual flow): ordered sequence of fixations. → `attention-saliency`
- **Spatial agency bias** (SAB): agents placed on the side where the viewer's script begins (left for left-to-right readers). → `culture-direction`
- **Troxler fading**: unchanging peripheral stimuli fade from awareness. → `value-contrast`
- **WAVE** (Weighted Affective Valence Estimate): Palmer & Schloss model, color preference follows liking for objects of that color. → `color`
- **"What" and "Where" systems**: Livingstone's terms for the color/detail pathway and the colorblind luminance pathway. → `value-contrast`

## Image statistics
- **1/f spectrum** (scale invariance, Fourier slope): natural scenes and much art have Fourier power falling with spatial frequency at a slope near −2. → `image-statistics`
- **Edge-orientation entropy** (EOE): how evenly edge directions are spread across an image. High in art and nature. → `image-statistics`
- **Fractal dimension D**: measure of how detail repeats across scales, from 1 (smooth line) to 2 (filled plane). → `image-statistics`
- **PHOG self-similarity**: measure of how alike image parts look at different scales. → `image-statistics`
- **RMS contrast**: standard deviation of luminance. A simple global contrast measure. → `value-contrast`

## Formal elements and principles
- **Asymmetrical balance** (informal balance, occult balance): unequal elements balanced by weight, not mirroring. → `balance`
- **Balance** (equilibrium, pictorial balance): distribution of visual weight around the frame's axes. → `balance`
- **Center of mass** (CoM, visual center of gravity): weighted average location of visual weight. → `balance`
- **Closed form / open form**: composition contained within the frame vs one that implies continuation beyond it. Wölfflin pair. → `framing-cropping`
- **Contrast** (in principle): difference in any property that creates emphasis. → `value-contrast`, `hierarchy-focal-point`
- **Contrast and affinity**: Block's principle. Difference raises visual intensity, similarity lowers it. → `visual-structure`
- **Direction** (vector, directional force): implied movement from lines, gaze, gesture or motion. → `line-direction`
- **Dominance / subordination**: one element leads, others support. → `hierarchy-focal-point`
- **Emphasis** (focal point, center of interest, point of interest): the element seen first. → `hierarchy-focal-point`
- **Hierarchy** (visual hierarchy): ranked order of attention. → `hierarchy-focal-point`
- **Movement** (implied motion, visual flow): path the eye travels. → `line-direction`
- **Negative space** (white space, empty space, breathing room, ma): unoccupied area. → `negative-space`
- **Pattern / rhythm / repetition**: recurring elements and the beat they create. → `rhythm-pattern`
- **Proportion**: relation of parts to each other and to the whole. → `scale`, `geometry-proportion`
- **Radial balance**: elements radiating from a center. → `balance`
- **Scale**: size relative to other elements or to the viewer. → `scale`
- **Structural skeleton** (structural net): Arnheim's hidden map of center, axes and diagonals that pulls on elements. → `balance`
- **Symmetry** (bilateral, radial, translational): mirror, rotational or repeated sameness. → `balance`, `geometry-proportion`
- **Tension**: felt instability that asks to resolve. → `line-direction`
- **Unity / variety** (harmony / contrast): coherence vs difference. → `unity-variety`
- **Visual weight**: how strongly an element pulls on balance (size, value, color, isolation, interest, position). → `balance`

## Light, value, color
- **Aerial perspective** (atmospheric perspective): distant things lose contrast, saturation and shift cool. → `depth-space`
- **Chiaroscuro**: strong light-dark modeling of form. → `light`
- **Chroma / saturation**: color intensity. → `color`
- **Color script**: a sequence of small paintings planning color across a film (Pixar practice). → `color`
- **Color temperature**: warm (orange) to cool (blue), measured in kelvin for light sources. → `color`, `light`
- **Contrast ratio** (lighting ratio, key-to-fill ratio): brightness of key vs fill side. → `light`
- **Contrast ratio** (text legibility): luminance ratio between text and background, per WCAG. → `accessibility-legibility`
- **Dynamic range**: span from darkest to brightest recordable value. → `photo-craft`
- **High key / low key**: mostly light tones with soft shadow / mostly dark tones with strong shadow. → `light`
- **Hue**: color family (red, blue...). → `color`
- **Key light / fill light / back light** (rim, kicker, hair light): main, shadow-filling and separating lights. → `light`
- **Motivated light**: light that appears to come from a source in the scene. → `light`
- **Notan**: Japanese term used by Arthur Wesley Dow for a 2 to 4 value light-dark pattern. → `value-contrast`
- **Rembrandt lighting**: key high and to one side, leaving a small triangle of light on the shadow cheek. → `light`
- **Simultaneous contrast**: a color looks different depending on its surround. → `color`
- **Split-complementary, analogous, triadic, complementary**: classic harmony schemes. → `color`
- **Tenebrism**: extreme chiaroscuro with large dark areas (Caravaggio). → `light`
- **Value** (tone, lightness, luminance): how light or dark, regardless of hue. → `value-contrast`
- **Zone System**: Ansel Adams and Fred Archer's 11-zone exposure and printing method. → `photo-craft`

## Space and depth
- **Deep / flat / limited / ambiguous space**: Block's four space types. → `depth-space`
- **Deep focus** (deep staging): foreground to background all sharp. → `depth-space`
- **Depth cues** (monocular cues): occlusion, relative size, relative density, height in field, aerial perspective, motion parallax, plus binocular disparity. → `depth-space`
- **Foreground framing** (frame within a frame, natural frame): near objects that frame the subject. → `framing-cropping`
- **Occlusion** (overlap, interposition): nearer objects hide farther ones. Strongest depth cue at every distance. → `depth-space`
- **Planes** (foreground, midground, background): layered depth zones. → `depth-space`
- **Vanishing point** (one-, two-, three-point perspective): where parallel lines converge. → `depth-space`, `line-direction`

## Geometry and layout
- **Armature** (compositional armature, underlying structure): geometric scaffold used to place elements. → `geometry-proportion`
- **Baseline grid**: horizontal lines that type sits on. → `grids-layout`
- **Column / gutter / margin / module / field**: grid parts. → `grids-layout`
- **Dynamic symmetry**: Jay Hambidge's system of root rectangles and diagonals (1920). → `geometry-proportion`
- **Golden ratio** (phi, golden section, golden mean, 1.618): proportion with weak preference evidence. → `geometry-proportion`, `myths-evidence`
- **Modular scale**: type or spacing sizes stepped by a fixed ratio. → `typography`
- **Rabatment**: swinging the short side of a rectangle into the long side to find a square. → `geometry-proportion`
- **Root rectangles** (√2, √3, √5): rectangles built from square-root proportions. ISO paper is √2. → `geometry-proportion`
- **Rule of thirds** (thirds grid, power points): placing elements on lines dividing the frame in thirds. Weak as a quality rule. → `myths-evidence`
- **Safe area** (action safe, title safe, graphics safe): zones guaranteed visible on screens. → `broadcast-standards`

## Camera and film grammar
- **180-degree rule** (axis of action, the line): keep the camera on one side of the action to preserve screen direction. → `continuity-attention`
- **30-degree rule**: change angle by at least about 30° between cuts on the same subject to avoid a jump cut. → `continuity-attention`
- **Aspect ratio** (1.33, 1.37 Academy, 1.66, 1.78/16:9, 1.85, 2.39 scope, 9:16 vertical, 4:5, 1:1): frame shape. → `framing-cropping`
- **ASL** (average shot length): film running time divided by number of shots. → `film-statistics`
- **Coverage**: set of shots filmed of one scene. → `shot-grammar`
- **Dutch angle** (canted angle, Dutch tilt): camera rolled off level. → `camera-lens-movement`
- **Eyeline match**: a look in one shot points at what the next shot shows. → `continuity-attention`
- **Headroom**: space above a subject's head. → `shot-grammar`
- **Jump cut**: cut between similar framings that makes the subject jump. → `continuity-attention`
- **Match on action** (match cut on action): cut in the middle of a movement. → `continuity-attention`
- **Over-the-shoulder** (OTS), **two-shot**, **single**, **insert**, **cutaway**, **establishing shot**: standard shot types. → `shot-grammar`
- **Pillow shot**: Ozu's term in criticism for still transitional shots of places or objects. → `filmmakers`
- **POV shot** (point-of-view, subjective shot): camera as a character's eyes. → `shot-grammar`
- **Shot size** (shot scale: ECU, CU, MCU, MS, MLS/cowboy, LS/full, ELS/extreme wide): how much of the subject the frame holds. → `shot-grammar`
- **Short side / long side** (short-siding, broad-siding): subject faces the near edge (tense) or open space (comfortable). → `spatial-composition`
- **Staging in depth** (blocking in depth): arranging actors along the camera axis. → `depth-space`

## Motion design and broadcast
- **Anticipation / follow-through / overlap**: Disney animation principles for preparing and finishing motion. → `motion-principles`
- **Bug** (score bug, network bug, DOG): persistent on-screen graphic. → `broadcast-standards`
- **Easing** (slow in, slow out, ease in, ease out): acceleration and deceleration curves. → `motion-principles`
- **Glanceability**: readable in one short look. → `broadcast-standards`, `clutter-peripheral`
- **Lower third** (L3, chyron, super, name strap): caption graphic in the lower part of frame. → `broadcast-standards`
- **Reframing** (smart crop, auto-reframe, cutdown): adapting a composition to another aspect ratio. → `reframing`
- **Staging** (motion): presenting one idea at a time so it reads. → `motion-principles`

## Critique and analysis
- **Feldman method**: describe, analyze, interpret, judge. → `formal-analysis`
- **Formal analysis**: describing a work by its visual elements and their arrangement. → `formal-analysis`
- **Iconography / iconology**: Panofsky's levels of meaning. → `formal-analysis`
- **Pairwise comparison** (A/B judgment): choosing the better of two versions. More reliable than single scores. → `protocol`
- **Wölfflin's pairs**: linear/painterly, plane/recession, closed/open, multiplicity/unity, absolute/relative clarity. → `formal-analysis`

## AI and computation
- **AesEval-Bench, ShotBench, PICD, CADB**: benchmarks for design, cinematography and composition. → `datasets-benchmarks`
- **ControlNet**: method to steer image generation with depth, edges or pose. → `generation-loop`
- **IoU** (intersection over union): overlap score between two boxes. → `metric-recipes`
- **IQA / IAA**: image quality assessment / image aesthetic assessment. → `tools-models`
- **Saliency model** (DeepGaze, UMSI): predicts where people look. → `tools-models`
- **Segmentation** (SAM): separating objects into masks. → `tools-models`
- **SRCC / PLCC**: rank and linear correlation between model scores and human ratings. → `datasets-benchmarks`
