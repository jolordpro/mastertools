# Master painters

## Quick answer
- The most transferable lessons: value-first structure (Caravaggio, Rembrandt), light as storyteller (Vermeer, Hopper), radical cropping (Degas, ukiyo-e), context-rich frames (Hokusai), equiluminance for glow (Monet).
- `DOC` = documented by measurement or primary record. `RETRO` = later analysis. Most "secret geometry" claims are RETRO.

## Case studies
### Caravaggio (1571 to 1610)
- Tenebrism: large dark fields, a single raking light picking out faces and hands. `[Practice]`
- Maximum value contrast placed at the narrative focus (e.g. *The Calling of Saint Matthew*: light beam and pointing hand lead to Matthew). `RETRO`, consistent with luminance and gaze-cue science.
- Lesson: one light, one focus, darkness as negative space.

### Rembrandt (1606 to 1669)
- Warm golden light emerging from deep shadow; detail and impasto at the focal area, looser handling elsewhere. `[Practice]`
- "Rembrandt lighting" named after his portraits: small triangle of light on the shadow cheek. `[Practice]`
- Lesson: detail hierarchy and light pools guide the eye.

### Vermeer (1632 to 1675)
- Side light from a left window, quiet interiors, careful geometry and stillness. `[Practice]`
- Use of optical devices (camera obscura) debated: Hockney-Falco thesis; *Tim's Vermeer* (2013). `[Unverified; contested]`
- Lesson: one consistent soft light source and calm horizontal-vertical structure.

### Hokusai and ukiyo-e
- *The Great Wave off Kanagawa* (about 1831): massive foreground wave framing a small Mount Fuji; strong diagonals and curves. `[Practice]`
- High horizons and small figures, consistent with East Asian context-inclusive composition (Masuda et al. 2008). `[Infer]`
- Flat color areas, bold contours and asymmetric cropping influenced Western artists. `[Unverified]`

### Degas (1834 to 1917)
- Off-center subjects, figures cut by the frame edge, high viewpoints, empty floor space. `[Practice]`
- Influence of ukiyo-e and snapshot photography often cited. `[Unverified]`
- Lesson: open form and edge cropping create immediacy.

### Monet (1840 to 1926)
- *Impression, Sunrise* (1872): the sun matches the sky's luminance, so it shimmers. `DOC` measurement (Livingstone); intent `RETRO`.
- Lesson: equiluminance for glow and atmosphere; value contrast for structure.

### Hopper (1882 to 1967)
- Strong directional light shapes, large empty spaces, isolated figures, architecture as frame. `[Practice]`
- Often cited as an influence on cinematographers' framing and light. `[Unverified]`
- Lesson: negative space and hard light for loneliness and tension.

### Pollock (1912 to 1956)
- Fractal dimension of drip paintings rose from about 1.1 to 1.3 (1945) to about 1.7 to 1.9 (1950) (Taylor et al. 2011). `DOC` measurement; fractal authentication debated.
- Lesson: all-over composition can still have measurable structure.

## Patterns across masters `[Infer]`
- Big value masses first, detail last.
- A single dominant light source.
- Focus defined on several channels at once (light, detail, gaze lines).
- Edges used actively (cropping, framing).
