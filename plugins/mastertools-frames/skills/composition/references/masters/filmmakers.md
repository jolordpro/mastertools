# Master filmmakers

## Quick answer
- Hitchcock: precise attention control. Kubrick and Anderson: centered symmetry and one-point perspective. Ozu: low fixed camera and unconventional space. Welles and Toland: deep focus staging. Deakins: motivated naturalistic light and clean geometry. Lubezki: natural light and long takes. Willis: darkness as composition. Storaro: color as meaning.

## Case studies
### Alfred Hitchcock
- *Bang! You're Dead* produced over 65% cortical inter-subject correlation (Hasson et al. 2008). `DOC` measurement.
- Hasson et al. quote Hitchcock describing creation as based on an exact science of audience reactions. `[secondhand]`
- Lesson: control what the viewer sees and when (POV, inserts, eyeline chains).

### Stanley Kubrick
- Centered one-point perspective corridors and symmetric frames (*The Shining*, *2001*). `[Practice]` Consistent with aesthetic center bias for frontal subjects. `[Infer]`
- Lesson: symmetry reads as order, authority, or menace.

### Wes Anderson
- Planar tableaux, centered subjects, flat space, controlled palettes, whip pans. `[Practice]`
- Lesson: flat space plus symmetry plus palette create a recognizable world.

### Yasujirō Ozu
- Low static camera, frontal framing, "pillow shots" of places between scenes, 360° space that crosses the axis (Bordwell 1988). `[Unverified]`
- Lesson: a consistent system can replace continuity rules (see `rule-breaking`).

### Orson Welles and Gregg Toland
- *Citizen Kane* (1941): deep focus and staging in depth; low angles with visible ceilings. `[Unverified details]`
- Lesson: let the viewer read relationships across planes without cutting.

### Roger Deakins
- Motivated, often single-source light; strong silhouettes; clean graphic compositions (*Blade Runner 2049*, *1917*, both Academy Award wins). `[Practice]`
- Lesson: simplify, and let light define shape and focus.

### Emmanuel Lubezki
- Natural and available light, wide lenses close to actors, long takes (*Children of Men*, *Birdman*, *The Revenant*). Three consecutive Academy Awards (*Gravity*, *Birdman*, *The Revenant*). `[Practice]`
- Lesson: in long takes, staging and camera movement do the job of cuts. `[Infer]`

### Gordon Willis
- Top light that hides eyes, deep shadows, underexposure as storytelling (*The Godfather*). Nicknamed "the Prince of Darkness." `[Unverified]`
- Lesson: withholding information through darkness creates mystery and power.

### Vittorio Storaro
- Color as symbolic language across films (*Apocalypse Now*, *The Last Emperor*). `[Unverified]`
- Lesson: plan color as a script (see `color`).

### Conrad Hall
- Naturalistic, often backlit or rain-soaked light; *Road to Perdition*, *American Beauty*. `[Unverified]`

## Patterns `[Infer]`
- A system per film (light, palette, lens, space type) rather than shot-by-shot prettiness.
- Focus built on several channels: light, staging, lens, movement.
- Rule-breaks are consistent and motivated.
