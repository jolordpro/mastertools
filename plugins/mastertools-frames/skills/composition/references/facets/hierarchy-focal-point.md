# Hierarchy and focal point

## Quick answer
- Emphasis comes from contrast in any channel against a calm field: value, color, size, sharpness, isolation, motion, shape. `[E-strong]`
- Plan 2 to 4 ranked levels: primary, secondary, tertiary. `[Practice]`
- The focal point should win on several channels at once, and nothing else should win on any. `[Practice]`

## Definition
- **Focal point**: the element seen first and remembered.
- **Hierarchy**: ranked order of attention among elements.
- **Eye path**: the route the eye takes from the focal point through supporting elements.

## Mechanism and evidence
- Pop-out: an item is found instantly when it differs from its local surround in a feature (Rosenholtz summary-statistics account). `[E-strong]`
- Faces, text, motion onset and highest contrast attract attention. `[E-strong]`
- Clear hierarchy raises processing fluency and so liking (Reber et al. 2004). `[E-strong]`

## Ways to create emphasis `[Practice]`
- Contrast: value first, then saturation, hue, size, sharpness, texture.
- Isolation: surround with negative space.
- Convergence: lines, gazes, light falloff point to it.
- Placement: center for frontal subjects; off-center with lead room for directional ones.
- Anomaly: one different item in a repeated pattern.
- Framing: frame within a frame.
- Motion: the moving element wins in video; keep others still.
- Detail: the most rendered or sharpest area.

## Eye path techniques `[Practice]`
- Entry point (often the highest contrast), then a planned route via lines, gazes and secondary accents, ending back at the subject.
- Stoppers at edges (darker corners, vignettes, vertical elements) to keep the eye in.
- Avoid exits: bright edges, lines running off corners.

## By medium
- Photography: one subject; vignette and depth of field support.
- Design: headline → image → subhead → body → call to action. Typography is hierarchy (see `typography`).
- Film: hierarchy changes over time; blocking and focus pulls move the focal point.
- Broadcast: the live action is primary; graphics are secondary and must not out-shout it except during dedicated full-screens.
- Motion: stagger reveals to set reading order.

## How to measure
- Saliency map → peak 1 / peak 2 strength ratio. `[Infer]` Target > 1.5.
- Overlap of peak 1 with the intended subject mask.
- Channel audit: for the subject vs the next strongest element, compare ΔL*, chroma, size, sharpness, isolation distance. Count channels where the subject wins.
- For layouts: ranked size and contrast of text blocks vs intended order.

## Failure modes
- Two or more equal focal points (split attention).
- Brightest or most saturated element is not the subject.
- Hierarchy flattened: all text the same size and weight.
- Eye exits the frame via an edge highlight or line.

## Critique questions
- What do you see first, second, third? Does it match the message?
- On how many channels does the subject win?
- Where does the eye leave the frame, if anywhere?
