# Framing and cropping

## Quick answer
- The frame's edges are active: they pull on nearby elements and define the inward bias. `[E-strong]`
- Crop with intent: tight for intimacy and intensity, loose for context and calm. `[Practice]`
- Aspect ratio changes what compositions work; recompose, don't only crop, when changing format. `[Practice]`

## Definition
- **Framing**: choosing what the rectangle includes.
- **Cropping**: cutting into a subject or removing area after capture.
- **Frame within a frame**: doorways, windows, arches, shoulders that enclose the subject.
- **Closed form**: everything contained in the frame. **Open form**: implies continuation beyond it (Wölfflin pair). `[Unverified]`
- **Aspect ratios**: 1:1 square; 4:5 social portrait; 1.33 (4:3); 1.37 Academy; 1.66; 1.78 (16:9 HD); 1.85 flat; 2.39 scope (historically 2.35/2.40); 9:16 vertical.

## Evidence
- Forman et al. 2021: the inward bias is driven by frame borders; viewers keep a roughly constant front-to-back space ratio across frame widths. `[E-strong]`
- Palmer et al. 2008: center bias for frontal objects holds across frames. `[E-strong]`
- Degas's cropping influenced by ukiyo-e and photography. `[Unverified]`

## Techniques
- `[Practice]` Frame within a frame to focus attention and add depth.
- `[Practice]` Crop between joints, not at them, unless tension is wanted.
- `[Practice]` Bleeding a subject off the edge (open form) suggests a larger world or motion.
- `[Practice]` Keep critical elements away from edges by a margin; edges are where displays and platforms crop.
- `[Practice]` Horizon placement: low for sky drama, high for land detail, centered for symmetry and reflection.
- `[Practice]` Wide ratios (2.39) favor landscapes, groups, isolation via empty space; square and vertical favor single subjects and stacked compositions.
- `[Practice]` Vertical 9:16: subject in upper-middle, leave room for platform UI at top and bottom.

## By medium
- Photography: crop in post for intent, not to rescue focus.
- Design: bleed images off the page edge for scale and energy.
- Film: ratio is a world-building choice; some films change ratio for flashbacks or perspective.
- Broadcast: 16:9 with safe areas; protect for 4:3 only if a downstream feed requires it (see `broadcast-standards`).
- Social and multi-format: plan a "protection zone" that works in 16:9, 1:1, 4:5 and 9:16 (see `reframing`).

## How to measure
- Distance from subject mask to each edge as % of frame.
- Edge tangency: subject contour within a few pixels of an edge without crossing.
- Crop-through-joint check from pose landmarks (wrist, elbow, knee, ankle near an edge).
- Horizon height as % of frame height; tilt in degrees.
- Multi-format survival: does the subject mask stay inside each target crop?

## Failure modes
- Subject kisses the frame edge (tangent).
- Amputation at joints.
- Accidental frame-within-frame that traps a secondary element.
- Composition built for 16:9 brutally center-cropped to 9:16.

## Critique questions
- What does the frame exclude, and does the exclusion help?
- Are the edges clean (no tangents, no amputations)?
- Will this crop survive every delivery format?
