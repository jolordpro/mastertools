# Shape and form

## Quick answer
- Big simple shapes carry composition; detail lives inside them. `[Practice]`
- Shape associations (Bang): rounded = safe, pointed = threatening, horizontal = stable, diagonal = active, larger = stronger. `[Practice] [Unverified]`
- A strong silhouette reads instantly and at small sizes. `[Practice]`

## Definition
- **Shape**: 2D area defined by edge, value or color.
- **Form**: shape that appears 3D through shading and perspective.
- **Mass**: grouped shapes that read as one weight.
- **Silhouette**: the outline shape alone.
- **Positive / negative shape**: object shape vs the shape of the space around it (see `negative-space`).

## Bang's principles `[Practice] [Unverified for exact wording]`
- Smooth flat horizontals feel stable and calm.
- Verticals feel active and energetic, reaching.
- Diagonals imply motion and tension.
- The upper half of a picture feels freer, happier, lighter; the lower half heavier, sadder, grounded.
- The center is the most effective point of attention.
- White or light backgrounds feel safer than dark.
- Pointed shapes feel threatening; rounded shapes feel secure.
- Larger objects feel stronger.
- Viewers associate the same or similar colors more than the same shapes.
- Contrast enables seeing.

## Techniques
- `[Practice]` Shape language in character and logo design: circles for friendly, squares for stable, triangles for energetic or dangerous.
- `[Practice]` Silhouette test: fill the subject black; if it still reads, the pose or object is clear.
- `[Practice]` Group small elements into one larger mass to simplify.
- `[Practice]` Vary shape sizes (big, medium, small) to avoid monotony.
- `[Practice]` Repeat a shape motif to unify (see `rhythm-pattern`).

## By medium
- Photography: look for shapes before subjects; backlight creates silhouettes.
- Design: logo and icon shape language sets brand tone.
- Film and animation: character shape language; set shapes frame emotion.
- Broadcast: graphic packages often build on one shape motif (angled cuts for sports energy). `[Practice]`

## How to measure
- Segmentation masks → contour simplification → convexity, angularity (corner count), aspect ratio.
- Silhouette legibility: classify the subject from mask only (proxy for clarity).
- Size distribution of segments (big/medium/small variety).

## Failure modes
- Weak, ambiguous silhouettes (limbs merging with body).
- All shapes the same size.
- Shape language at odds with the message (soft round forms for aggressive promo).

## Critique questions
- What are the three biggest shapes, and do they organize the frame?
- Does the silhouette read on its own?
- What does the shape language say emotionally?
