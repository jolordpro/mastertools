# Unity and variety

## Quick answer
- Unity: elements feel like they belong to one system. Variety: enough difference to hold interest. `[Practice]`
- Coherence raises fluency and liking; some variation raises interest. `[E-strong / E-mixed]` (see `fluency-complexity`)
- Build unity first (shared palette, shape motif, alignment, light direction), then add controlled variety at the focal point. `[Practice]`

## Definition
- **Unity / harmony**: consistency across elements (color, shape, scale system, style, light).
- **Variety / contrast**: deliberate difference.
- Block's equivalent: affinity (similarity, lower intensity) vs contrast (difference, higher intensity). `[Unverified]`

## Techniques
- `[Practice]` Limited palette and one type family.
- `[Practice]` One light direction across all elements.
- `[Practice]` Repeat a shape or angle motif.
- `[Practice]` Introduce one strong variation (size, color, direction) at the focal point.
- `[Practice]` In a graphics package, unity across all assets beats novelty in any single asset.

## By medium
- Photography series: consistent tone and grade across a set.
- Design: design systems and grids.
- Film: visual rules for a world, broken at story turns.
- Broadcast: a package (bugs, lower thirds, full-screens, transitions) must read as one system.

## How to measure
- Palette distance across assets in a set.
- Style consistency: CLIP or DINO embedding variance across a package.
- Light direction agreement across composited elements (hard; LLM judgment).

## Failure modes
- Kitchen-sink design: every element a different style.
- Over-unified: no focal variation, flat and dull.

## Critique questions
- What ties everything together?
- Where is the one deliberate difference?

## Terms
- unity, harmony, coherence, variety, contrast, affinity, consistency, design system
