# Negative space

## Quick answer
- Empty space isolates the subject, sets scale and gives the eye rest. `[Practice, consistent with clutter and isolation evidence]`
- Space is active when its shape matters and when it sits in the subject's direction. `[Practice]`
- More space is not always better: without clear hierarchy, emptiness reads as unfinished. `[Practice]`

## Definition
- **Negative space**: area not occupied by the main subject or elements.
- **Active space**: space in front of a facing or moving subject, or space whose shape reads.
- **Passive space**: space behind the subject or leftover space.
- **Ma** (Japanese): meaningful interval or pause. `[Unverified]`

## Mechanism
- Isolation raises salience (see `hierarchy-focal-point`). `[E-strong]`
- Lower clutter improves glanceability (see `clutter-peripheral`). `[E-strong]`
- Space ahead of a subject satisfies the inward bias (see `spatial-composition`). `[E-strong]`

## Techniques
- `[Practice]` Small subject in large field for loneliness, scale, contemplation.
- `[Practice]` Generous margins in layouts signal calm and premium positioning.
- `[Practice]` Shape the negative space so it forms an interesting secondary shape.
- `[Practice]` Use space to separate groups (proximity, see `gestalt`).
- `[Practice]` Leave space for type in photography and key art when the image will carry text.

## By medium
- Photography: sky, water, walls, fog as space.
- Design: margins, gutters, spacing scale.
- Film: wide shots with small figures (scale, isolation); space ahead in singles.
- Broadcast: graphics need clear space around them; avoid filling every corner with bugs and tickers.
- Social: vertical frames need planned space for platform UI overlays (captions, buttons). `[Practice]`

## How to measure
- % of frame outside salient or segmented regions.
- Distribution of empty space around the subject (front vs behind, top vs bottom).
- Largest empty rectangle area and position (for text placement).
- Design: spacing consistency between groups vs within groups.

## Failure modes
- Space evenly spread with no shape or direction.
- Subject crammed against an edge with empty space behind.
- Space so large the subject loses its role.

## Critique questions
- Is the empty space working (direction, isolation, shape, scale), or leftover?
- Is there room for planned text or overlays?

## Terms
- negative space, white space, active space, passive space, margin, gutter, breathing room, ma, isolation
