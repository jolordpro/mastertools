# Value and contrast

## Quick answer
- Composition lives mostly in value. If the grayscale version fails, the image usually fails. `[E-strong physiology, Practice application]`
- Plan 2 to 4 big value masses before color. `[Practice]`
- Put maximum value contrast at the focal point.

## Definition
- **Value**: how light or dark, independent of hue.
- **Value structure** (notan, value plan): the arrangement of big light and dark masses.
- **Contrast**: difference in value between areas; global (whole image) and local (at edges).
- **Equiluminance**: different hues, same value.

## Mechanism
- Livingstone (*Vision and Art*): the "Where" system is colorblind and handles depth, motion, figure-ground and position; the "What" system handles color, detail and recognition. `[E-strong]`
- SAYS (Livingstone): color comes from subtracting cone responses, luminance from summing them.
- Equiluminant edges look unstable because the luminance system can't see them. Example: the sun in Monet's *Impression, Sunrise* matches the sky's luminance, so it shimmers; a brighter sun would look less vibrant. `[E-strong measurement; artist intent RETRO]`
- A microsaccade study links the sun's fading to Troxler fading. `[E-mixed, single study]`

## Techniques
- `[Practice]` Notan study: reduce to 2, 3 or 4 values; the design should still read (Dow 1899). `[Unverified]`
- `[Practice]` Dark subject on light ground or light on dark; avoid mid on mid.
- `[Practice]` Value grouping: keep lights together and darks together, avoid a checkerboard of small values.
- `[Practice]` Squint test: squinting removes detail and shows masses.
- `[Practice]` Reserve pure white and pure black for the focal area.
- `[Practice]` Use equiluminance deliberately for shimmer, glow, dreaminess.

## By medium
- Photography: check with a monochrome preview; use the Zone System thinking for placement of key tones (see `photo-craft`).
- Design: text needs luminance contrast, not only hue contrast (see `accessibility-legibility`).
- Film: high contrast for drama; flatter tonality for realism or softness. Film trended darker over decades (Cutting et al. 2011).
- Broadcast: score bugs and lower thirds over unpredictable live video need their own background plate or strong edge to guarantee contrast.
- Motion and 3D: render a luminance AOV or desaturated preview to judge structure.

## How to measure
- Convert to luminance (CIELAB L* or Rec. 709 luma).
- Histogram spread, RMS contrast.
- Posterize to 3 and 4 clusters (k-means on L*) → area share and position of each value mass.
- Subject vs ground ΔL*. `[Infer]` Target ΔL* ≥ 20 for key text and focal subject.
- Equiluminance flag: hue difference large while ΔL* < about 5. `[Infer]`
- Local contrast at focal point vs median local contrast elsewhere.

## Failure modes
- Mid-value soup: everything gray, no structure.
- Color contrast without value contrast (red text on green, blue on purple).
- Many small scattered darks and lights: busy, no masses.
- Brightest highlight away from the subject.

## Critique questions
- Does the image read in grayscale at thumbnail size?
- How many value masses, and which is the largest?
- Is the strongest light-dark junction at the focal point?
