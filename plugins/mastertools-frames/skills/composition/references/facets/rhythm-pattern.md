# Rhythm and pattern

## Quick answer
- Repetition creates unity and a beat; a single break in a pattern becomes the focal point. `[Practice; pop-out is E-strong]`
- Rhythm types: regular, alternating, progressive (gradually changing), flowing (organic). `[Practice]`
- In film and motion, rhythm is also temporal: shot lengths, cuts, movement beats. `[Practice]`

## Definition
- **Pattern**: elements repeated in a predictable arrangement.
- **Rhythm**: the felt beat from repetition and intervals.
- **Pattern break**: one element that differs.

## Mechanism
- Repetition raises fluency (Reber et al. 2004). `[E-strong]`
- An anomaly in a homogeneous field pops out (Rosenholtz). `[E-strong]`

## Techniques
- `[Practice]` Repeat a shape, color or interval to tie a layout together.
- `[Practice]` Progressive rhythm (diminishing columns, receding poles) for depth and motion.
- `[Practice]` Break the pattern at the focal point (one red umbrella in gray ones).
- `[Practice]` Rule of odds (3 or 5 elements): common studio advice, no controlled evidence found. `[Unverified]`
- `[Practice]` In edits, vary shot length to avoid monotony; accelerate toward climaxes (see `visual-structure`).

## By medium
- Photography: architecture, crowds, nature patterns.
- Design: grids create regular rhythm; spacing scale sets the beat.
- Film: cutting rhythm and camera move rhythm.
- Motion and broadcast: staggered reveals (offset timing) create rhythm; keep stagger intervals consistent within a package.

## How to measure
- Autocorrelation or periodicity in the image (FFT peaks).
- Spacing regularity between repeated elements (variance of intervals).
- Outlier detection among similar elements (anomaly = candidate focal point).
- Video: shot length sequence, variance, trend.

## Failure modes
- Pattern with no break: monotony with no focus.
- Near-repetition that looks like a mistake (almost-even spacing).
- Stagger timing inconsistent across a graphics package.

## Critique questions
- What repeats, and where does the pattern break?
- Does the rhythm build or flatline?

## Terms
- rhythm, pattern, repetition, interval, progression, alternation, anomaly, cadence, stagger
