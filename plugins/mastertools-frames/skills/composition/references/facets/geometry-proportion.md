# Geometry and proportion

## Quick answer
- No grid rule (thirds, phi, spiral) predicts image quality. `[Lore as a law]`
- Thirds placement helps as an exit from dead center for subjects that face or move, which is the inward bias at work. `[E-strong via inward bias]`
- Armatures (thirds, root rectangles, diagonals, triangles) are useful scaffolds for planning and for describing structure. `[Practice]`

## Definition
- **Rule of thirds**: divide the frame into a 3×3 grid; place key elements on lines or intersections.
- **Golden ratio** (phi ≈ 1.618): the ratio where the whole is to the larger part as the larger is to the smaller.
- **Golden spiral**: logarithmic spiral from nested golden rectangles.
- **Dynamic symmetry** (Hambidge 1920): construction using root rectangles and diagonals (the frame's main diagonals plus reciprocals). `[Unverified]`
- **Root rectangles**: √2 (ISO paper), √3, √5 proportions.
- **Rabatment**: swing the short side into the long side to mark a square; its edge is a placement line.
- **Triangle, circle, S/C/L shapes**: simple shape armatures for grouping elements.

## Evidence
- Rule of thirds
  - Amirshahi, Hayn-Leichsenring, Denzler & Redies 2014: ten raters scored 200 photos, plus saliency-based thirds measures on large sets; thirds play a minor role, if any. `[Lore as quality law]`
  - Cutting 2015: 41% of faces in film frames fall in thirds zones overall, 19% in one-shots; author calls it not strong evidence.
  - Torabi & Teeravarunyou 2021 (eye tracking, 30 participants): thirds did not make photos more appealing to novices; more interesting to experts. `[E-mixed]`
- Golden ratio `[E-mixed, mostly Lore]`
  - Green 1995 review: preference is real but fragile, mostly an average across people.
  - Fechner's 10-rectangle study (per Green): 35.0% chose the golden rectangle; 20.6% chose 1.5:1.
  - McManus 1980: moderately good evidence for preference in the region, but 1.618 vs 1.5, 1.6 or 1.75 is unclear; large stable individual differences.
  - McManus & Weatherby 1997: objects preferred at golden sections horizontally, between them vertically. `[E-mixed]`
  - Stieger & Swami 2015: no automatic preference for golden-ratio placement in art pictures.
  - Olariu (565 paintings, Bellini to Cézanne): mean side ratio 1.34 ± 0.12, far from 1.618.
- Dynamic symmetry: historical use by some illustrators and painters; little preference testing. `[Practice; contested]`

## Techniques
- `[Practice]` Use thirds to place a directional subject off-center with room ahead.
- `[Practice]` Place horizons off-center to give sky or land dominance; center them for symmetry and reflection.
- `[Practice]` Align strong lines with the frame's diagonals for energy (see `line-direction`).
- `[Practice]` Triangular grouping of three figures or masses for stability and focus at the apex.
- `[Practice]` Use rabatment lines in wide formats (2.39, banners) where thirds feel arbitrary.
- `[Infer]` Report armature alignment as description ("main figure sits on the left rabatment line"), never as a score.

## By medium
- Photography: in-camera thirds grid is a reminder, not a rule.
- Design: modular grids carry real structure (see `grids-layout`); golden-ratio type scales are a stylistic choice.
- Film: wide aspect ratios invite off-center singles with look room; symmetry for formal moments.
- Broadcast: safe areas and graphics positions constrain placement more than any armature.

## How to measure
- Distance of subject centroid to nearest thirds line, phi line, rabatment line, center.
- Main line orientations vs frame diagonals.
- Output as descriptive tags only.

## Failure modes
- Moving a frontal subject off-center to satisfy thirds.
- Drawing a golden spiral over a finished image to justify it.
- Ignoring meaning, direction and balance in favor of grid compliance.

## Critique questions
- Why is the subject where it is? Name the reason (direction, balance, meaning), not the grid.
- Does the underlying shape (triangle, diagonal, circle) organize the elements?
