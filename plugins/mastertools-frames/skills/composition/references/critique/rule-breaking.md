# Rule-breaking

## Quick answer
- A break works when it creates a meaning the default cannot, and when it is consistent. `[Practice]`
- Defaults are measurable (center and inward bias, value-first hierarchy, continuity), so departures read as intent. `[Infer]`
- Ask: is the break systematic or accidental, and does it serve the message?

## Common motivated breaks `[Practice]`
- Subject facing out with no lead room: confinement, ending, threat behind.
- Dead-center horizon: symmetry, reflection, stasis.
- Extreme imbalance: unease, emptiness, isolation.
- Equiluminant color: shimmer, dream, glow (Monet).
- Clutter as subject: chaos, crowds, overload.
- Axis crossing and jump cuts: disorientation, energy, style (Ozu's system, Godard's jump cuts).
- Low audience synchrony as goal: open, contemplative viewing (Hasson: high synchrony doesn't mean better).
- Ambiguous space: dream, uncertainty.
- Cropping through faces: intimacy or violence.

## Context that changes the rules
- Culture: horizon height, figure size and direction defaults shift (`culture-direction`).
- Audience expertise: experts accept more ambiguity and complexity (`aesthetic-models`, `fluency-complexity`).
- Medium: broadcast and UI have little room for ambiguity; fine art and festival film have more.
- Brand systems may mandate deliberate departures (fixed logo corners, signature angles).

## How to judge a break
1. Is it consistent across the piece (a system) or a one-off?
2. Does it map to a story or brand meaning you can name?
3. Is everything else under control, so the break reads as chosen?
4. Would the default version say less?

## Critique questions
- What rule is being broken, and what does the break say?
- Is it repeated or justified elsewhere in the piece?

## Terms
- motivated break, style system, intentional tension, ambiguity, defamiliarization
