# Formal analysis frameworks

## Quick answer
- Feldman's four steps (describe, analyze, interpret, judge) are the backbone of the critique protocol. `[Practice]`
- Wölfflin's five pairs give vocabulary for style and structure. `[Practice]`
- Panofsky's three levels separate what is shown from what it means. `[Practice]`

## Feldman (1967, *Art as Image and Idea*) `[Unverified details]`
- Describe: list what is there, no judgment.
- Analyze: how elements relate (balance, emphasis, rhythm, space).
- Interpret: what it means or expresses.
- Judge: is it successful, and why.

## Wölfflin (1915, *Principles of Art History*) `[Unverified details]`
Each pair: Renaissance tendency ↔ Baroque tendency.
- Linear ↔ painterly: contour-defined forms vs tonal masses.
- Plane ↔ recession: layers parallel to the picture plane vs diagonal depth.
- Closed form ↔ open form: contained by the frame vs continuing beyond it.
- Multiplicity ↔ unity: independent parts vs parts subordinate to a whole.
- Absolute clarity ↔ relative clarity: everything legible vs selective emphasis.

## Panofsky (1939, *Studies in Iconology*) `[Unverified details]`
- Pre-iconographic: forms and motifs (a man, a sword, a gesture).
- Iconographic: conventional meaning (this is Saint Peter).
- Iconological: deeper cultural meaning (what the work reveals about its time).

## Studio critique formats `[Practice]`
- Silent viewing first, then description by others before the maker speaks.
- "What's working, what's not, what's next."
- Critique against the stated intent, not the critic's taste.
- Focus on one or two highest-leverage changes.
