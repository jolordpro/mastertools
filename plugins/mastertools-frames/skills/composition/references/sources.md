# Sources

## Quick answer
- Grouped by area. Peer-reviewed first, then standards, then canonical books.
- `(Unverified)` after a book means its details in this library were not checked against the text this pass.

## Spatial composition and balance
- Palmer, S. E., Gardner, J. S., & Wickens, T. D. (2008). Aesthetic issues in spatial composition: Effects of position and direction on framing single objects. *Spatial Vision*, 21, 421–449.
- Sammartino, J., & Palmer, S. E. (2012). Aesthetic issues in spatial composition: Representational fit and the role of semantic context. *Perception*, 41, 1434–1457. Vertical-position paper in *JEP:HPP*: palmerlab.berkeley.edu/Sammartino&Palmer.vertical.JEPHPP.pdf
- Gardner, J. S., Fowlkes, C., Nothelfer, C., & Palmer, S. E. (2008). Exploring aesthetic principles of spatial composition through stock photography. *VSS abstract*.
- Bertamini, M., Bennett, K. M., & Bode, C. (2011). The anterior bias in visual art. *Laterality*, 16(6).
- Miton, H., et al. (2020). Profile portraits and inward bias. *Cognitive Science*.
- Bode, C., et al. (2016). Inward bias in film frames.
- Forman, et al. (2021). The center cannot hold: Variations of frame width help to explain the inward bias. *Attention, Perception & Psychophysics*.
- McManus, I. C., Stöver, K., & Kim, D. (2011). Arnheim's Gestalt theory of visual balance. *i-Perception*, 2, 615–647. doi:10.1068/i0445aap
- Wilson, A., & Chatterjee, A. (2005). The assessment of preference for balance. *Empirical Studies of the Arts*, 23, 165–180.
- Hübner, R., & Fillinger, M. G. (2016). Comparison of objective measures for predicting perceptual balance. *Frontiers in Psychology*.
- Hübner, R., & Fillinger, M. G. (2019). Perceptual balance, stability and aesthetic appreciation. *i-Perception* (PMC6566492).
- Jahanian, A., et al. (2015). Visual balance and saliency. MIT CSAIL, *Electronic Imaging*.
