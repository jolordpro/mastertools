# Photographic craft

## Quick answer
- Viewpoint, focal length, aperture, shutter and timing are composition tools, not only exposure settings. `[Practice]`
- Move your feet before you zoom: camera position sets the relationship between foreground and background. `[Practice]`
- The decisive moment is when form and content peak together. `[Practice]`

## Viewpoint and distance
- Camera height changes power and geometry (low = monumental, high = diminishing or mapping). `[Practice]`
- Distance to subject, not focal length, controls perspective. Focal length only sets the crop. `[Physics]`
- Change the background by moving sideways a step; small moves change overlaps a lot. `[Practice]`

## Focal length `[Practice, full-frame equivalents]`
- Ultra-wide (14 to 24 mm): exaggerated depth, strong lines, distortion near edges.
- Wide (24 to 35 mm): environmental context, documentary feel.
- Normal (around 40 to 58 mm): close to natural perspective impression. `[Unverified exact range]`
- Short tele (85 to 135 mm): flattering portraits, background compression.
- Long tele (200 mm and up): strong compression, stacked planes, isolation; sports staple.

## Aperture and depth of field
- Wide aperture (low f-number): shallow depth of field, subject isolation, bokeh.
- Narrow aperture: deep focus, landscape and architecture.
- Depth of field grows with distance and shrinks with longer focal lengths and larger sensors. `[Physics]`

## Shutter and time
- Fast shutter freezes peak action.
- Slow shutter: motion blur conveys speed; panning keeps the subject sharp against streaks.
- Long exposure smooths water and clouds into negative space.

## Exposure and tone
- Zone System (Adams and Archer, about 1940): 11 zones, 0 (black) to X (white); Zone V = middle gray. Visualize where key tones fall before shooting. `[Unverified details]`
- Expose to protect what matters: highlights for skies and white uniforms, shadows for detail.
- Dynamic range limits decide whether a silhouette or a full-detail image is possible.

## Timing
- Cartier-Bresson, *Images à la sauvette* (1952; US title *The Decisive Moment*): simultaneous recognition of the significance of an event and the precise organization of forms. `[Unverified exact wording]`
- `[Practice]` Pre-compose the frame (background, light, lines), then wait for the subject to enter the right spot.

## How to measure
- EXIF: focal length, aperture, shutter, ISO.
- Blur map for depth of field; motion blur direction for panning.
- Histogram clipping in highlights and shadows.

## Failure modes
- Zooming instead of moving, losing a better background.
- Shallow focus that misses the eyes.
- Clipped highlights on the subject.
- Shooting the moment after the peak.

## Critique questions
- Would a different camera position have improved the background?
- Is the focal length serving the relationship between planes?
- Is this the peak moment?
