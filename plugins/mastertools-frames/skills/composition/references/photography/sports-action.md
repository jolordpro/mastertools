# Sports and action composition

## Quick answer
- Lead room in the direction of play is the single most reliable framing rule for action. `[E-strong via inward bias]`
- Peak action plus a readable face and the ball or puck in frame makes the strongest single image. `[Practice]`
- Clean backgrounds matter more in sports than almost anywhere, because crowds and boards are maximum clutter. `[Practice; clutter evidence E-strong]`

## Elements of a strong action frame `[Practice]`
- Peak moment: full extension, contact, apex, reaction.
- Face visible, ideally eyes, with emotion.
- The object of play (ball, puck, bat, stick) in frame and connected to the action.
- Direction: athlete moving into the frame.
- Separation: subject value and focus differ from the background.
- Story: interaction (contest for the ball, celebration, dejection) beats a lone athlete.

## Techniques `[Practice]`
- Long lens, wide aperture to blur crowds and compress the field.
- Low angle for heroism; overhead for patterns of play.
- Tight crop on emotion, wide frame for scale of moment (walk-off, championship).
- Panning at slower shutter to convey speed.
- Remote cameras for unusual angles (above the net, behind the glass).
- Shoot reactions after the play: benches, fans, coaches.
- Classic reference: Neil Leifer's 1965 Ali-Liston overhead and ringside frames for geometry and dominance. `[Unverified details]`

## Key art and athlete portraits
- Hero pose at low angle, strong rim light, dark or graphic background for type.
- Leave planned negative space for titles, logos and sponsor marks.
- Compose for all crops (16:9 broadcast, 9:16 social, 1:1 feed, 4:5) from the start.

## Broadcast frames and replays
- Graphics must not cover the ball path or faces during key moments.
- Freeze-frame telestration: draw attention with one vector at a time.
- For social cutdowns of game footage, track the ball and the primary player, not the frame center (see `reframing`).

## How to measure
- Subject and ball detection; is the ball inside frame and near the athlete?
- Facing and motion vectors vs frame center (inward score).
- Background clutter around the subject.
- Face visibility and size.

## Failure modes
- Back of the athlete with no face or ball.
- Subject running out of the frame.
- Busy crowd with signage competing at equal sharpness.
- Key art with no room left for type.

## Critique questions
- Is this the peak, and can we see the face and the object of play?
- Is the athlete moving into the frame?
- Does the background stay quiet?

## Terms
- peak action, lead room, panning, compression, reaction shot, remote camera, hero shot, telestration
