## 1. Core Definition & Scope

**Psychological Safety** is a shared belief held by members of a team or larger system that the interpersonal climate is safe for interpersonal risk-taking — speaking up with ideas, questions, concerns, mistakes, challenges, and AI-related issues without fear of embarrassment, rejection, punishment, or career damage.

This Super Page treats psychological safety as a **multi-layer cascade phenomenon**, not only a team-level climate:
- Team psychological safety climate
- Multi-layer / cascade psychological safety (how safety travels, amplifies, or dies across hierarchical levels)
- Voice quality and frequency (especially about risk, errors, dissent, and AI)
- Artificial Harmony (the dangerous counterfeit of safety)
- Leader behaviors that create, protect, or destroy safety (including sequencing and Jekyll-and-Hyde justice variability)
- Cultural contingencies of what “safe voice” and “productive challenge” look like
- AI-era criticality: safety for reporting AI errors, overrides, Decision Drift, and judgment concerns

It is a foundational **substrate** (with Energy and Capacity for Complexity). Without it, Adaptive Space collapses, Enabling Leadership fails, multi-layer sensing distorts, generativity dies, sponsorship weakens, and AI judgment loops become dangerous silent failure modes.

## 2. Key Frameworks & Models

### 2.1 Edmondson’s Psychological Safety (Team Level)
Four commonly observed pillars:
1. Attitude toward risk and failure (learning vs. blame)
2. Open conversation / willingness to speak up
3. Willingness to help (mutual support)
4. Inclusion and respect for diversity of thought

Classic 7-item team psychological safety scale has strong predictive validity for learning behavior, error reporting, innovation, and performance under uncertainty.

### 2.2 Multi-Layer / Cascade Psychological Safety
Safety does not automatically cascade. Key mechanisms and failure points:
- **Vertical cascade**: The safety climate a Leader of Leaders creates with their direct reports (other leaders) is one of the strongest predictors of the safety those leaders create with their teams.
- **Skip-level sensing quality**: When lower levels feel unsafe speaking to their manager, usable truth rarely reaches higher levels → Cascade Distortion.
- **Justice variability (Jekyll-and-Hyde)**: Inconsistent fairness, emotional predictability, or response to bad news from a leader destroys safety far more than consistently tough-but-fair leadership.
- **Sponsorship of voice**: High-quality sponsorship acts (visibility + advocacy + protection + risk-taking on behalf of others’ voice) are among the strongest multi-layer safety amplifiers.
- **Artificial Harmony**: The appearance of safety (politeness, rapid consensus, absence of visible conflict) while real concerns are suppressed. Often more dangerous than overt low safety because it is harder to detect and is frequently misread as high safety + high alignment.

### 2.3 Leader Behaviors That Create or Destroy Safety — Sequencing Matters
Research consistently shows a required sequence for challenge to land as productive rather than threatening:
1. **Supportive** behaviors first (care, respect, belonging cues, “I’ve got your back”)
2. **Consultative** behaviors (seeking input, genuine listening, incorporating or explaining why not)
3. **Then** Challenge / high standards / stretch / dissent invitation

Challenge without prior Supportive + Consultative climate is frequently experienced as threat, especially in higher power-distance contexts, lower baseline safety, or under energy depletion. This sequencing is culturally moderated (CQ-001).

**Concrete micro-moves for Leaders of Leaders:**
- Open generative forums with explicit belonging and learning frames before inviting dissent.
- Respond to the first piece of bad news or challenge in a meeting with visible appreciation and curiosity (not defense or problem-solving alone).
- Publicly update a decision or mental model when lower-level input improves it.
- Protect the person who raised a difficult issue from later social or career penalty.
- Name and interrupt justice variability in themselves and other leaders.

### 2.4 Artificial Harmony — Differential Diagnosis
**Artificial Harmony** = low expressed conflict + low genuine voice + high apparent agreement.

**Behavioral clusters:**
- Rapid “yes” or “makes sense” with little questioning of assumptions or risks
- Absence of questions about downsides, second-order effects, or AI limitations
- Private side conversations that contradict public meeting content
- “Everything is fine / we’re aligned” language when leading indicators (energy, quality, customer, AI error rates) are yellow or red
- Polished, sanitized upward communication; surprise failures
- Low skip-level signal volume or heavily filtered content

**Differential:**
- **Genuine high safety + high performance**: Disagreement is visible, respectful, and resolved; private and public conversations largely match; bad news travels fast; learning language is common.
- **Overt low safety / fear**: Visible anxiety, minimal contribution, deferential silence, clear punishment stories.
- **Artificial Harmony**: The dangerous middle — looks calm and aligned; truth is suppressed; Adaptive Space and multi-layer sensing are quietly dying.

### 2.5 AI-Era Psychological Safety (Critical)
Elevated and new requirements:
- Safety to report AI errors, hallucinations, biased outputs, and concerning agent behavior
- Safety to override or question AI recommendations without career or status risk
- Safety to discuss Decision Drift and Judgment Depreciation concerns
- Safety to treat “the model said so” as insufficient justification
- Protected channels and norms for documenting overrides and exceptions
- Leader modeling of non-defensive response to AI-related challenge

Without this layer, AI systems amplify existing safety problems and create new silent cascade risks.

### 2.6 Cultural Contingencies (Power Distance & Voice)
What counts as “safe” voice and “productive challenge” varies significantly (GLOBE Power Distance, In-Group Collectivism, etc.):
- **Lower power-distance contexts**: Direct public challenge, open disagreement with leaders, and real-time dissent often signal safety.
- **Higher power-distance contexts**: The same behaviors can signal disrespect or threat. Safety is more often expressed through private channels, carefully framed questions, third-party voice, or post-meeting input. Public challenge may reduce actual safety if imposed without translation.

**Practical rule**: Never assume low power-distance norms are universal. Apply Cultural Context Protocol (02). Default to more conservative interpretation until culture is clarified. Translate practices rather than copy-paste.

### 2.7 Safety as Leading Indicator, Substrate, and Measurement Risk
Psychological safety is simultaneously:
- A core **Dashboard domain** (BRIDGE-001)
- A **necessary condition** for Adaptive Space, Enabling Leadership, honest multi-layer sensing, generativity, sponsorship, and healthy AI judgment loops
- Bidirectionally linked with **Energy** (depletion reduces voice capacity; low safety increases load)
- Subject to **Goodhart risk**: measuring it can create performative responses and Artificial Harmony if sensing methods are crude or high-stakes

**Non-distorting sensing principles**: Prefer behavioral observation and skip-level qualitative signal over high-stakes anonymous scores alone; make learning (not ranking) the explicit purpose; protect the people who surface problems; triangulate with energy, quality, and AI error data.

### 2.8 Minimum Viable Multi-Layer Safety (Resource-Constrained or Crisis)
Even under heavy load:
- One protected channel for bad news / AI concerns that is actually used and non-punitive
- Leader modeling of Supportive + non-defensive response to the first challenge in key forums
- Explicit simple principle: “Voice about risks, errors, and AI outputs is protected”
- Rapid containment of any visible retaliation or justice variability
- Energy protection so people have capacity to speak

## 3. Behavioral Indicators Library

### Positive Signals (Healthy Multi-Layer Safety)
**Micro-behaviors in meetings and 1:1s**
- Leader opens with belonging/learning frame before inviting dissent or challenge.
- First piece of bad news or AI concern is met with visible appreciation + curiosity (“Thank you — tell me more”).
- People raise risks, errors, and dissenting views in real time (not only offline or after the decision).
- “I might be wrong / help me see what I’m missing” language is modeled by leaders and repeated by others.
- Conflict stays on ideas, designs, and data — not on people or motives.
- Supportive and Consultative moves are observable *before* Challenge is applied.
- Public updating of a decision or mental model when lower-level input improves it.

**Cascade / Multi-Layer**
- Skip-level signal is present, reasonably direct, and of usable quality.
- Middle managers report they can escalate concerns without retaliation or career tax.
- Sponsorship acts that protect voice (visibility, advocacy, protection, risk-taking) are visible and cascading.
- Private and public conversations largely match (low Artificial Harmony).
- Justice and emotional predictability from key leaders are experienced as consistent.
- Voice about cascade health (energy, safety, Adaptive Space, judgment, AI) is protected and used.

**AI-Era Specific**
- AI errors, overrides, and limitations are discussed routinely and non-defensively.
- “The model recommended X” is treated as input, not conversation-stopper.
- People document override rationales without fear.
- Concerns about Decision Drift or Judgment Depreciation can be raised.

### Red Flags (Safety Damage or Artificial Harmony)
**Early Warning**
- Meetings produce rapid consensus with little questioning of risks, assumptions, or AI outputs.
- Bad news travels slowly or arrives heavily sanitized.
- Leaders react to challenge with defensiveness, subtle coldness, sarcasm, or withdrawal of warmth (justice variability onset).
- Real concerns are saved for private side conversations or external parties.
- AI errors or overrides are rarely discussed or only after external discovery.
- Skip-level signal volume is low or content is polished.

**Active Damage**
- Overt or covert retaliation for speaking up (career, exclusion, loss of sponsorship, public shaming).
- Jekyll-and-Hyde pattern: unpredictable fairness or emotional climate from a leader.
- High-performing toxic individuals create local climates of fear that the system tolerates because of results (DARK-001 contagion).
- Safety scores or Dashboard metrics look fine while private reality and leading indicators diverge (Goodhart + Artificial Harmony).
- Chronic energy depletion coincides with declining voice (vicious cycle).
- Low power-distance challenge norms imposed in higher power-distance contexts, producing silence, resentment, or performative compliance.
- “The model said so” ends discussion; no protected path to challenge AI.

### Level Differentiation
- **Leading Others**: Team climate, local Supportive→Consultative→Challenge sequencing, inclusion of every voice, local Artificial Harmony detection.
- **Leading Leaders (primary)**: Climate with direct reports (other leaders), multi-layer sensing quality, protection of voice across the cascade, containment of justice variability and toxic capture, sponsorship of voice, cascade amplification of safety practices.
- **Enterprise / Board**: Organizational climate, decision-rights design that protects upward voice, board modeling of learning orientation and non-defensive response to challenge, AI governance that includes safety for error reporting and overrides.

## 4. Diagnostic Question Bank

**Climate, Voice & Artificial Harmony**
1. How safe do people currently feel raising risks, errors, dissent, or AI concerns in this team / cascade?
2. What happens (really) when someone brings bad news or challenges a leader’s view? What happened the last three times?
3. Where do we see Artificial Harmony — rapid agreement that does not match private reality or leading indicators?
4. How consistent is justice and emotional predictability from key leaders (Jekyll-and-Hyde risk)?

**Sequencing & Leader Behavior**
5. Do leaders consistently demonstrate Supportive and Consultative behaviors *before* applying Challenge? What does that look like in actual meetings?
6. What specific leader behaviors in the last 30 days most increased or decreased willingness to speak up?
7. When was the last time a leader publicly updated a decision or mental model because of lower-level input?

**Multi-Layer & Cascade**
8. What is the quality and volume of skip-level and cross-layer signal right now? What is missing?
9. Where is Cascade Distortion occurring (sanitized upward communication, surprise failures)?
10. How visible and cascading are sponsorship acts that protect voice (visibility, advocacy, protection, risk-taking)?

**AI-Era**
11. How safe is it, right now, to report AI errors, question recommendations, document overrides, or raise Decision Drift concerns?
12. What language do we hear around “the model said so”? Is it treated as input or as unchallengeable authority?

**Substrates, Culture & Measurement**
13. How is current energy level affecting people’s capacity and willingness to take interpersonal risks?
14. Are we seeing the Energy–Safety vicious cycle?
15. How culturally calibrated are our current safety practices and challenge norms? What would a higher power-distance colleague experience?
16. Are our safety sensing methods (surveys, Dashboard, skip-level) creating performative responses or Artificial Harmony?

**Action Design**
17. What is the highest-leverage move to improve safety right now — specific leader behavior change, system redesign (forums, decision rights, simple principles, channels), containment of a toxic actor, measurement redesign, or energy protection?
18. If we improve safety without also protecting energy and Adaptive Space, what is likely to happen?
19. How will we know safety is actually improving (behavioral signals) rather than just scores looking better?

## 5. Failure Modes & Edge Cases

- **Artificial Harmony**: Most dangerous counterfeit. Looks like high safety; suppresses truth, Adaptive Space, and multi-layer sensing. Differential diagnosis required.
- **Challenge Without Support**: High standards/stretch applied without prior Supportive + Consultative climate → threat response, especially across cultural differences or under depletion.
- **Jekyll-and-Hyde / Justice Variability**: Inconsistent fairness or emotional climate destroys safety more effectively than consistent toughness. Early detection is high-leverage.
- **Toxic High Performer Tolerance**: System protects results at the expense of local and cascade safety (DARK-001). Contagion risk is high; containment is often the correct system move.
- **Measurement Distortion (Goodhart)**: Safety surveys or Dashboard metrics become performative; true voice declines while scores look fine; Artificial Harmony increases.
- **Cultural Imposition**: Low power-distance voice practices forced into high power-distance contexts without translation → reduced actual safety, resentment, or empty ritual.
- **Energy–Safety Vicious Cycle**: Chronic depletion reduces capacity for voice and productive conflict; declining safety increases load and further depletes energy.
- **AI Silence**: No protected channels or norms for reporting AI errors, overrides, and judgment concerns → silent accumulation of Decision Drift and risk.
- **Skip-Level Bypass Damage**: Overuse or unskilled use of skip-level can undermine middle managers and create new safety problems even while surfacing some truth.
- **Safety as End Rather Than Means**: Treating safety as the goal instead of as the condition for learning, adaptation, and performance → stagnation or Artificial Harmony.
- **Minimum Viable Neglect**: Under resource pressure, the last protected voice channels and non-punitive response norms are eliminated, guaranteeing later cascade failure.

## 6. Person-Focused vs. System-Focused Decision Guidance

**Default Bias:** Hybrid, with strong system orientation for climate, Artificial Harmony, multi-layer, AI-channel, and toxic-capture issues; person orientation for specific leader skill gaps in sequencing or justice consistency.

**System-focused or Hybrid (system first) when:**
- Safety problems appear across multiple teams or layers.
- Artificial Harmony, Cascade Distortion, or justice variability patterns are present.
- Decision rights, meeting design, simple principles, voice channels, or measurement systems are contributing.
- A toxic high performer is being protected by results.
- AI error-reporting channels and norms are missing or unsafe.
- Cultural miscalibration is systemic.
- Energy depletion is co-occurring (address both).

**Person-focused when:**
- A specific leader lacks skill in Supportive→Consultative→Challenge sequencing.
- A leader’s justice variability or emotional unpredictability is the primary local driver.
- The developmental target is a leader’s second-order awareness of how their behavior shapes climate.

**Hybrid Sequence (most common and highest leverage):**
1. Diagnose climate vs. individual leader contribution (recurrence test + multi-layer sensing + Artificial Harmony check).
2. Address system conditions first or in parallel (forums, decision rights, simple principles protecting voice, AI channels, measurement redesign, containment of toxic actors, energy protection).
3. Coach specific leaders on sequencing, justice consistency, response to bad news, and sponsorship of voice.
4. Explicitly build cascade amplification: coached leaders apply the same safety practices and Bridge downward.
5. Recheck energy so people have capacity to use the improved safety.

**Required Output Format:** Always use the Intervention Mode block + dosage when recommending action.

## 8. AI Coach Application Notes

- Default to Path B (Cascade Diagnosis) when safety signals appear; always integrate with BRIDGE-001 Dashboard.
- Always check for Artificial Harmony vs. genuine safety vs. overt fear (differential diagnosis).
- Run Person-vs-System with recurrence test; prefer system/hybrid for climate, multi-layer, AI-channel, and toxic issues.
- In AI-related conversations, force assessment of safety for error reporting, overrides, and Decision Drift voice (Critical).
- Apply Cultural Context Protocol: default conservative / higher power-distance interpretation until culture is clarified; never impose low power-distance norms uncritically.
- When Jekyll-and-Hyde or toxic patterns appear → integrate DARK-001 early; consider containment before pure developmental coaching.
- Use second-order moves to help leaders see how their own sequencing, justice consistency, and response to bad news shape climate.
- Cite as `SAFE-001`. Communicate evidence_strength accurately (team Strong; multi-layer Moderate; AI-era and cultural translation Emerging).
- In Path K (overwhelmed) or Path F (crisis): prioritize containment, energy protection, and one protected voice channel over expansive safety development work.
- Watch for Coach-side failure modes: imposing low power-distance norms, over-emphasizing individual “courage,” ignoring measurement distortion, colluding in multi-party character attacks, or treating safety as the end rather than the means.
